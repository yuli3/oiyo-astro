The autumn equinox (秋分) is the sixteenth of the 24 solar terms, the moment the sun reaches **180°** of solar longitude — directly opposite the spring equinox at 0°.

## 1. When it falls — measured values

Entry moments as published by the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute (KASI).

| Year | Date | Entry moment (KST) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2000 | 23 Sep | 02:28 |
| 2001 | 23 Sep | 08:04 |
| 2008 | 23 Sep | 00:44 |
| 2016 | 22 Sep | 23:21 |
| 2024 | 22 Sep | 21:44 |
| 2025 | 23 Sep | 03:19 |
| 2028 | 22 Sep | 20:45 |

From 21:44 in 2024 to 03:19 in 2025 the moment slipped about 5 hours 35 minutes later — the common-year rule of roughly **5 hours 49 minutes** of drift (the actual figure varies by tens of minutes because Earth's orbit is elliptical). When that accumulated drift is about to cross a whole day, a leap day pulls it back; that is why 2028 falls again on 22 September.

## 2. Day and night are not exactly equal

The equinox is often introduced as "the day when day and night are the same length." Strictly, it is not.

Its astronomical definition is **the moment the centre of the sun crosses the celestial equator from north to south**. But observed sunrise and sunset are skewed in favour of daylight, for two reasons:

- Sunrise and sunset are measured from the sun's **upper limb** touching the horizon, not its centre
- Atmospheric refraction lifts the sun into view while it is still geometrically below the horizon

So the day on which light and dark actually balance — the *equilux* — arrives a **few days after** the equinox. On the equinox itself, daylight is still several minutes longer.

Put precisely, the equinox is not the day day and night are equal. It is **the turning point at which night begins to overtake day**.

## 3. The three pentads

The three pentads for the autumn equinox, from the *Shixunjie* chapter of the *Yi Zhou Shu*:

| Pentad | Original | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | 雷始收聲 | Thunder withdraws its voice |
| Second | 蟄蟲坏戶 | Hibernating insects seal their burrows |
| Third | 水始涸 | The waters begin to dry |

All three are acts of **gathering in and closing up**. Thunder takes back its sound, insects shut their doors, the water withdraws. Where the spring equinox pentads open and emerge, these are their mirror image.

## 4. Customs and farm work

The equinox opens the harvest. The *Nongga Wollyeongga* places rice cutting and storing in this stretch.

The Joseon court offered rites at this time to Canopus (老人星, the "Old Man Star"), which rises low in the southern sky, praying for long life. At Korea's latitude the star only appears briefly and close to the southern horizon in autumn, so seeing a star that is hard to see at all was itself taken as an auspicious sign.

## 5. Where the equinox sits in Saju

The autumn equinox is a **junggi (中氣)**. Therefore **the Saju month pillar does not change at the equinox.**

Month boundaries are opened by *jeol*, and the jeol on either side are White Dew (165°) and Cold Dew (195°). The Rooster month runs from White Dew until just before Cold Dew, and the equinox passes through its middle.

What the equinox does as a junggi is take part in deciding leap months: a lunar month containing no junggi at all becomes the leap month, and the equinox is one of the twelve junggi used in that test.
