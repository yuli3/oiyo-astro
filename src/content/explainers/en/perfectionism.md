Understanding perfectionism involves more than how high a standard is: consider **what your self-worth depends on and what maintaining that standard costs**. Wanting to do well is not automatically a problem, and perfectionism is not another name for anxiety.

[Western Australia's CCI educational material](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer-Modules/Perfectionism-in-Perspective/Perfectionism-in-Perspective---01---What-is-Perfectionism.pdf) describes persistently pursuing excessively demanding standards, relying too heavily on achievement when evaluating self-worth, and continuing despite costs. It does not ask everyone to abandon high standards or diagnose an individual reader's motives.

## 1. Three dimensions are not three kinds of people

[Hewitt and Flett's 1991 paper](https://hewittlab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2014/11/Hewitt-Flett-1991-Perfectionism-in-the-self-and-social-contexts-conceptualization-assessment-and-association-with-psychopathology.pdf) distinguishes self-oriented, other-oriented and socially prescribed dimensions. Several can occur together in one person; continuous degrees should not become grades assigned to a whole person.

| Dimension | Direction of standards and evaluation | Limit on interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-oriented | Demanding high standards of yourself and evaluating your performance | May involve striving or avoiding failure, but does not establish success or illness |
| Other-oriented | Demanding high standards of other people's performance and evaluating it | Actual demands and relationship context matter; it is not a verdict that someone is bad |
| Socially prescribed | Perceiving that important others demand high standards of you | Perceiving a demand is different from establishing what the other person actually requires |

Do not diagnose people as a “driver,” “judge” or “victim,” or read the socially prescribed dimension as “the most dangerous person.” A research-scale result and an everyday self-description are also different kinds of information.

## 2. Look at rigidity and costs, not just standards

To examine whether standards help, ask whether you can revise them to fit the situation. Turning an unfinished draft into “I have no worth” differs from “The scope and time did not fit; I need to revise the plan.” These are explanatory examples, not questionnaire items separating two types of people.

If repeated checking delays completion and repeatedly displaces sleep or other commitments, examine whether more checking improves necessary quality or merely adds costs. Procrastination is not always caused by perfectionism; actual workload and clarity of instructions also deserve attention.

[CCI's material on changing standards](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer-Modules/Perfectionism-in-Perspective/Perfectionism-in-Perspective---04---Changing-Perfectionism.pdf) discusses thinking along a continuum instead of in black and white, replacing rigid standards with realistically high ones, and choosing a specific area and small behavioral goals. It is not an efficiency formula or treatment promise that “20% effort produces an 80-point result.”

## 3. What parenting and mental-health research can tell us

[Curran and Hill's meta-analysis abstract](https://ray.yorksj.ac.uk/id/eprint/5802) distinguishes correlations involving parental expectations and criticism from trends over time in university students' perceptions of parental attitudes. An association does not mean every critical parent produces a perfectionistic child. Do not infer past parental behavior from current answers or assign one person as the cause.

[Smith and colleagues' meta-analysis](https://cruxpsychology.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Smithetal.2018.522-542.pdf) examines associations between aspects of perfectionism and suicidal thoughts or attempts across 45 studies with 11,747 participants. Thoughts, attempts and deaths are different outcomes; associations or longitudinal predictions cannot be converted into an individual's probability of death. This evidence does not justify claiming the “highest correlation” among all psychological characteristics or assigning a person's current risk level.

Distress or safety concerns need attention independently of a result label. If everyday functioning becomes seriously difficult or you are concerned about your safety, consider qualified mental-health support rather than drawing conclusions from a self-guided activity. A different result label does not guarantee safety either.

## 4. Make standards concrete for a low-stakes task

The table is an editorial reflection example for a task with small consequences, such as private notes. It is not a validated treatment program or productivity guarantee.

| What to examine | Example: private reading notes |
| --- | --- |
| Necessary quality | Write three points that help you recall the main ideas later |
| Scope of checking | Check once for errors that change your understanding |
| When to finish | At a predefined stopping time, record remaining questions and stop |
| Actual costs and counterexamples | Consider when checking helped understanding and when it consumed time without helping |

Adjusting standards does not mean always submitting quickly. Do not reduce required verification or professional standards in tasks involving life, health, law or safety. Writing a work product's possible improvements separately from your human worth is also a reflection example, not proof of treatment efficacy.

## 5. What does the OIYO activity calculate?

The current perfectionism test address leads to the same-language [lazy perfectionist activity](/en/lazy-perfectionist/test/). The activity uses eight items with four choices and shows one of four editorial results: `starter`, `planner`, `critic` or `recovering`. It is not an officially validated form measuring the three research dimensions above.

For each item, the calculation adds predetermined baseline points to all four result axes, then adds three points to the axis corresponding to the selected option. After eight items it selects the highest total; ties favor the earlier result in the order `starter → planner → critic → recovering`. It therefore does not merely count choices, and is not a population-norm score or risk assessment.

A label involving “recovery” or `recovering` does not establish clinical recovery, treatment progress, a cause or future success. This explanation does not certify item validity, translation equivalence or any necessary rights. Use the result only as a prompt to discuss real situations, not for diagnosis, hiring or judging another person's worth.
