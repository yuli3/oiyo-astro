Rain Water (雨水) is the second of the 24 solar terms. It follows Start of Spring and is defined as the moment the sun reaches **330°** of solar longitude.

This article does not open with proverbs about ice breaking on the river. It starts with when the term actually falls, measured, and explains why those numbers move the way they do.

## 1. When it falls — measured values

Entry moments for Rain Water as published by the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute (KASI). Note first that they are announced as times to the minute, not as dates.

| Year | Date | Entry moment (KST) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2000 | 19 Feb | 17:33 |
| 2001 | 18 Feb | 23:27 |
| 2008 | 19 Feb | 15:49 |
| 2016 | 19 Feb | 14:34 |
| 2024 | 19 Feb | 13:13 |
| 2025 | 18 Feb | 19:07 |
| 2028 | 19 Feb | 12:26 |

Between 2000 and 2001 the term moved a day earlier. So did it between 2024 and 2025. There is a rule behind this.

The sun takes about 365.2422 days to return to 330°. In a 365-day common year the sun has not quite arrived, so the entry moment comes **about 5 hours 49 minutes later** the following year. But when 29 February falls in between, 366 days elapse and the sun overshoots, so the entry moment arrives **about 18 hours earlier** instead. Between 19 February 2000 and 18 February 2001 lay 29 February 2000; subtract 18 hours 6 minutes from 17:33 and you land exactly on 23:27 the previous day.

This is what it means to say that **the term is not what wobbles — the calendar is.**

## 2. What the name says

Rain Water (雨水) is literally "rain water": the time when what falls from the sky changes from snow into rain.

One caution. Rain Water is **not a rainy term.** Late February on the Korean peninsula is among the drier stretches of the year. The name points not at the *amount* of precipitation but at its *form* — the switch from falling frozen to falling melted.

## 3. The three pentads

Classical calendars split each solar term into three five-day pentads and attached a natural sign to each: 24 × 3 = 72 pentads. The three for Rain Water, as recorded in the *Shixunjie* chapter of the *Yi Zhou Shu*:

| Pentad | Original | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | 獺祭魚 | The otter lays out the fish it has caught |
| Second | 鴻雁來 | The wild geese return north |
| Third | 草木萌動 | Grass and trees begin to sprout |

All three are things that **only become possible once the ice has broken**. The otter needs open water, the geese need a survivable north, the shoots need thawed ground. The name and the pentads are two faces of one event.

## 4. Customs and farm work

What the *Dongguk Sesigi* and the *Nongga Wollyeongga* record for this stretch is concentrated on **preparation** for the year's farming: mending the water channels of the fields, sorting seed, sharpening tools.

The custom of starting soy sauce and bean paste around Rain Water comes from the same place. Too cold and fermentation will not take hold; too warm and unwanted microbes grow first. The narrow window between the two falls here. This is not superstition but an empirical judgement about temperature.

## 5. Where Rain Water sits in Saju

Rain Water is a **junggi (中氣)**, and that has a concrete consequence.

**The Saju month pillar does not change at Rain Water.** Month boundaries are opened by *jeol*, and the jeol on either side are Start of Spring (315°) and Awakening of Insects (345°). The Tiger month runs from Start of Spring until just before Awakening of Insects, and Rain Water merely passes through its **middle**.

Junggi have a different job. When a lunar month contains no junggi at all, that month becomes the leap month — the *no-junggi leap placement* rule. Rain Water is one of the twelve junggi used in that determination.
