Major Cold (大寒) is the **last** of the 24 solar terms, the moment the sun reaches **300°** of solar longitude. After it comes Start of Spring (315°) and the cycle begins again.

## 1. When it falls — measured values

Entry moments as published by the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute (KASI).

| Year | Date | Entry moment (KST) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2000 | 21 Jan | 03:23 |
| 2001 | 20 Jan | 09:16 |
| 2008 | 21 Jan | 01:43 |
| 2016 | 21 Jan | 00:27 |
| 2024 | 20 Jan | 23:07 |
| 2025 | 20 Jan | 05:00 |
| 2028 | 20 Jan | 22:22 |

2024 and 2025 share the same date — 20 January — yet the moment jumps from 23:07 to 05:00. Between those two points lies 29 February 2024, so 366 days elapsed and the sun overshot 300°, bringing the entry moment **about 18 hours earlier**. Read as dates alone they look like "the same day"; in fact the term moved by nearly a full day.

Handle solar terms as dates and this movement is invisible. That is why the Saju engine here uses the moment, not the date.

## 2. What the name claims, and the actual temperature

Major Cold means "great cold" — yet **the coldest stretch in Korea is not Major Cold but Minor Cold.** A Korean proverb makes the mismatch exactly: *Major Cold went to visit Minor Cold's house and froze to death there.*

The cause is where the names come from. The 24 terms were named against the climate of the North China Plain, whose cold-wave rhythm is not Korea's.

Seasonal lag compounds it. Insolation bottoms out at the winter solstice (270°), but land and ocean release heat slowly, so the temperature minimum arrives roughly a month later. In Korea that minimum lands near Minor Cold (285°).

The name is therefore **not wrong so much as calibrated elsewhere**. Read as a weather forecast, the terms drift; read as a scale of solar position, they stay exact.

## 3. The three pentads

The three pentads for Major Cold, from the *Shixunjie* chapter of the *Yi Zhou Shu*:

| Pentad | Original | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | 雞始乳 | Hens begin to brood |
| Second | 征鳥厲疾 | Birds of prey grow fierce and swift |
| Third | 水澤腹堅 | Ponds freeze solid to their depths |

That the first pentad of the coldest season is **breeding** is worth pausing on. Hens brood not because the cold has ended but because the days have started to lengthen. Living things respond first to the growing light after the solstice — the old observation already encodes the fact that the floor of temperature and the floor of daylight do not coincide.

## 4. Customs and farm work

As the last term of the cycle, Major Cold mixes **closing out and preparing**. The customs the *Dongguk Sesigi* records here lean toward clearing away the old and readying for the new year.

Farming households treated the stretch from the day after Major Cold until Start of Spring as *singugan* — the gap between the old and new — and used it for moving house or repairs. On Jeju in particular this window was held to be free of malign influences, and moves clustered into it.

## 5. Where Major Cold sits in Saju

Major Cold is a **junggi (中氣)**. Therefore **the Saju month pillar does not change at Major Cold.**

Month boundaries are opened by *jeol*, and the jeol on either side are Minor Cold (285°) and Start of Spring (315°). The Ox month runs from Minor Cold until just before Start of Spring, and Major Cold passes through its middle.

One thing matters more here than elsewhere. **The Saju year turns not on 1 January nor on Lunar New Year, but on Start of Spring.** Major Cold is the junggi immediately before it, so anyone born between Major Cold and Start of Spring still carries the previous year's year pillar — and that boundary is decided by the entry moment of Start of Spring, not by a date.
