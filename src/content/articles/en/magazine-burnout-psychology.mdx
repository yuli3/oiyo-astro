## Distinguish the workplace context from explanatory models

Finding work difficult is not a reason to conclude that you lack willpower. Equally, recognizing yourself in an explanation does not establish a diagnosis.

In the WHO explanation of ICD-11, burnout is an occupational phenomenon related to chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed; it is not classified as a medical condition. This classification is not a claim about all national healthcare arrangements. Difficulties with studying, job searching, and family caregiving matter, but are not automatically covered by this workplace definition. Burnout also appeared in ICD-10 before WHO's 2019 explanation.

## A stage chart is not a diagnosis or a prediction

A proposed explanatory model is not evidence that everyone follows the same sequence. This article does not use twelve stages to assign your current stage or predict a future collapse. Depression is not an inevitable next state. Declining to label your experience burnout is not, by itself, a symptom.

## Six work-life areas are not a diagnostic score

Maslach and Leiter discuss workload, control, reward, community, fairness, and values as areas of the relationship between people and their workplace. These are examples for considering mismatches. One item does not establish a cause, and adding them does not produce a diagnosis.

## A table cannot distinguish burnout from depression for you

Burnout and depression can be related or overlap, but they are not interchangeable. Whether enjoyment or a sense of self returns after rest does not rule out depression, and burnout does not inevitably progress into depression. If symptoms persist or interfere with daily functioning, consult a qualified health or mental health professional. Do not turn the assessment described in [NIMH guidance on depression](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression) into a burnout self-diagnosis rule.

## Questionnaire scores and individual diagnosis are different

Maslach and Leiter's review explains that burnout instruments can differ in their target populations, dimensions, and operational definitions. The same number from different instruments need not represent the same condition. Do not turn this article's examples into validated questionnaire items or cutoffs.

A self-report score can represent how someone responds about their experience. It does not independently determine objective performance, the cause of health difficulties, or treatment needs. Consider the instrument's name, version, population, and validation purpose. Measurement used in research is not automatically an individual diagnosis.

## How far can a research finding take you?

An association between exhaustion and work conditions does not, by itself, establish which causes which or whether every person follows the same process. Maslach and Leiter discuss limitations of self-report and cross-sectional research. The design, population, and measurements affect what can be concluded.

Likewise, taking part in a particular leisure activity does not establish that it is numbing rather than recovery. The sources used here do not classify Netflix viewing or social media use as having one effect for everyone. Describe the burden, the impact on daily life, and the support you need instead.

## An explanation can help you start asking for support

WHO guidance on mental health at work considers organizational risk reduction alongside personal support. Workload, discretion, support, and fairness are not conditions everyone can change through willpower alone. Available adjustments depend on the country, employment arrangement, and organization.

If symptoms persist or interfere with daily functioning, consult a qualified health or mental health professional. If you cannot keep yourself safe now or are at immediate risk of self-harm, contact local emergency services immediately. This article does not replace diagnosis or treatment.
