Inside Spring Warm, **Vivid** is the clear, high-chroma end of the palette. Soft Spring Light stays pale. Dusty Spring Mute stays grayed. Vivid is the warm color that still looks clean next to the face: coral, clear orange, golden yellow. This is a draping guide for clothes and makeup. It does not describe a disease, a gene, or a body type.

## What Spring Warm Vivid means

In the color-consulting trade, Spring rests on a warm cast. **Vivid**, inside that season, is usually described like this:

- Chroma: **high** (clear, not dusty)
- Value: **medium to light**
- Temper: clean and bright, not smoky
- Impression: a warm spring flower, not a deep autumn leaf

If Spring Light is a thin watercolor, Spring Vivid is the same warmth painted a step clearer. It is not the same claim as Winter Bright, which pushes contrast much harder. Do not treat high contrast as the definition here.

## A draping checklist

Look in daylight, with makeup off, and hold cloth beside the face. These are trade observations. They are not a medical exam.

### Skin

- [ ] Gold, peach, or ivory reads first
- [ ] A gold ring looks kinder next to the face than a silver one
- [ ] Cheek color reads peach or coral, not a cool blue-pink
- [ ] Copper or peach cloth beside the face looks warm and clear
- [ ] There is a yellow cast, but the face does not look muddy

**Compared with Spring Light**: Light often prefers a paler, sheerer color. Vivid usually looks more awake when the warm color has more chroma.

How skin tans, and what color a blemish leaves behind, are not part of this check. That is skin, not draping.

### Eyes

- [ ] Deep brown through amber, including dark eyes with a warm cast
- [ ] A gold, amber, or hazel glint
- [ ] The eye area looks defined rather than faded
- [ ] The white of the eye reads ivory more than a cold blue-white

A different eye color does not ban the palette. The cloth test comes first.

### Hair

- [ ] Natural hair reads warm brown, chestnut, or caramel
- [ ] Gold or a little red shows in the hair
- [ ] A cool jet-black dye looks hard beside the face
- [ ] A warm, lighter brown makes the face look brighter

Blonde or red hair is not a requirement. The list below is about dye direction, not a natural-hair rule.

### Color reactions

**The palette is more likely when these look lively and tidy**

- Coral red (red with orange in it)
- Golden yellow
- Clear orange
- Clear coral-orange
- Apricot and peach

**The palette is also more likely when these look dull or separate from the face**

- Cool blue-pink
- Deep burgundy
- Grayed, muted colors
- Cool white

## Palette

### Reds

- **Coral red**: the red this palette reaches for first
- **Tomato red**: a clear red with yellow in it
- **Orange red**: red that has moved toward orange

Reds to skip: blue-red, deep burgundy

### Orange and apricot

- **Clear orange**: the color that shows the chroma most plainly
- **Coral orange**: orange with apricot in it
- **Mango orange**: a bright yellow-orange
- **Apricot**: the softer everyday color

### Yellows

- **Golden yellow**: yellow with a gold cast
- **Sunflower yellow**: clear, not mustard
- **Corn yellow**: a light maize yellow

Yellows to skip: lemon yellow, fluorescent yellow

### Greens

- **Spring green**: a fresh yellow-green
- **Mint with a yellow cast**: warmer than a blue mint
- **Lime**: clear and light

### Blues and purples

Cool blue is hard to wear in a large area next to this palette. If you try it, keep the area small.

- **Aqua**: blue-green with a little yellow
- **Cobalt**: a clear blue, as a point of color only
- Blues to skip: gray-blue, ice blue, powder blue

### Neutrals

- Ivory
- Milk cream
- Golden beige
- Camel
- Warm tan

Neutrals to skip: cool gray, blue-white, and a large field of stark white right beside the face

## Makeup

### Base

The aim is warm clarity, not a blank white face.

- **Foundation**: yellow-beige. A pink base often sits apart from the face
- **Concealer**: ivory to peach
- **Powder**: a milk tone. A little shimmer is enough
- **Highlighter**: gold, on the cheekbone and the T-zone

### Eyes

- **Shadow**: coral brown, golden beige, orange terracotta, warm khaki
- **Liner**: brown is the easy choice. Gold works as a small point
- **Mascara**: brown looks natural. Black is fine too

Eye colors to skip: cool gray, powder blue, blue-purple

### Cheeks

- **Blush**: coral orange, peach, light apricot
- **Highlighter**: golden pearl, peach gold

### Lips

- Coral red
- Orange red
- Coral pink
- Apricot nude
- Clear coral-orange

Lips to skip: blue-pink rose, deep burgundy, cool red, wine

## Clothes

This palette can carry a clear warm color. That does not mean every neon works. Clear next to the face and loud in a way that floats off the face are different.

### Combinations to try

**Coral and ivory**: a coral blouse with ivory trousers. Bright, and light.

**Orange and camel**: an orange top with a camel coat. A clear warm point on a warm neutral. This is not the dusty depth of an autumn palette.

**Golden yellow and ivory**: a golden-yellow dress with ivory shoes. Close to a spring flower.

**Coral red in one piece**: a coral dress, worn as one color. Check the mirror so the face does not look lost above it.

### Combinations to skip

- Strongly grayed mutes (smoky gray, khaki gray, near-black)
- A wide field of cool blue or cool purple beside the face
- Cold blue-white

## Hair color

### Directions that usually sit well

- Caramel brown
- Copper
- Golden brown
- Honey brown
- Warm light brown

Ash is usually a cool direction, even when the name sounds soft. It is not on the list above.

### Directions to treat carefully

- Blue-black dye
- Cool ash
- Wine burgundy

---

Spring Warm Vivid is the palette where coral, orange, and golden yellow make the face look clearer. This guide does not name public figures. Season labels for celebrities disagree from one consultant to the next, and one photograph is not a draping session. Hold a bright coral next to the face and see whether you look more awake.
