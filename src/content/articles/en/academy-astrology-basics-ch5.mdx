## Calculation and symbolic interpretation are different tasks

An **aspect** classifies the angular relationship between two bodies using their ecliptic longitudes. “Energy” in this context is an astrological metaphor, not a measured physical energy or force acting on a person. Being able to calculate an angle does not establish the validity of personality, talent, or event predictions.

### Start with the smaller angle

Use longitudes λ₁ and λ₂ from the same reference system and time, normalized to at least 0° and less than 360°.

1. Find the difference d = |λ₁ − λ₂|.
2. Take the smaller angle Δ = min(d, 360° − d).
3. Calculate the deviation ε = |Δ − A| from the target angle A.
4. With a selected orb o, classify the pair as that aspect when ε ≤ o.

For example, 359° and 1° have a smaller separation of 2°, not 358°. They qualify as a conjunction under an 8° orb. By contrast, 1° Aries and 29° Aries are 28° apart and do not qualify under that rule. Sharing a sign is not enough to establish a conjunction.

The [Swiss Ephemeris technical documentation](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swisseph.htm) explains position calculations and reference-system choices. It does not empirically certify the orbs or symbolic interpretations below.

## Major aspects and the orbs used in this chapter

An **orb** is a classification setting: how far a pair may deviate from an exact target angle. Schools, bodies, and techniques use different settings. These values are choices for consistent worked examples, not a universal standard or a statement about the service engine's configuration.

| Aspect | Target angle | Orb used here | Traditional symbolic theme |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Conjunction | 0° | 8° | Combination and concentration |
| Sextile | 60° | 4° | Cooperation and opportunity |
| Square | 90° | 8° | Tension and adjustment |
| Trine | 120° | 8° | Harmony and flow |
| Opposition | 180° | 8° | Contrast and balance |

An implementation should also state whether boundary values are included, whether the Sun and Moon receive different orbs, and how overlapping classifications are handled. A wider orb includes more pairs; it does not imply greater accuracy.

### Worked example: classifying a trine

Longitudes of 125° and 242° give a separation of 117°. The deviation from the 120° target is 3°, within this chapter's 8° orb. A method using a 2° orb would exclude it.

This demonstrates that **classification depends on the settings**. A smaller deviation means closer agreement with the target angle, not a measured increase in psychological influence.

## Reading the five aspects

- **Conjunction:** Read the two assigned themes together. Do not assume it is always the strongest or automatically beneficial.
- **Sextile:** Cooperation and opportunity are interpretive conventions, not guarantees of opportunities or social luck.
- **Square:** Tension and adjustment form a narrative framework. Do not use it to justify suffering as necessary for growth or to promise stronger abilities.
- **Trine:** Harmony and flow are symbolic themes. Distinguish the sign classification of an exact 120° relationship from degree-based classification using an orb. This is not a talent or success test.
- **Opposition:** Consider contrasting themes side by side. A chart does not establish another person's intentions, consent, or the cause of relationship conflict.

| Symbolic combination | Interpretation exercise | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Sun–Saturn conjunction | Describe self-expression alongside limitation | Actual responsibility or ambition |
| Mars–Saturn square | Form questions contrasting action and limitation | A diagnosis of impulsivity or patience |
| Moon–Venus trine | Reflect on feelings and harmony in relationships | Artistic talent or others' approval |

## Minor aspects and compound patterns

Minor aspects include the semi-sextile at 30°, semi-square at 45°, sesquiquadrate at 135°, and quincunx at 150°. Specify separate orb rules rather than automatically borrowing those for major aspects.

| Pattern | Angular relationships to check | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Grand Trine | All three pairs approximately 120° | Sharing an element is insufficient |
| T-Square | One pair approximately 180°, the other two approximately 90° | The apex body participates in both squares |
| Grand Cross | Four points roughly 90° apart, with two opposite pairs approximately 180° | Checking only some pairs does not establish the whole pattern |
| Yod | One pair approximately 60°, the other two approximately 150° | Not evidence of a special mission |
| Stellium | Multiple bodies grouped in a sign or house | State the body count and included objects; distinguish it from a fixed-angle pattern |

Positions in an actual chart are not independent arbitrary numbers. Exercises made from invented degrees demonstrate geometry. To present one as a real birth chart, its date, time, place, and calculation settings must reproduce it.

## Keep a calculation record before interpreting

Record the longitudes, time, reference system, and birth-time uncertainty first. Specify target angles, orbs, and boundary rules; then calculate Δ and ε for each pair and check patterns. Add symbolic interpretation separately. There is no single universally followed interpretation order.

If the birth time is uncertain, do not assert a precise Moon position or relationships involving the Ascendant and houses. Distinguishing applying from separating aspects also requires relative motion over time, not just a list of angles at one instant.
