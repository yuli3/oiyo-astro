## What is the day pillar (日柱)?

Among the four pillars of a saju chart, the **day pillar (日柱) is the seat read as the self**. The day stem (天干) is one's element in the chart. The day branch (地支) is the ground one stands on. Reading the day branch as the spouse palace is an old chart convention, especially in male-centered texts. It is not a fixed fact about gender.

A day pillar is a pairing of **10 stems and 12 branches**. Once yin and yang are matched, there are **60 (六十甲子)**.

This page is tradition. It is not a prediction of fate, and it is not medicine. Below is a subset that writing often names. It is not a detailed reading of thirty pillars.

---

## How a day pillar is read

Three pieces are read together:

| Piece | What the tradition reads |
|---|---|
| Day stem (日干) | The element taken as one's own |
| Day branch (日支) | The ground one stands on, and the old spouse-palace seat |
| Day stem × day branch | The ten-god relation (peers / output / wealth / officer / resource) |

The ten god of the branch toward the stem is what these sketches use when they talk about temperament. One pillar does not decide the chart.

## Day pillars of stem Gap (甲)

### Gap-Ja (甲子)

**Heavenly stem 甲 wood + earthly branch 子 water (편인, Pyeonin 偏印)**

Ja is yang water. It produces Gap, yang wood (水生木). The same polarity makes this indirect resource, 편인 (偏印), not direct resource (정인, 正印). Traditional writing sketches original learning and intuition.

- **Energy often named**: a pioneering push, plus a deep intuition
- **Sketch**: a leading posture with a fine inner life. An original idea opening a path others have not taken
- **Work often named**: researcher, strategist, founder
- **Caution**: a tendency to try to carry everything alone

### Gap-In (甲寅)

**Heavenly stem 甲 wood + earthly branch 寅 wood (비견, Bigyeon 比肩)**

The stem and the branch are the same yang wood, so the ten god is a peer, 비견 (比肩). Traditional writing describes independence and self-regard as very strong.

- **Energy often named**: an unblocked push, a doubled wood
- **Sketch**: a brave pioneering picture. Two tigers running side by side
- **Work often named**: sport, the military, the police, business
- **Caution**: refusing compromise, stiffness

### Gap-O (甲午)

**Heavenly stem 甲 wood + earthly branch 午 fire (식신, Siksin 食神)**

O fire is where Gap wood's energy is expressed. The production is 木生火. Both are yang, so the ten god is the eating god, 식신 (食神). A picture of overflowing expression belongs to traditional writing. It is not a measurement.

- **Energy often named**: expressive heat, and a presence people notice
- **Sketch**: a strong push plus a bright way of showing it. A place where attention gathers, in that picture
- **Work often named**: politics, performance, creating
- **Caution**: spent energy, impulse

### Gap-Sin (甲申)

**Heavenly stem 甲 wood + earthly branch 申 metal (편관, Pyeongwan 偏官)**

The yang metal of Shin controls Gap wood (金剋木). The same polarity makes this indirect officer, 편관 (偏官). Traditional writing sketches growth inside challenge and pressure.

- **Energy often named**: a hardness that meets pressure
- **Sketch**: early trials in the traditional picture, read as what builds a practiced inner strength. A temperament often placed next to the military, medicine, and the law
- **Work often named**: law, the military, medicine, audit
- **Caution**: the tension traditional texts describe. If the body shows symptoms, see a clinician

## Day pillars of stem Eul (乙)

### Eul-Myo (乙卯)

**Heavenly stem 乙 wood + earthly branch 卯 wood (비견, Bigyeon 比肩)**

Stem and branch are both yin wood, the same polarity, so the ten god is a peer, 비견 (比肩). Traditional writing sketches artistry and a rich sensitivity.

- **Energy often named**: a flexible vitality, a plain beauty
- **Sketch**: a fine artistic sense. An image like a flower field in spring
- **Work often named**: artist, counselor, beauty, design
- **Caution**: dependence, hesitation

### Eul-Yu (乙酉)

**Heavenly stem 乙 wood + earthly branch 酉 metal (편관, Pyeongwan 偏官)**

Yu metal controls Eul wood (金剋木). Both are yin, so the ten god is indirect officer, 편관 (偏官). Traditional writing sketches a pillar that hardens through outside trials.

- **Energy often named**: a fine sensitivity joined to a sharp temperament
- **Sketch**: flexible, and decisive at the moment that counts. The image of a flower on a blade
- **Work often named**: medicine, law, art, psychology
- **Caution**: inner conflict, sensitivity

## Day pillars of stem Byeong (丙)

### Byeong-O (丙午)

**Heavenly stem 丙 fire + earthly branch 午 fire (비견, Bigyeon 比肩)**

Byeong fire and O fire are both yang fire, so the ten god is a peer, 비견 (比肩). It is not the robber god, 겁재 (Geopjae, 劫財).

- **Energy often named**: a picture of fire stacked on fire
- **Sketch**: traditional texts often call this hot. This page drops the claim that being noticed is fate. It also drops any rank of the most intense among twelve day pillars
- **Work often named**: politics, performance, religious leadership
- **Caution**: an extreme leaning, spent energy

### Byeong-Ja (丙子)

**Heavenly stem 丙 fire + earthly branch 子 water (편관, Pyeongwan 偏官)**

Ja water controls Byeong fire (水剋火). Both are yang, so the ten god is indirect officer, 편관 (偏官). The image is the sun meeting a winter sea.

- **Energy often named**: heat, plus an inner tension
- **Sketch**: a bright outside and a loneliness inside, in the traditional picture. Growth is read through challenge
- **Work often named**: researcher, strategist, artist
- **Caution**: swings of mood, a sense of being alone

## Day pillars of stem Jeong (丁)

### Jeong-Hae (丁亥)

**Heavenly stem 丁 fire + earthly branch 亥 water (편관, Pyeongwan 偏官)**

Hae water controls Jeong fire (水剋火). Both are yin, so the ten god is indirect officer, 편관 (偏官). Among Jeong-fire day pillars, traditional writing calls this energy the most distinctive. That phrase is not a measured rank.

- **Energy often named**: a candle that shakes and, in the image, does not go out
- **Sketch**: a deep inner life and a strong vitality. One's light is pictured as kept through difficulty
- **Work often named**: artist, religious life, psychotherapy
- **Caution**: a swirl of feeling, sensitivity

### Jeong-Yu (丁酉)

**Heavenly stem 丁 fire + earthly branch 酉 metal (편재, Pyeonjae 偏財)**

Jeong fire controls Yu metal. Both are yin, so the ten god is indirect wealth, 편재 (偏財), not direct wealth. The category is the wealth star. Traditional writing sketches a harvest tied to money.

- **Energy often named**: a fine skill placed next to making wealth
- **Sketch**: detailed, perfectionist, and practical, in that picture. An output compared to a jewel
- **Work often named**: finance, medicine, design, art
- **Caution**: perfectionism taken too far

## Day pillars of stem Mu (戊)

### Mu-Sul (戊戌)

**Heavenly stem 戊 earth + earthly branch 戌 earth (비견, Bigyeon 比肩)**

Stem and branch are both yang earth, so the ten god is a peer, 비견 (比肩). This page drops any rank of the most stable.

- **Energy often named**: a mountain on a mountain, a solid steadiness
- **Sketch**: a will that, once set, is pictured as unmoving, like a mountain. A presence read as a pillar one can lean on
- **Work often named**: real estate, construction, management
- **Caution**: refusing change, a heavy conservatism

### Mu-Ja (戊子)

**Heavenly stem 戊 earth + earthly branch 子 water (편재 / 土剋水)**

戊子 is 편재 / 土剋水. Mu earth controls Ja water. The relation is 土剋水, not water controlling earth (水剋土). Both are yang, so the ten god is indirect wealth, 편재 (偏財, Pyeonjae). It is not indirect officer (편관, 偏官). An older page had this control backwards.

- **Energy often named**: a picture of earth handling water, read as wealth and flow
- **Sketch**: traditional texts place practical sense and calculation together. Reading this pillar as 편관 flips the control
- **Work often named**: distribution, trade, strategy, management
- **Caution**: inner conflict, a course that is hard to predict

## Day pillars of stem Gyeong (庚)

### Gyeong-O (庚午)

**Heavenly stem 庚 metal + earthly branch 午 fire (편관, Pyeongwan 偏官)**

O fire controls Gyeong metal (火剋金). Both are yang, so the ten god is indirect officer, 편관 (偏官). The image is steel tempered in fire.

- **Energy often named**: a strength the forging picture treats as hard to break
- **Sketch**: many early trials in the traditional picture, and a strength that picture says is hard to copy
- **Work often named**: the military, the police, sport, medicine
- **Caution**: impulse, harshness

### Gyeong-Sin (庚申)

**Heavenly stem 庚 metal + earthly branch 申 metal (비견, Bigyeon 比肩)**

Stem and branch are both yang metal, so the ten god is a peer, 비견 (比肩). This page drops any rank of the strongest.

- **Energy often named**: a sword with the edge set
- **Sketch**: decision and push drawn to an extreme. Straight toward a goal, without the compromise the texts mention
- **Work often named**: surgery, law, athletics, finance
- **Caution**: coldness, refusing compromise

## Day pillars of stem Im (壬)

### Im-O (壬午)

**Heavenly stem 壬 water + earthly branch 午 fire (편재, Pyeonjae 偏財)**

Im water controls O fire (水剋火). Both are yang, so fire is indirect wealth for this water, 편재 (偏財), not direct wealth. Traditional writing sketches an energy that handles large wealth.

- **Energy often named**: an image of the sea holding fire
- **Sketch**: traditional texts place large wealth and a social energy here, and a way of taking chances across fields
- **Work often named**: business, investment, entertainment planning
- **Caution**: instability, impulsive investment

### Im-Ja (壬子)

**Heavenly stem 壬 water + earthly branch 子 water (비견, Bigyeon 比肩)**

Stem and branch are both yang water, so the ten god is a peer, 비견 (比肩). This page drops any rank of the strongest.

- **Energy often named**: resourcefulness pictured as a deep sea
- **Sketch**: traditional texts say the resourcefulness runs deep. This page drops any rank of the finest among twelve day pillars, and it does not call this pillar the most strategic
- **Work often named**: strategist, diplomat, writer, researcher
- **Caution**: no fixed direction, chasing freedom too far

## Day pillars of stem Gye (癸)

### Gye-Hae (癸亥)

**Heavenly stem 癸 water + earthly branch 亥 water (비견, Bigyeon 比肩)**

Stem and branch are both yin water, so the ten god is a peer, 비견 (比肩). This page drops any rank of the deepest.

- **Energy often named**: an inner life pictured as the deep sea
- **Sketch**: intuition and sensitivity drawn very far, in the traditional picture. A strong pull toward art, philosophy, and mysticism
- **Work often named**: artist, philosopher, psychotherapist, religious life
- **Caution**: traditional texts describe a deep inner mood. This is not a diagnosis of depression. If the body shows symptoms, see a clinician

### Gye-Myo (癸卯)

**Heavenly stem 癸 water + earthly branch 卯 wood (식신, Siksin 食神)**

Myo wood is where Gye water's energy is expressed (水生木). Both are yin, so the ten god is the eating god, 식신 (食神). Traditional writing sketches creativity and expression.

- **Energy often named**: spring rain opening flowers
- **Sketch**: creating something beautiful from a fine sensitivity. Artistic talent and a soft presence, in the traditional picture
- **Work often named**: artist, writer, counselor, teacher
- **Caution**: spent energy, being too sensitive to other people's moods
