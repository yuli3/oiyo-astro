In many English lists, the March birth flower is the daffodil, in the genus Narcissus. Jonquil is often written next to it. A birth flower is a symbol tied to a month. It is not a portrait of people born in March.

Hartmann et al. (2006) did not find that birth date predicts personality. Controlled tests of birth-date or birth-chart personality prediction did not find an effect (Carlson, 1985; Hartmann et al., 2006). Those studies are about Western astrology and birth date. They are not a trial of this flower.

## The daffodil, and which plant that name covers

Narcissus pseudonarcissus is the wild daffodil. Garden daffodils are often hybrids, so a stem sold as a daffodil is not always that wild species.

### A myth, not a lesson

Ovid's Metamorphoses, book 3, tells of Narcissus wasting away over his reflection. Tellings differ about how the flower begins. It is a myth. It is not a finding that the death of the ego makes a person beautiful, and it is not a reading of character.

### A poem about the flowers

William Wordsworth's poem *I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud*, in *Poems in Two Volumes* (1807), is about daffodils. The poem is a literary response. It does not fix a meaning for the month, and it does not describe a person.

### Wales, the daffodil, and the leek

In Wales, a daffodil (Cennin Pedr) is worn on St David's Day, 1 March. The leek is the older emblem. This page does not treat the daffodil as the single official national flower, as if the leek were not there.

### A saying about one stem, or three

A repeated English folk saying holds that a single daffodil given as a gift is unlucky, and that the flowers are given in bunches, often of three. It is a saying, not a rule.

Gold is sometimes used only as a color metaphor for the solar-plexus image, not as anatomy, a symptom, or a frequency.

## Jonquil, a different species

Jonquil is Narcissus jonquilla. It often carries several flowers on one stem, with rush-like leaves and a strong scent. It shares the genus Narcissus with the daffodil. It is not the same species as the wild daffodil, Narcissus pseudonarcissus.

In a 19th-century popular language of flowers, jonquil was given the line *I desire a return of affection*. That line belongs to a list from that period. It is not a classical definition of the plant, and it is not a promise about love.

## The same common name is not always the same plant

The narcissus of Lunar New Year is usually Narcissus tazetta var. chinensis. It is a relative. It is not the same plant as the wild European daffodil.

Persian poetry often uses narges, and Japanese poetry often uses suisen (スイセン). Those images are frequently a tazetta narcissus as well. Putting them in one species with Narcissus pseudonarcissus would blur the plants.

Self-love is a gloss often repeated in modern lists for the narcissus. This page did not check that gloss in a classical flower dictionary, and it does not add respect as a sourced meaning.

## What the month symbol does not do

On these lists, the daffodil and the jonquil stand for the month of March. They do not announce who a March-born person is. They do not say that anyone is the start of a season. The flower is a traditional picture for a month, not a result about a person.
