As winter releases its grip and spring begins to breathe life back into the world, March arrives with its breathtaking birthstone — **aquamarine**. Named from the Latin *aqua marina* (sea water), this pale blue-green gem captures the essence of the ocean itself: vast, calming, and full of hidden depth. For centuries, it has been the crystal of choice for anyone seeking courage, clear communication, and emotional renewal.

## What Is Aquamarine?

Aquamarine belongs to the **beryl mineral family** — the same family as emerald. The iron content determines the color, ranging from pale sky blue to rich blue-green. As iron concentration increases, the stone deepens toward a more vibrant teal.

**Mohs hardness: 7.5–8**, making it an exceptionally durable stone suitable for all types of jewelry. Major sources include **Brazil** (Minas Gerais), Pakistan, Russia (Ural Mountains), Madagascar, and Nigeria.

The most prized color is called **Santa Maria**, after the Santa Maria de Itabira mine in Minas Gerais, Brazil — a rich, intense blue reminiscent of the deep ocean on a clear day. Today the name is also used for stones of similar color from other sources.

## History and Legend

### Ancient Sailors' Talisman

The relationship between aquamarine and the sea runs deep through history. Sailors of old are said to have linked aquamarine with the sea god **Poseidon (Neptune)** and carried it aboard ships as a talisman for good luck and safe passage.

**The Mermaid Legend**: Across many cultures, aquamarine was believed to be the treasure of mermaids. Stones washed ashore were considered gifts left by these mythical beings — which explains why the gem was thought to bring good fortune to those who found it.

**Medieval Divination**: Beryl, including aquamarine, was used as a scrying tool. Practitioners are said to have gazed into beryl spheres or bowls of water to see visions of the future or locate lost objects.

**Modern Royalty**: The aquamarine necklace and earrings given to Queen Elizabeth II by the president and people of Brazil for her 1953 coronation, together with the tiara later made to match them, are among the best-known royal aquamarines.

Aquamarine's blue energy aligns perfectly with the **throat chakra**, the energy center governing self-expression, honest communication, and the courage to speak one's truth. When the throat chakra is blocked, we may struggle to express ourselves, swallow important words, or feel unheard.

In crystal lore, aquamarine symbolizes easing these blocks — inviting clearer, more courageous communication. Some people wear an aquamarine necklace as a personal reminder before difficult conversations, presentations, or negotiations.

### Heart Chakra (Anahata)
The blue-green quality of aquamarine also resonates with the heart chakra. It symbolizes emotional renewal, forgiveness, and gently washing away old wounds that we've been carrying too long.

## Working with Aquamarine

### Wearing
Symbolically, aquamarine fits best as a **necklace**, close to the throat chakra it is associated with. On days when you need to speak clearly — or when you're processing difficult emotions — an aquamarine pendant or choker becomes more than jewelry.

### Meditation
Sit quietly with aquamarine in both hands. Imagine yourself floating peacefully on the open ocean, waves gently rocking you. Visualize old worries, fears, and grief being drawn away by the tides, leaving you lighter and clearer.

### Water Ritual
Aquamarine has a natural affinity with water. Place the stone in a bowl of clean water and leave it in moonlight overnight. The next morning, use this water to wash your face or hands as a cleansing ritual.

### Cleansing Methods
- **Moonlight**: Leave under the open sky on a clear night
- **Running water**: Rinse under flowing water for 1–2 minutes (aquamarine handles water well)
- **Sage smoke**: Pass through cleansing smoke
- **Avoid**: Extended harsh sunlight, chemical cleaners
