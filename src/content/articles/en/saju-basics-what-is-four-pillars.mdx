# The Architecture of Time: Decoding the Four Pillars

**Saju (the Four Pillars)** records birth year, month, day and hour using Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, then interprets those symbols within a traditional system. Reproducible calendar calculations and claims about personality or fate are different questions. The research cited here does not establish saju as a biological blueprint or a system validated by quantum mechanics.

The roots, seasons and trees below are metaphors for traditional interpretations, not biological mechanisms or diagnoses of a person.

## I. Calculation and interpretation are separate steps

The [Encyclopedia of Korean Culture entry on saju](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0025957) describes both the calendar structure—two characters for each of year, month, day and hour—and the tradition of reading fortune from it.

- **Calculation** converts birth information into four pillars under calendar rules. A pillar month need not begin on the first day of a civil-calendar month.
- **Interpretation** applies phase and relationship symbolism to the resulting characters. Agreement on a chart does not establish the accuracy of a reading.

When comparing charts, check the original inputs and the time and solar-term conventions used. Distinguish a calculation difference from an interpretive disagreement.

## II. Calendar calculations are not biological mechanisms

Solar terms, calculated from the Sun’s position, are used in determining the month pillar. A reproducible calendar rule does not by itself validate the interpretation that five phases determine personality or future events.

[NIH’s account of circadian rhythms](https://www.nigms.nih.gov/education/fact-sheets/Pages/circadian-rhythms) describes approximately 24-hour biological cycles responding to cues such as light and darkness. [Its explanation of epigenetics](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/understanding/howgeneswork/epigenome/) concerns regulation of gene activity without changing the DNA sequence. Neither account establishes that a solar-system electromagnetic field imprints personality on the nervous system at birth through saju.

Research relating seasons or environments to biology does not, on its own, validate saju readings. The traditional idea of qi should not be equated with a molecular mechanism.

## III. What information do the four pillars contain?

Each pillar contains one Heavenly Stem and one Earthly Branch: eight characters altogether. The relationship and life-stage themes below are traditional interpretations, not observations of a person’s family, workplace or marriage.

| Pillar | Calendar information | Themes often used in traditional readings |
|---|---|---|
| Year | Stem–branch pair for the birth year | Family background and early environment |
| Month | Pair for the month determined by solar terms | Season and surrounding conditions |
| Day | Pair for the birth day | Self and relationships, using the day stem as a reference |
| Hour | Pair corresponding to the birth time | Children, later generations and later-life symbolism |

The day master is the Heavenly Stem of the day pillar. Treating it as ‘the self’ is an interpretive convention, not evidence that the character causes personality. Fixed age bands such as 0–19, 20–39 and 40–59 are not universal laws of human development; assignments vary across traditions and schools.

## IV. Do not turn chart relationships into diagnoses

Clash, combination and void use different traditional rules. These examples identify chart categories; they are not evidence about a person’s character or future.

| Term | Rule and example |
|---|---|
| Branch clash | Opposite positions among twelve branches; 子–午 is an example. |
| Six branch combinations | Six specified pairs; 子–丑 is one. Stem combinations are separate. |
| Void | Two branches unpaired in a ten-pair xun; 戌 and 亥 in the 甲子 xun. |

Count the ten pairs from 甲子 through 乙丑, 丙寅, 丁卯, 戊辰, 己巳, 庚午, 辛未, 壬申 and 癸酉. Each stem appears once, but the branches run only from 子 to 酉, leaving 戌 and 亥 unpaired. This is not a count of missing phases in a chart.

See *Sanming Tonghui*, [volume 2 on clashes and six combinations](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/三命通會/卷二), and [volume 3 on void](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/三命通會/卷三#论空亡). Its fortune interpretations are not treated here as validated predictions. A combination does not establish stress, talent or a marriage’s condition.

## V. Use an interpretation as a question

If the image of ‘a tree born in winter’ resonates, turn it into a question such as ‘Which activities help me at the moment?’ It does not establish that particular people, jobs or hobbies are necessary for survival, or that a missing phase determines fate.

Base important health, career, financial and relationship decisions on actual circumstances and verifiable information. Reproducible chart calculation and valid prediction of an individual’s future are separate questions.

## References

The classical works below provide background for traditional readings of stems, branches and phases. Carlson (1985) tested personality interpretations in Western natal astrology; Hartmann and colleagues (2006) examined birth date and Sun signs in relation to personality and general intelligence in their samples. Neither is presented here as a direct test of saju or a finding that rules out every influence of the birth environment.
