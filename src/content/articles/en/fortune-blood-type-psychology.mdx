## Check what was measured before asking whether there is a relationship

Read sample, scale, analysis and effect size together. A large survey finding mostly nonsignificant differences and a different measure finding a small significant difference can coexist. Neither establishes all personality traits from blood type.

This article compares research methods and interpretations. [Blood type stories and stereotypes](/en/blood-type-personality-complete/) separately discusses cultural images and judgments in conversation. Neither article offers career aptitude lists, compatibility scores or medical prescriptions.

## Nawata 2014: original respondents and item-specific analysis counts

[Nawata’s official article page](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjpsy/85/2/85_85.13016/_article) and [official PDF](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjpsy/85/2/85_85.13016/_pdf/-char/en) analyse three Japanese and US datasets secondarily. Distinguish 11,729 original respondents from each item’s analysis count. Conditions such as excluding US respondents who did not know their ABO type change those numbers.

The measures comprise 21+26+21 everyday attitude items, 68 in total. Differences were nonsignificant for 65; the largest table η² rounds to .0030, about 0.3% or less. Everyday attitude questions and standardized personality tests are not identical measures. “Exactly zero differences in every personality trait” exceeds this scope.

## Tsuchimine 2015: keep the small significant differences

[Tsuchimine and colleagues’ PLOS original](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0126983) recruited 1,572 and analysed 1,427 with complete data. Medical students and hospital staff completed the Temperament and Character Inventory (TCI) self-report and underwent genetic testing.

The paper reports small significant Persistence differences: partial η² .010 for genotype and .006 for phenotype. Saying “no ABO association appeared at all” is inaccurate. This does not mean an individual of a particular type must be more persistent or suited to a career.

The recruited group, insufficient control of environmental factors and self-report limit interpretation. A statistical group difference is not individual prediction accuracy. Observing an association does not establish a mechanism by which ABO produces personality.

## Rogers & Glendon 2003: analysed sample and access scope

Official abstract search returns from [the publisher’s page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886902001010) distinguish 360 recruited from 240 finally analysed, with 30 in each blood type–sex combination. Predicted blood type personality differences were not confirmed on Big Five and LOT-R.

We read official abstract search returns; direct full-text access failed. Effect size was not verified. We do not rank its effect against other studies or conclude there is no effect in every culture, age or condition.

## Significance, effect size and individual prediction are different questions

Statistical significance does not mean a large effect or usefulness for everyday decisions. Small group differences cannot describe diverse individuals as four personalities or validate their job performance or relationship behaviour.

η² and partial η² are distinct effect-size measures and require their models and conditions. These studies measure different outcomes: attitudes, TCI, Big Five and LOT-R. Comparing .0030 and .010 alone cannot establish accuracy or how many times stronger a blood type explanation is.

Career decisions require tasks and experience; relationships require consent and actual behaviour. A significant finding cannot decide another person’s wishes or actions for them.

## Separate biology and diet study scopes too

[NCBI’s ABO textbook](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK2267/?report=printable) explains carbohydrate A/B determinants, glycoproteins and glycolipids. Type O lacks A/B determinants but has the H precursor; “no antigens at all” is wrong. This biology is not a personality scale or a personality mechanism study. It also does not establish that ABO cannot have any association with the brain or disease.

[Wang and colleagues’ 2014 PLOS original](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0084749) analysed cross-sectional data from 1,455 and did not support additional benefits of matching diets to blood type. Dietary pattern–health associations are not evidence that matching caused them. Cross-sectional data establish neither causation nor personal prescriptions and do not refute every ABO–disease study. Do not turn these findings into blood type diet or exercise instructions.

## Check date and remaining limits

On 9 October 2026, we checked Nawata’s official PDF methods and results, the Tsuchimine and Wang originals and relevant NCBI passages. Rogers was checked through official abstract search returns only; full text and effect size remain unverified. No original-data reanalysis or personal prediction model validation was performed.

This comparison preserves differences in the research. Both “no study found any difference” and “blood type determines personality” fail to convey the scope of these sources accurately.
