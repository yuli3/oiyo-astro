## Anxiety is not explained by one practised skill

Repeated worry does not mean repetition is its only cause. [NIMH’s anxiety disorders guide](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders) distinguishes everyday anxiety from disorders that persist or interfere with life, and describes multiple contributing factors. Calling anxiety only a “well-practised skill” can overlook biological, environmental and personal conditions and place responsibility for not improving on the person experiencing it.

[NIMH’s generalized anxiety disorder guide](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/generalized-anxiety-disorder-gad) explains causes and treatment. Experiencing repeated worry alone does not diagnose a particular disorder. This article does not provide a diagnosis or individual treatment either.

## Learning studies and anxiety treatment ask different questions

Neuroplasticity research explores change. Whether a particular self-training exercise treats anxiety requires direct evidence about that exercise and clinical outcomes. [Draganski and colleagues’ juggling MRI study](https://dbm.neuro.uni-jena.de/pdf-files/Draganski-Nature.pdf) divided 24 people without juggling experience into a learning group and a nonlearning control group matched for age and sex. MRI comparisons after three months of practice and three months without practice showed some grey matter changes in the learning group, while the nonlearning group had no changes over the same period. The microscopic mechanisms are unclear. The study did not test anxiety treatment or personality redesign.

[Lally and colleagues’ habit study abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674) concerns 96 people repeating eating, drinking or activity behaviours over 12 weeks and self-reporting automaticity. It did not measure worry or anxiety disorder treatment outcomes. Familiarity with an action, an imaging difference, symptom reduction and improved everyday functioning are separate outcomes.

## Naming a brain mechanism does not prove an effect

Explanations of labelling a thought, shifting attention and congratulating yourself sometimes use phrases such as “activating the prefrontal cortex,” “calming the amygdala” or “dopamine securing a new pathway.” The sources checked here did not measure these brain changes in the individual reader or validate the anxiety treatment effects of this three-step combination.

This article does not propose those actions as a new treatment protocol. Distinguish a description containing brain terminology from a clinical effect. Persistent discomfort does not establish insufficient practice or weak willpower. You are not instructed to suppress worry completely or always replace it with another thought.

## Twenty-one days, 66 days and six months are not personal recovery deadlines

In [Lally results returned from the university repository](https://repositorio.ispa.pt/bitstream/10400.12/3364/1/IJSP_998-1009.pdf), 66 days is the estimated median time to 95% of the modelled automaticity asymptote among 39 participants with well-fitting models. The range of 18 to 254 days is also estimated. It is neither the mean for all 96 participants nor an anxiety treatment deadline, and it does not mean everyone was directly followed for 254 days. Direct full-text access failed; these detailed results were checked through search returns only.

That evidence does not establish neural recovery stages at one to four weeks, two to three months and six months. Not becoming calm on a schedule does not mean failure. Treatment choices and review timing need discussion in relation to individual circumstances and professional assessment.

## Lifestyle measures do not replace treatment

NIMH’s generalized anxiety disorder guide describes support including psychotherapy, medication or a combination, with choices depending on the individual situation. Lifestyle measures do not replace professional treatment. This article does not select a treatment for you or advise starting or stopping medication.

If anxiety persists or affects sleep, work, study or relationships, you can discuss your condition and possible support with a medical or mental health professional. A duration alone does not diagnose you, and you do not have to endure everything by yourself before seeking help.

## Notes for a consultation are not treatment exercises

When preparing for a consultation, you can note when discomfort occurred and how everyday life was affected. The table is an editorial communication example, not a diagnostic scale, treatment exercise or recovery score. You can ask for support without a detailed record.

## Sources checked and remaining limits

On 9 October 2026, we directly checked NIMH’s anxiety disorders page and the causes, treatment and lifestyle sections of its generalized anxiety disorder guide, revised in 2025. We read the methods, results and mechanism limitations in Draganski’s original and the Wiley abstract. Detailed Lally results were checked through repository search returns. We did not review or reanalyse every full paper.

These sources do not establish prefrontal, amygdala or dopamine mechanisms for a particular self-training protocol or a fixed anxiety recovery schedule. They do not establish that every self-care action is ineffective either. This article separates what research actually observed from the assessment an individual may need.
