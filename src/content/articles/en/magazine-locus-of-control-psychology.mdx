## Do you expect your actions to change the outcome?

After several rejected job applications, you might wonder: “Should I change my preparation, or has hiring changed?” Locus of control concerns your expectations about the relationship between your actions and their outcomes.

In Rotter’s concept, the focus is on generalized expectations about the relationship between behavior and reinforcement, not just explaining causes after an event. An internal expectation connects outcomes with your own actions or characteristics; an external expectation places them more with luck, chance or other people’s power. These expectations do not measure your actual authority, available resources or the fairness of a situation.

They do not divide people into “good” and “bad,” or “active” and “lazy.” This article is a reflection aid, not a way to diagnose mental health, ability or personal responsibility for what happens in someone’s life.

## Expectations, actual options and responsibility

You can believe different preparation might help without being able to decide how many jobs are available. Conversely, not knowing the assessment criteria does not mean there is nothing you could review in your application.

The following table is an OIYO explanation aid, not a set of validated subscales.

| Question to separate | Example in a job application | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What do you expect could change the outcome? | You think different preparation might affect the assessment | How much the assessment would actually change |
| What authority and resources do you have? | You can edit a paragraph before submitting, but cannot set hiring numbers | Your worth or the quality of your personality |
| What responsibilities and support are relevant? | Checking factual accuracy or seeking help with an unfair process | That the entire outcome is one person’s fault |

Making higher internal control a goal in itself can mean taking responsibility for outcomes that are not entirely yours to determine. Recognizing an external constraint is not necessarily avoiding responsibility. But a higher external expectation does not establish a protective mental-health effect either.

Before acting, seeking support or stopping, you can examine the information and resources available, along with the likely costs. This is a suggestion for thinking, not a clinical standard for “healthy” control.

## Learned helplessness: keep research contexts distinct

Learned helplessness research and locus of control are not the same theory or measure. The former has experimentally compared controllable and uncontrollable conditions; the latter concerns expectations.

[Maier and Seligman’s 2016 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4920136/) revises the original interpretation of the mechanism. Drawing particularly on animal research, it distinguishes passive responding to prolonged aversive stimulation from learning control. Reducing this to “repeated failures teach the brain helplessness” would omit that revision.

Animal experiments, human experimental tasks and long-term living conditions are not interchangeable. The review does not establish that a few small successes restore everyone’s agency. Nor does it diagnose why a particular person cannot act or justify calling them lazy.

## A job application: what can you actually check?

This is a fictional example, not an experiment measuring a method’s effectiveness. After three rejections, Minseo thinks: “Nothing I do matters.” Yet the rejections alone cannot distinguish a problem in the application, a mismatch with experience requirements or reduced hiring. They do not prove a lack of effort either.

<details>
<summary>See how Minseo separates observations from interpretations</summary>

1. **Observed:** three applications were rejected, without detailed reasons.
2. **Still unknown:** the criteria actually used, the clarity of the application and changes in hiring require separate checks.
3. **An option:** if Minseo wants to, compare one job posting with a paragraph of the application, or ask a trusted person what that paragraph communicates. Taking a break or choosing another route remains an option if the burden is too high.
4. **The interpretation limit:** feedback that the paragraph is clearer concerns that paragraph. It does not guarantee a job or establish an improved locus of control or greater personal worth.

</details>

The point is not to increase activity at any cost. It is to separate facts from missing information. If discrimination or unfair demands are the issue, asking only for a change of mindset does not address the problem.

## If you want to, examine one situation

These three areas are an optional OIYO organizing aid, not Rotter’s scale or a validated recovery program. You can choose not to write anything. The boundaries can change with the situation and your resources.

| Area to examine | What you might note | A caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A directly available choice | Editing a sentence before submission, deciding whether to ask a question | Include time, health and cost constraints |
| An action requiring agreement | Asking for feedback, proposing a schedule change | Another person’s response is not guaranteed |
| Outside your current authority or still unknown | Assessment reasons, hiring numbers, a past decision | Do not immediately assign the unknown to your fault or to luck |

If you try something, you could start with a specific question: “Does this paragraph communicate my experience more clearly?” A small action is not always better than a large one; choose its scope according to the information you need and the burden involved.

If nothing changes, that does not establish a lack of will or agency. An unsuitable method, insufficient information and external constraints remain alternative explanations. Recording actions, observations and uncertainties separately is an option, not an obligation.

The [OIYO locus of control test](/en/locus-of-control-test/) can also be a reflection prompt. It uses its own questions and score bands: do not equate it with Rotter’s original scale or interpret its results as validated diagnoses or developmental stages. You can focus on which situation you had in mind when answering.
