## Stress relief = self-care

"Relieving stress" is vague. It becomes actionable only when you turn it into **concrete actions on three axes**. (Self-care definition →)

### 1. Physical care
Sleep, food, movement, rest. This page does not claim that most emotional problems are blood sugar. If headache, heartburn, neck and shoulder tension, sleep changes, hair loss, or skin changes show up, do not explain them as stress alone. See a clinician.

### 2. Mental care
This is about the quality of stimulation: an information diet, an absorbing hobby, learning. Yerkes and Dodson (1908) reported that a middle level of arousal helped some learning tasks, and that too little or too much arousal hurt performance. It is not a law of all vitality.

### 3. Emotional / everyday care
Naming emotions, time without a mask, time with people you like. Holt-Lunstad et al. (2010) studied social relationships and mortality risk, not a happiness score. Lieberman et al. (2007) reported that putting feelings into words reduced amygdala response.

## Your mind's protective response — defense mechanisms

Under stress, people use responses that protect the self. That is not simply bad, and it is common. The groups below are a simplified Vaillant hierarchy. This page does not diagnose the reader.

- **Mature** — sublimation (channeling urges into creation or exercise), humor, suppression
- **Neurotic** — rationalization, repression, reaction formation
- **Immature** — projection, regression
- **Psychotic** — denial

The more intense the stress, the easier it is to slip into immature mechanisms. When the thought "that person hates me" (projection) arises, simply knowing it may be a defense mechanism lets you step back. (Defense mechanisms →)

## Relief and avoidance are different

This is the distinction people mix up. **Binge eating, heavy drinking, and endless scrolling** can postpone the signal. This page has no evidence that those acts themselves restore you.

## How do you know it's relieved?

The standard is less your subjective mood and more **the change in signals**.

1. Has your **sleep quality** returned?
2. Has **physical tension** eased?
3. Has your **emotional range** recovered — neither numb nor over-reactive?
4. Do you find yourself **wanting to do things again**?

The fourth matters most. The opposite of apathy isn't pleasure — it's **the desire to do**.

## Check your state now

Move past a vague feeling and start with measurement.

👉 [Holmes-Rahe stress test](https://oiyo.net/en/stress-events/test/) · [Burnout test](https://oiyo.net/en/burnout/test/) · [Emotion diary](https://oiyo.net/en/emotion-diary/)

Stress isn't an enemy to eliminate but a lifelong companion to manage. The goal isn't zero, but a **sweet spot** — and a **rhythm of recovery** that brings you back when you fall.
