Traditional East Asian medicine and myeongri (the study of saju, the Four Pillars) both rest on **five-phase (五行) theory**. The classics of traditional medicine sorted organs, senses and emotions into five groups, and some saju commentaries have borrowed that scheme to talk about health. This guide explains what those traditional correspondences are and how far they can reasonably be taken.

<Callout type="warning">
**Medical notice**: This article introduces a traditional system of thought and is not medical advice. Saju and the five phases cannot predict or diagnose illness, and there is no scientific evidence that any food or supplement can «strengthen» an element to prevent or cure disease. If you have symptoms or are worried about your health, please see a doctor.
</Callout>

## The five phases and the body: traditional correspondences

| Phase | Organs | Sense / tissue | Emotion |
|------|------|---------|------|
| 木 Wood | Liver, gallbladder | Eyes, tendons | Anger |
| 火 Fire | Heart, small intestine | Tongue, blood vessels | Joy |
| 土 Earth | Spleen, stomach | Mouth, flesh | Pensiveness, worry |
| 金 Metal | Lungs, large intestine | Nose, skin, body hair | Grief |
| 水 Water | Kidneys, bladder | Ears, bones, head hair | Fear |

This table is a **symbolic classification** set out in ancient medical texts such as the *Huangdi Neijing*, which sorted the world into five categories. The «liver» or «kidneys» here are not the same as the organs of modern anatomy, and these correspondences have not been validated by modern medicine. Please read what follows as an account of what the tradition has said.

## Traditional readings, phase by phase

### 木 Wood

- **Traditional correspondences**: liver and gallbladder, tendons, eyes, spring, sour taste
- **Emotional theme**: anger, frustration. In saju readings, «blocked Wood» is often used as a metaphor for the pent-up feeling of not being able to pursue what you want.
- **Something to reflect on**: whether you have an outlet for built-up anger, such as exercise, conversation or writing.

### 火 Fire

- **Traditional correspondences**: heart and small intestine, blood vessels, tongue, summer, bitter taste
- **Emotional theme**: joy, excitement. The tradition saw even excessive excitement as a loss of balance.
- **Something to reflect on**: whether you set aside quiet time to rest when stimulating days pile up.

### 土 Earth

- **Traditional correspondences**: spleen and stomach, flesh, mouth, the turn of the seasons (late summer), sweet taste
- **Emotional theme**: pensiveness, worry. The tradition linked Earth with going over the same thought again and again.
- **Something to reflect on**: whether you have a habit of writing worries down or sharing them with someone you trust.

### 金 Metal

- **Traditional correspondences**: lungs and large intestine, skin, nose, autumn, pungent taste
- **Emotional theme**: grief, loss. The tradition saw Metal as the energy of sorting out and letting go.
- **Something to reflect on**: whether you have a place to express sadness in a healthy way rather than holding it in.

### 水 Water

- **Traditional correspondences**: kidneys and bladder, bones, ears, head hair, winter, salty taste
- **Emotional theme**: fear, and on its other side, willpower. The tradition saw Water as the power to store and endure.
- **Something to reflect on**: when a vague fear grows large, writing down exactly what you are afraid of can help.

The pairing of tastes with phases (sour with Wood, bitter with Fire, and so on) is only a traditional classification. It does not mean that eating a certain flavour will benefit the matching organ. Physical symptoms such as fatigue, headaches, indigestion, coughing or back pain should not be read through the five phases; see a doctor to find out the cause.

## About readings that link the day master or yongsin to health

Some saju commentaries use the day master (the heavenly stem of your birth day) or the yongsin (useful element) to name «organs to watch». There is no evidence that a birth date can predict the risk of any particular disease, and relying on such readings could lead you to skip check-ups you actually need.

Regular sleep, a balanced diet, steady exercise, not smoking, drinking in moderation and age-appropriate health screenings are recommended for everyone, whatever their saju.

## The seasons and the five phases

The tradition held that in each season some phases rise and others wane (the cycle of prosperity and decline, 旺相休囚死).

| Season | Rising phase | Waning phase | Everyday rhythm |
|------|---------|------------|-----------|
| Spring | 木 | 土 | Gradually spend more time outdoors |
| Summer | 火 | 金 | Mind the heat and drink enough fluids |
| Seasonal turns | 土 | 水 | Adjust clothing and sleep to temperature swings |
| Autumn | 金 | 木 | Watch for dry air and air out rooms |
| Winter | 水 | 火 | Keep warm and rest well |

The right-hand column lists ordinary seasonal habits; it does not mean that five-phase theory supports them medically.
