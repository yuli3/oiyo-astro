### Where the sentence comes from

**All problems are interpersonal relationship problems.** The line is widely quoted. Alfred Adler did not write it. Ichiro Kishimi and Fumitake Koga put it in *The Courage to Be Disliked* (『嫌われる勇気』, 2013, Diamond Inc.), a dialogue that introduces Adlerian ideas.

The theme Adler himself kept returning to is closer to social interest, Gemeinschaftsgefühl: an interest in other people and a sense that one contributes to something.

The book offers a thought experiment. If you were the only person left on Earth, which worries would remain? Money, appearance, and school comparisons often take their shape from other people. Depression does not. A person can be depressed with no one else present. This page does not repeat the claim that inner depression cannot exist without others.

---

### 1. An inferiority feeling is not an inferiority complex

Comparing height or salary with someone else sits closer to what Adler called an inferiority feeling. The height is a fact. Reading that fact as inferior is an interpretation made in comparison.

An inferiority complex is a different term. It names a stuck pattern, not every moment of comparison.

### 2. Vertical and horizontal, as the book uses them

*The Courage to Be Disliked* calls ranking people above or below you a vertical relationship, and seeing them as equal companions a horizontal one. Those are the book's terms. They are not the Wikipedia articles on hierarchy or on equality.

Ranking can make life feel like a race that does not end. Seeing people as companions is a practice the book proposes. It is not a switch that turns relationships from threats into blessings.

### 3. Separation of tasks

What that person thinks of you is, in the book, that person's task. You can do well and still be disliked. You can make a mistake and still be supported. Their reading is outside your hands.

People do postpone their own tasks to meet someone else's expectation. This page uses self-respect for the question of whose task it is. It does not link that word to an unrelated encyclopedia entry.

### 4. Social interest

Adler's social interest is a move away from a self that has grown only toward itself, toward an interest in others and a sense of contributing. The contribution does not have to be a grand sacrifice. A greeting, or doing the work that is yours, belongs in that account.

The feeling does not guarantee happiness. The book's proposal is that asking what you can offer leaves more of the initiative with you than asking only what you can get.

### 5. The courage to be disliked

The courage to be disliked is not the last piece that cuts off every worry. Trying to be loved by everyone does spend a lot of energy. The courage does not erase the worries.

Being disliked is not proof that you are free, and it is not a law of physics. Some of your choices will not be agreed with. The book asks whether you can live with that disagreement. Being disliked is not itself the goal.

---

### Closing

The line is not advice to cut people off. It is a frame for noticing whose gaze is standing inside a worry. Seeing the gaze does not turn a rock into a feather. Some weights get lighter. Some stay.

We live with other people. Togetherness is not the same thing as confinement. One thing you can do today is to separate your task from someone else's, once.
