## Introduction: Why Did Jung Obsess Over Weird Old Books?

Alchemy was a historical practice combining work on material substances with philosophical and religious inquiry. Its activities extended beyond metal transmutation to pigments and mineral studies. It is therefore inaccurate to say that alchemists ignored matter and only refined their souls.

Jung interpreted alchemical images as symbols of individuation. This is his psychological reading, not historical proof of every alchemist’s intentions or clinical evidence of a treatment’s effectiveness.

## Color symbols are not treatment stages

The following is a selective way to read nigredo, albedo and rubedo. It does not imply that every alchemical text follows one sequence or that everyone must experience it. In Jung’s framework, individuation concerns the relationship between consciousness and the unconscious; it is not a score for becoming a perfect person.

| Symbol | Possible reflective reading | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nigredo · blackening | Notice confusion when familiar explanations no longer fit | Depression or breakdown is necessary for growth |
| Albedo · whitening | Review experience and distinguish observations from assumptions | Emotions must be removed to become pure |
| Rubedo · reddening | Connect reflection with small everyday choices | Insight alone resolves life’s problems |

For example, discomfort after changing jobs is not automatically a “nigredo stage.” Workload, relationships, sleep and employment conditions deserve attention first. A symbol can help describe experience; it is not a test that identifies its cause.

## The philosopher’s stone and the Self

Calling the philosopher’s stone nothing but a psychological symbol would again confuse history with interpretation. Here we focus on Jung’s reading without reducing all alchemists’ aims to one explanation.

In this framework, the Self refers to a psychic totality broader than the conscious ego. The stone can be read as an image of that wholeness, not a guarantee of an “unbreakable” personality. Recognizing conflicting wishes is different from justifying harmful behavior. Integration does not remove responsibility or other people’s boundaries.

## Mercurius: a metaphor is not a prescription

In Jung’s interpretation of alchemy, Mercurius is treated as a symbol that holds contradictions together. A material name and a symbolic figure are not interchangeable. Language about poison becoming medicine must not be turned into instructions for using a substance.

Likewise, a troubling complex is not guaranteed to become the key to salvation. You might find meaning in difficulty, but distance, support and changes to your circumstances are also legitimate choices. You have no duty to make suffering valuable.

## Questions for everyday reflection

These are ordinary journaling examples proposed in this article, not Jung’s standardized treatment procedure or a validated intervention.

1. What actually happened, and what interpretation did I add?
2. Do I need more analysis, or rest, information, support or a change in circumstances?
3. What boundaries should remain while I acknowledge conflicting wishes?
4. What small, manageable action could I try today?

Wanting to do well and wanting to rest need not make either wish an impurity. You could consider both the deadline and recovery time, reduce the workload or ask for support. Stop the exercise if writing makes distress worse.

## Limits and support

Nigredo is not a depression diagnosis, and an alchemical metaphor is not a substitute for care. Persistent distress or difficulty managing everyday life can be a reason to seek help from a medical or mental health professional. In a dangerous situation, immediate safety takes priority over symbolic interpretation.

Change does not have to come through suffering. Learning within stable relationships and supportive conditions matters too.
