## Reflect on relationships without ranking people

Seeing a friend may help you start studying; a late gathering may disrupt revision. These experiences can prompt reflection. They do not establish that another person determines your life.

“You are the average of five people” is not a formula for income or personality. The group and the quantity being averaged are undefined. This article provides neither research validating that number nor a verified original quotation, and does not attribute it to Adler.

## 1. Separate theory from evidence of effects

The North American Society for Adlerian Psychology identifies style of life and social interest among Adler's concepts. Social interest considers others' welfare alongside one's own. This differs from choosing only successful friends. [Society overview](https://www.alfredadler.org/alfred-adler)

The suggestions below are editorial reflection prompts, not an Adler-authored assessment or validated treatment. They do not guarantee improved grades or income.

## 2. Similarity has several possible explanations

Friends may influence one another, form friendships because they were already similar, or share circumstances such as workplace schedules. Shalizi and Thomas examine the difficulty of separating these causes in observational network research. Their argument does not establish that influence never occurs. [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.4704)

| Observation | Possible explanation | Still unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Friends study at similar times | A shared schedule or previously similar habits | How much the friendship changes results |
| Colleagues work late together | Workload, team expectations, individual choices | Whether colleagues' attitudes cause it |
| Plans slip after a gathering | Travel time, fatigue, meeting format | Whether a particular person is “bad” |

Invoking mirror neurons does not establish why emotions or habits change in these situations. This article supplies no direct evidence that this neural mechanism explains these examples.

## 3. Choices come with constraints

Care responsibilities, housing costs, employment, health, and transport can limit the ability to change a setting. Remaining does not establish that someone wanted the situation or is responsible for harm done to them.

Rather than asking why someone failed to leave, ask what they can safely adjust now. Reducing optional meetings has different costs and risks from changing a job or home. When threats or coercion are involved, prioritize safety and access to support rather than treating the situation as a productivity experiment.

## 4. Adjust repeated conditions in small ways

Imagine an exam candidate who misses revision after late meetings. Before deciding that a friend obstructs their growth, they could consider a different time or format. This is a hypothetical example, not an intervention with measured success rates.

| Condition | Small adjustment | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meeting time | Meet during the day rather than late at night | Sleep and the next commitment |
| Communication boundaries | Explain that replies may be delayed while studying | Whether both parties respect boundaries |
| Study support | Explain a problem together | Actual understanding |
| Travel demands | Consider an online meeting | Cost and fatigue |

One favorable result does not prove causation. Task difficulty or health may also have changed. Look for repeated patterns without converting observations into scores for people's worth.

<summary>Questions for reflecting on your environment</summary>

What happened? What did you do differently afterward? Where did you infer someone else's intentions? Separate adjustable conditions from those you cannot readily change. Check whether the other person can also choose or decline. Do not add answers into a score or use them as a rule for ending a relationship.

</details>

## 5. Consider the environment you offer others

Keeping time commitments, asking before giving advice, and accepting a refusal provide concrete things to reflect on. Other people are not simply resources for your success.

Being considerate does not guarantee that only considerate people will enter your life. A difficult relationship does not demonstrate low personal worth. The aim is not to replace five people, but to find more cooperative conditions while acknowledging real constraints.

- [Shalizi & Thomas author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.4704): a paper published in 2011, not a predictor of individual relationship outcomes.
