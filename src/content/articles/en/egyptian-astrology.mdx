**Please note**: The popular "Egyptian zodiac" of twelve gods with date ranges is a modern invention. No text from ancient Egypt assigns gods to birth dates. What is genuinely attested is different: the 36 **decans**, star groups painted on coffin lids from around the Middle Kingdom and used to tell the hours of the night, and the **Dendera zodiac**, a temple ceiling relief carved around 50 BCE in the Greco-Roman (Ptolemaic) period, which shows the Babylonian-Greek zodiac signs. There is no scientific evidence that birth dates shape personality. Please read the descriptions below as symbolic sketches for reflection.

## 1. Genesis: The Dawn of Time

The Egyptians created one of the earliest 365-day solar calendars. They noticed that when **Sirius**, the brightest star in the night sky, rose again at dawn, the Nile began to flood. The flood was not a disaster but a blessing of fertility. Modern "Egyptian astrology" borrows its imagery from this sacred seasonal rhythm.

## 2. Find Your Egyptian God

In the modern popular system, each birth date is assigned to one of twelve gods. This assignment has no ancient source, so treat each portrait as a symbol rather than a verdict.

### ① The Nile

- Symbol of constancy and peace.
- Emotional, avoids conflict, seeks serenity. The most tolerant of all.

### ② Amon-Ra

- King of the gods, the Sun.
- A born leader with charisma. Full of confidence and wants to be at the center of the world.

### ③ Mut

- The Great Mother.
- Protective and practical. Looks cool on the outside but holds a deep love within.

### ④ Geb

- God of the Earth.
- Reliable and serious. Proves themselves through quiet action rather than fine words.

### ⑤ Osiris

- Lord of resurrection and ruler of the underworld.
- A living paradox. Strong yet gentle, wise yet innocent. Always longing for a new beginning.

### ⑥ Isis

- Goddess of magic and the home.
- Intuitive and quick to read people. Finds joy in caring for others.

### ⑦ Thoth

- God of wisdom and writing.
- Endlessly curious, a natural problem solver. Trusts logic more than emotion.

### ⑧ Horus

- Falcon god of the sky.
- Brave and ambitious. Does not fear danger and has a strong sense of justice.

### ⑨ Anubis

- Guide of the dead.
- Introverted and mysterious (the hardest to read). Treasures time alone.

### ⑩ Seth

- God of chaos and storms.
- Seeks change and freedom. Cannot stand the ordinary and is always off on the next adventure.

### ⑪ Bastet

- Cat goddess.
- Charming and elegant. Territorial, yet deeply affectionate with those close to them.

### ⑫ Sekhmet

- Lioness goddess of war and healing.
- High moral standards, a perfectionist. A champion of justice who will not tolerate corruption.

## 3. Core Philosophy: The Feather of Ma'at

The Egyptians believed that after death the heart was weighed against a feather. If it was heavier with wrongdoing, it was devoured. That feather is **Ma'at (truth and harmony)**. Whatever system you use, the question Egypt leaves us is not about predicting luck but about reflection: "Is my life in balance with Ma'at?"

## 4. In Closing: Toward Eternal Life

For the ancient Egyptians, death was not an end but a beginning. Think of your symbolic guardian as a companion on the long journey along the Nile of this life toward the Afterlife. Borrow its wisdom to build your own pyramid of lasting achievements.

### FAQ

**Q: Did the ancient Egyptians use this 12-god zodiac?**
A: No. The date-based 12-god system is a modern creation. Ancient Egyptians used the decans to tell time at night, and the zodiac signs only appear in Egypt in the Greco-Roman period, as on the Dendera ceiling.

**Q: Can the dates overlap?**
A: Yes. In the modern system the periods are discontinuous. For example, the "Nile" sign covers dates in both January and November. These ranges come from modern books, not from ancient sources.

**Q: Can I use it alongside Western astrology?**
A: Of course, as long as you treat both as symbolic languages. If Western astrology looks to the "heavens", the Egyptian imagery looks to the "earth" and its seasons. Together they can offer a wider view for reflection.
