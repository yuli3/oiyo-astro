## Checking "ancient wisdom"

Spiritual and self-help content often describes a system as "a secret handed down for thousands of years." Trace the history of these systems, though, and you often find **older original traditions** mixed with **interpretations created in modern times**. Telling them apart lets you engage with each system more accurately — and more respectfully.

## Hermeticism and the "seven universal laws"

- **Original tradition**: the *Corpus Hermeticum* is a body of Greek texts from Egypt, roughly 2nd–3rd century CE, dealing with the relationship between God, the cosmos and humanity (Copenhaver, 1992). It strongly influenced Renaissance European thought.
- **Modern interpretation**: the **seven laws** — "mentalism, correspondence, vibration…" — come from *The Kybalion*, published in the United States in 1908 under the name "Three Initiates." It is a book strongly coloured by the early-20th-century New Thought movement; the ancient Hermetic texts contain no such list.

## Kabbalah and the Tree of Life

- **Original tradition**: Kabbalah is a Jewish mystical tradition. After early texts such as the *Sefer Yetzirah*, it was systematised with the appearance of the *Zohar* in 13th-century Spain. The Tree of Life, made of ten **sefirot**, expresses how divine attributes flow into the world (Scholem, 1941).
- **Modern interpretation**: 19th-century Western occult societies created "Hermetic Qabalah," linking the Tree of Life to tarot and astrology. It is a separate tradition from Jewish Kabbalah.

## Chakras: when did seven become the standard?

- **Original tradition**: chakras are centres of bodily energy described in Indian tantric texts. The number and placement vary between texts; some traditions speak of four, six or more.
- **Modern interpretation**: today's familiar **seven chakras with rainbow colours** spread in the West after John Woodroffe (writing as Arthur Avalon) translated and introduced the 16th-century text *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa* in *The Serpent Power* (1919); the colour scheme was added later by the New Age movement. No anatomical structures corresponding to chakras have been found.

## The three gunas

- **Original tradition**: Indian Sāṃkhya philosophy holds that all phenomena are made of three qualities — **sattva (clarity, balance), rajas (movement, passion) and tamas (heaviness, inertia)**. The *Bhagavad Gītā* discusses them in detail in chapter 14.
- **How to use it**: as a language for reflection, naming your current state of mind by the three qualities. It is not a tool for sorting people into fixed types.

## The Maya Tzolk'in and "In Lak'ech"

- **Original tradition**: the **Tzolk'in** is a **260-day ritual calendar** combining 13 numbers with 20 day names. In K'iche' Maya communities of highland Guatemala, "daykeepers" still keep this calendar today (Tedlock, 1982).
- **Modern interpretation**: the "13 Moon" calendar and the system known as "Dreamspell" are modern creations by José Argüelles in the 1980s (Argüelles, 1987) and do not match the traditional Maya day count. **"In Lak'ech"** ("you are my other me") spread through the Chicano movement via Luis Valdez's poetry in the early 1970s; evidence that it comes from Classic Maya texts is weak.

## Celtic tree astrology

- **Original tradition**: **Ogham** is a real script used in Ireland around the 4th–6th centuries. Medieval texts associated its letters with tree names.
- **Modern interpretation**: the **"Celtic tree calendar"** that assigns a tree sign by birthday began with Robert Graves's *The White Goddess* (1948). There is no historical evidence that the ancient Celts used such an astrological calendar.

## Human Design

- **Origin**: a system created in 1987 by Alan Krakower (Ra Uru Hu), based on a revelation he said he received. It combines the I Ching, astrology, Kabbalah and chakras to sort people into types such as Generators and Projectors (Bunnell & Ra Uru Hu, 2011).
- **Evidence**: no research has tested its type classification or the effects of its "strategies." It helps to know it is **a modern composite system**.

## Summary at a glance

| System | Original tradition | Added in modern times |
|---|---|---|
| Hermeticism | *Corpus Hermeticum* (2nd–3rd c.) | "Seven laws" from *The Kybalion* (1908) |
| Kabbalah | Jewish mysticism, *Zohar* (13th c.) | Hermetic Qabalah (19th-c. occultism) |
| Chakras | Tantric texts, varying numbers | Seven-chakra standard (1919), rainbow colours |
| Gunas | Sāṃkhya philosophy, *Bhagavad Gītā* | Use as a language for reflection |
| Maya Tzolk'in | 260-day ritual calendar (still in use) | "13 Moons" and Dreamspell (1980s) |
| Celtic trees | The Ogham script | Tree astrology calendar (1948) |
| Human Design | — | A composite system created in 1987 |

Being old does not make something true, and being new does not make it worthless. But **knowing where something comes from** is the first step in respecting each tradition.
