## 1. The History and Origins of Tarot

Tarot cards are one of the best-known divination tools in the world today. Their origins, however, lie not in fortune-telling but in a **card game**.

### 15th-Century Italy: The Game of Trionfi

The earliest form of tarot is generally traced to **Trionfi** cards, which appeared in northern Italy in the 1440s. They were made for a card game popular among the nobility and, unlike tarot today, were not used for divination.

The most famous early tarot deck is the **Visconti-Sforza tarot**, made in the mid-15th century for the Visconti and Sforza families of Milan. These hand-painted cards are among the oldest surviving tarot cards.

### 18th-Century France: Becoming a Divination Tool

Tarot began to be used for divination in **18th-century Europe**, especially in France. In 1781, Antoine Court de Gébelin claimed that the tarot was a book containing the secret wisdom of ancient Egypt. That claim has since been shown to have no historical basis, but fortune-tellers such as Etteilla went on to publish divination decks and guides, and tarot reading spread widely among occultists.

In 1909, **Arthur Edward Waite** and **Pamela Colman Smith**, both members of the British occult society the **Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn**, released the widely used **Rider-Waite-Smith Tarot** (often called the Rider-Waite Tarot) through the publisher Rider. The deck gave every card, including the numbered pip cards, an illustrated scene, which made the cards easier to read from their pictures. Especially in the English-speaking world, it became something like the reference deck for modern tarot.

## 2. How Tarot Is Structured: The 78 Cards

A tarot deck has **78 cards** in total.

- **Major Arcana**: 22 cards – the big themes of life and the spiritual journey
- **Minor Arcana**: 56 cards – the details of everyday events and experiences

"Arcana" is Latin for **secrets or mysteries**.

## 3. The 22 Major Arcana: The Soul's Journey

The Major Arcana are 22 cards that begin with card 0, **The Fool**, and end with card 21, **The World**. Modern tarot writers often read this sequence as a soul's journey from innocence to wisdom, known as **The Fool's Journey**.

### Cards 0–7: The Journey Through the Material World

**0. The Fool**
New beginnings, pure potential, the courage to leap. Like the Fool stepping off the edge of a cliff, it marks the start of an adventure that looks reckless but feels free. Reversed: recklessness, lack of preparation.

**1. The Magician**
Willpower, creativity, using your resources. With the four suits (Wands, Cups, Swords, Pentacles) laid out on the table, it is the active force that has every tool it needs. Reversed: manipulation, wasted talent.

**2. The High Priestess**
Intuition, inner wisdom, hidden knowledge. It represents the ability to see the truth beyond appearances. Reversed: blocked information, ignoring your inner voice.

**3. The Empress**
Abundance, nurturing, nature, creation. It expresses rich vitality and sensual pleasure. Reversed: dependence, creative block.

**4. The Emperor**
Authority, structure, stability, the father figure. It stands for protection and control through order and systems. Reversed: tyranny, rigidity.

**5. The Hierophant**
Tradition, religion, shared beliefs, the teacher. It represents following established systems and teachings. Reversed: challenging convention, new approaches.

**6. The Lovers**
Love, choice, aligned values. Beyond romance, it points to important life choices and the values behind them. Reversed: disharmony, a poor choice.

**7. The Chariot**
Victory, determination, control, forward motion. It symbolizes the will to master opposing forces and drive toward a goal. Reversed: loss of direction, overcontrol.

### Cards 8–14: The Journey Through the Inner World

**8. Strength**
Inner courage, patience, gentle power. Like the woman calming a lion with her hands, it is strength that wins through love rather than force. Reversed: self-doubt, burnout.

**9. The Hermit**
Solitude, introspection, guidance, wisdom. It symbolizes the inward journey and the discovery of your true self. Reversed: isolation, narrow-mindedness.

**10. Wheel of Fortune**
Change, cycles, luck, turning points. It represents the ever-turning cycles of life and their unpredictability. Reversed: resistance, bad luck.

**11. Justice**
Fairness, cause and effect, balance. It symbolizes the principle that actions have consequences. Reversed: unfairness, avoiding responsibility.

**12. The Hanged Man**
Pause, a new perspective, sacrifice, acceptance. It suggests deep insight gained by willingly letting go and waiting. Reversed: delay, resistance.

**13. Death**
Transformation, endings and beginnings, release. This card does not mean literal death; it means the passing of the old and the start of something new. Reversed: resisting change, clinging.

**14. Temperance**
Balance, harmony, patience, moderation. Like the angel pouring water between two cups, it is the wisdom of avoiding extremes and finding the middle way. Reversed: imbalance, excess.

### Cards 15–21: The Journey Toward Spiritual Awakening

**15. The Devil**
Bondage, attachment, materialism, the shadow self. It helps you notice the chains you have placed on yourself. Reversed: freedom, release.

**16. The Tower**
Sudden change, collapse, revelation, chaos. It symbolizes the shock of a structure built on false foundations coming down. Reversed: averted disaster, a delayed crisis.

**17. The Star**
Hope, healing, inspiration, renewal. Like a calm sky after a storm, it brings the energy of hope and healing. Reversed: despair, loss of faith.

**18. The Moon**
Illusion, fear, the unconscious, uncertainty. Just as everything looks distorted in moonlight, it stands for a confusing time when it is hard to see what is real. Reversed: confusion clearing, truth coming out.

**19. The Sun**
Joy, success, vitality, clarity. One of the most positive cards in the deck, it symbolizes happiness and prosperity. Reversed: temporary disappointment, overconfidence.

**20. Judgement**
Awakening, absolution, an inner calling, rebirth. It is a spiritual awakening that lets go of the past and moves to a higher level. Reversed: self-criticism, clinging to the past.

**21. The World**
Completion, integration, achievement, wholeness. It is the moment the Fool's Journey is fulfilled and one full cycle comes to a close. Reversed: incompleteness, delay.

## 4. The Four Suits of the Minor Arcana: Stories of Everyday Life

The Minor Arcana consist of four suits, each with 14 cards (Ace through 10, plus Page, Knight, Queen and King). The element, season and field associations below are symbolic correspondences organized in the modern occult tradition, and the seasons in particular differ from system to system.

### Wands – Fire – Passion and Will

Wands are linked with the element of **Fire**. They represent intuition, creativity, passion, ambition, inspiration and drive.
- **Associated fields**: entrepreneurship, creative work, new projects
- **Season**: summer

The Ace of Wands is the spark of new inspiration, the Ten of Wands is a heavy load of responsibility, and the King of Wands is a charismatic leader.

### Cups – Water – Emotions and Relationships

Cups are linked with the element of **Water**. They represent emotion, love, intuition, dreams, creativity and relationships.
- **Associated fields**: the arts, counseling, healing, caregiving
- **Season**: autumn

The Ace of Cups is the start of a new love, the Ten of Cups is complete happiness and family, and the Queen of Cups is a person of high emotional intelligence.

### Swords – Air – Thought and Conflict

Swords are linked with the element of **Air**. They represent intellect, communication, conflict, truth, change and hardship. The sharpest suit in the Minor Arcana, Swords reveal truths that are honest but sometimes painful.
- **Associated fields**: law, medicine, science, writing
- **Season**: spring

The Ace of Swords is clear truth, the Five of Swords is conflict and defeat, and the King of Swords is a cool-headed intellectual.

### Pentacles – Earth – Material Life and Reality

Pentacles are linked with the element of **Earth**. They represent material security, money, work, health and practical achievement.
- **Associated fields**: finance, real estate, agriculture, crafts
- **Season**: winter

The Ace of Pentacles is a new financial opportunity, the Ten of Pentacles is inheritance and abundance, and the King of Pentacles is a successful businessperson.

## 5. Upright vs. Reversed Readings

When a card comes up upside down, it is called **reversed**. Readers interpret reversals in different ways, but there are three common approaches.

**Traditional reading**: the reversed card carries the opposite of its upright meaning (negative energy)
**Internalized reading**: the card's energy is working inwardly rather than outwardly
**Delayed reading**: the card's energy is delayed or blocked

If you are a beginner, it is perfectly fine to skip reversals at first and practice with upright meanings only.

## 6. Three Ways to Lay Out a Spread

A **spread** is the pattern in which cards are laid out, and different spreads suit different kinds of questions.

### One-Card Spread

The simplest method: you draw a single card to reflect on the day's energy or a simple question.

**How to use it**: calm your mind, focus on your question, shuffle the deck and draw one card.
**Good questions**: "What energy do I need today?" or "What should I focus on in this situation?"

### Three-Card Spread

This is the method most often recommended for tarot beginners. Three cards give you a more rounded view of a situation.

| Position | Meaning |
|------|------|
| Left | Past / cause |
| Center | Present / situation |
| Right | Future / outcome |

You can also use layouts such as **mind / body / spirit**, **situation / action / outcome**, or **me / the other person / us**.

### Celtic Cross Spread (10 Cards)

The most traditional and comprehensive spread. Ten cards are used to look at the overall flow of a situation.

1. **Present situation** – what is happening now
2. **Crossing card** – a challenge or obstacle
3. **Foundation** – the root cause of the situation
4. **Past** – influences that have recently passed
5. **Possibility** – a potential outcome
6. **Near future** – influences coming soon
7. **Self** – how the querent sees themselves
8. **Environment** – the influence of people around you
9. **Hopes and fears** – inner expectations and worries
10. **Final outcome** – the overall direction

## 7. A Learning Path for Tarot Beginners

Here is a step-by-step way to learn tarot if you are just starting out.

**Step 1: Choose a deck (1–2 weeks)**
For beginners, the **Rider-Waite Tarot** is a good choice. Every card has a detailed picture, which makes intuitive reading easier, and it has the richest supply of reference material.

**Step 2: Learn the Major Arcana (1–2 months)**
Start with the 22 Major Arcana. Take one or two cards a day, look closely at the picture, notice how it makes you feel, and connect it to its keywords.

**Step 3: Daily one-card readings (ongoing)**
Draw one card each morning as your "card of the day." At the end of the day, compare the card with what you actually experienced.

**Step 4: Study the Minor Arcana suit by suit (2–3 months)**
Work through Wands, Cups, Swords and Pentacles in that order, studying each suit's elemental energy together with the meaning of the numbers.

**Step 5: Practice three-card spreads**
Practice three-card readings for yourself, or for friends who have agreed to it. Keeping a tarot journal will help you improve faster.

## 8. Common Card Combinations

In a tarot reading, **combinations of cards** are thought to carry a more important message than single cards. The examples below are simply common symbolic readings; they do not predict real outcomes.

**The Lovers (6) + Two of Cups**: deep, mutual connection in love; a symbol of a closely bonded relationship
**The Tower (16) + Death (13)**: sudden and complete transformation, change with no going back
**The Empress (3) + Ace of Wands**: the birth of a creative project, the seed of a new venture
**The Moon (18) + Nine of Swords (the nightmare card)**: an image of intense anxiety and fear, a psychological crisis (if anxiety or sleeplessness lasts a long time in real life, please talk to a professional rather than relying on the cards)
**The Sun (19) + The World (21)**: an image of achievement, completion and a bright ending

## Closing Thoughts

Tarot cards are not a tool that hands down fixed predictions about the future. The 78 cards are a **psychological mirror** that captures life's many situations and emotions in symbols. A wise way to use tarot is to let the cards help you look into your unconscious, wake up your intuition, and make your own choices.

If you spend a little time with one card each day, you may find that the symbols of all 78 cards have quietly become a language of your own.
