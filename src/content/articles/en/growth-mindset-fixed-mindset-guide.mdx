"I'm just not a math person." "I've never had leadership skills." — Thoughts like these can shrink the challenges you take on. Stanford psychologist Carol Dweck proposed that beliefs about ability change effort and challenge. Large meta-analyses found only small links between mindset and academic achievement, and small effects of mindset interventions (Sisk et al., 2018). Those studies do not show that mindset decides happiness.

## Two Mindsets

### Fixed Mindset

**Core belief**: "Ability and talent are innate and unchangeable."

**Characteristics**:
- Views failure as proof of a lack of ability
- Avoids challenges and sticks to what's easy
- Takes criticism as a personal attack
- Feels threatened by other people's success
- Interprets effort as "I only have to try hard because I lack talent"

**Language patterns**:
- "That's just how I am."
- "This isn't suited to me."
- "I could never do that."
- "What if I fail?"

### Growth Mindset

**Core belief**: "Ability can be developed through dedication and effort."

**Characteristics**:
- Views challenges as opportunities to grow
- Looks for the lesson in failure
- Uses criticism as useful information
- Draws inspiration from other people's success
- Believes effort builds ability

**Language patterns**:
- "I'm not good at this yet, but I'm learning."
- "The harder this is, the more I'll grow."
- "Failure is data."
- "What can I learn from this person's success?"

<Callout type="info">
**The two mindsets aren't a strict binary**: people tend to have a growth mindset in some areas and a fixed mindset in others — and the same person can shift depending on the situation.
</Callout>

## What Neuroscience Tells Us About Mindset

### Neuroplasticity

Accounts of neuroplasticity say the brain can keep changing. Learning and practice are described as changing neural connections.

**Myelin**: One account says repeated practice thickens the myelin around nerve fibers. This page does not treat that as the whole of talent.

**The study**: Moser et al. (2011) reported that people with higher growth-mindset scores showed a larger neural response after errors and adjusted their next response more. The paper does not say that a fixed mindset makes brain activity fall.

## Practical Ways to Shift Your Mindset

### The Magic of "Yet"

Add the word "yet" to fixed-mindset thoughts.

| Fixed mindset | Growth mindset |
|-----------|------------|
| "I'm bad at presentations." | "I'm still not great at presentations." |
| "I have no talent for math." | "I'm not good at math yet." |
| "I'm not a leader." | "I'm still building my leadership skills." |

### Praising Process vs. Praising Outcomes

One of Dweck's experiments looked at what happens when children are praised differently.

**Outcome praise** ("You're so smart"):
- Later avoids hard problems
- After failure, concludes "I guess I'm not smart"

**Process praise** ("You worked really hard on that"):
- Takes on harder problems
- After failure, concludes "I need to try harder"

**How to apply this**:
- To yourself: instead of "good job," try "that took real effort — what did I learn?"
- To children or teammates: praise process, effort, and strategy over the outcome

### Reframing Failure

| Fixed-mindset interpretation | Growth-mindset interpretation |
|---------------|----------------|
| "I'm a failure." | "What did I learn from this attempt?" |
| "It's because I'm bad at this." | "I can try it differently next time." |
| "Trying hasn't worked." | "I haven't found the right approach yet." |

### Seeking Out Challenges

Once a week, deliberately take on something you "can't do yet."

**Examples**:
- Start a conversation you've always avoided
- Try cooking a dish you've never made
- Read a book on a topic you know little about

## Mindset at Work

### Companies With a Growth Mindset Culture

Dweck reported more innovation and more information sharing at companies she described as having a growth-mindset culture. That report is not the same size of effect as Sisk et al. (2018).

**Building a growth mindset team culture**:
1. Openly acknowledge mistakes and share what was learned from them
2. Hold "learning reviews" after finishing a project to discuss what was learned
3. Do not stock the list with only sure things. This page does not use a 90% success figure
4. Build a culture of "yet" — "If someone on this team doesn't know something, of course they can learn it"

## Instilling a Growth Mindset in Children

| What to avoid saying | What to say instead |
|-----------|-----------|
| "You're a natural genius." | "You really studied hard for that." |
| "That was easy, right? You're just good at this." | "If that felt easy, try something challenging enough to stretch you." |
| "You failed." | "What did you learn this time?" |
| "This is just your thing." | "You've put a huge amount of time into this." |

<Callout type="tip">
**Right now**: Think of one difficult thing you've been avoiding today. That's exactly the opportunity to practice a growth mindset.
</Callout>
