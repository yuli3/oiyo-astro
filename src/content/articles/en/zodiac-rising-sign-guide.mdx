## 1. A calculated point is not a personality finding

The Ascendant is the point where the ecliptic meets the eastern horizon at a particular time and place. It is not a planet or a newly appearing star. Its rising sign identifies the zodiac segment containing that point in the chosen zodiac system.

Astrology sometimes interprets it as a symbol of first impressions or approaching the world. Calculating a coordinate does not establish an ability to predict appearance, personality or other people's judgments. A Sun-sign description that feels wrong does not establish that the Ascendant explains the mismatch.

| Distinction | What is examined | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Sun and Moon positions | Celestial positions at a given time | Objective scores for identity or emotions |
| Ascendant | The local horizon's relation to the ecliptic | A scientific diagnosis of appearance or first impressions |
| Symbolic interpretation | An account within a chosen astrological tradition | A universal rule for people's behaviour |

## 2. Birth time alone is insufficient

You need the date, the local clock time, the birthplace's latitude and longitude, and appropriate time-zone handling. Converting local time to Universal Time requires the UTC offset and any daylight-saving rule for that place and date. Do not enter every overseas birth as Korean time.

The [Swiss Ephemeris programming manual](https://www.astro.com/swisseph-download/doc/swephprg.pdf) documents house functions taking a Universal-Time date and geographic coordinates, with the Ascendant returned separately. This is computational documentation, not evidence for personality interpretations. This article also does not report a numerical validation of OIYO's engine against it.

1. Check the recorded date, local time and birthplace.
2. Establish whether the tool expects local time or already-converted Universal Time.
3. Record settings such as tropical versus sidereal zodiac alongside the result.
4. Carry approximate birth-time information through to the result's uncertainty.

## 3. Why not use a universal two-hour schedule?

Dividing a day into twelve gives a rough explanatory average, not twelve equal rising durations. The geometry between the ecliptic and local horizon depends on place and time. A table assigning a sign to “6 a.m.” without a date and location is not a personal calculation.

Different zodiac settings can produce different sign labels. The Ascendant is also not identical to the entire first house; check the house-system setting. Extra decimal places in a result cannot make an uncertain birth time precise.

<details>
<summary>What if the record says “around 9 a.m.”?</summary>

Suppose a fictional record covers 08:45–09:15. You can compare several times within that range using the same location and settings. We have not performed those calculations here, so no particular Ascendant is asserted. If the sign changes within the range, retain the alternatives and their conditions rather than choosing one. An unchanged sign still does not validate a personality interpretation. Do not reverse-engineer a definite birth time from someone's appearance or a description that feels convincing.

## 4. Read the twelve labels as optional questions

These are OIYO editorial prompts adapted from familiar popular-astrology keywords. They are not validated personality scales or a table of appearance, ability or compatibility. You can use any question regardless of your sign, and skip it if it is unhelpful.

| Symbolic label | A question to consider |
|---|---|
| Aries — starting | When do you take the initiative, and when do you wait? |
| Taurus — continuity | When does familiarity help, and when is change needed? |
| Gemini — exchange | How do situations where you talk differ from those where you listen? |
| Cancer — care | Can you care for others while keeping your own boundaries? |
| Leo — expression | When do you want attention, and when do you prefer to be alone? |
| Virgo — checking | When does checking details help, and when does it become a burden? |
| Libra — coordination | How do you distinguish compromise from clearly stating your view? |
| Scorpio — depth | What do you actually observe when building trust? |
| Sagittarius — exploration | How do you choose new experiences and commitments to keep? |
| Capricorn — structure | When do you follow a plan, and when do you revise it? |
| Aquarius — another perspective | Do you listen to others' circumstances when proposing a different idea? |
| Pisces — imagination | How do you distinguish imagination from observed facts? |

## 5. Put experience before a label

Consider a recent situation rather than concluding “this is how I naturally appear.” Did you behave differently among strangers and close friends? Roles, culture and fatigue can matter too. Do not infer someone's birth time or character from appearance without their consent, or use the Ascendant for consequential hiring, health or relationship decisions.

[NASA's Star Finder guide](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/starfinder/en/) helps explain observing conditions and the sky's apparent motion. It is archived educational material, not support for astrological personality predictions. Observed star patterns and astrological zodiac segments are not interchangeable.
