## What Is Astrology?

**Astrology** is a traditional interpretive system that assigns meanings about personality and events to the positions of the Sun, Moon, and planets. Calculating a celestial position and demonstrating that it predicts personality are different tasks.

This course explains the vocabulary used in astrological texts and birth charts. Finding a description personally meaningful does not establish its accuracy as a personality measure or prediction.

<Callout type="note">
**Scope**: Descriptions of personality and life areas below are symbolic conventions, not validated psychological measurements. Precise astronomical calculations do not validate the interpretations attached to them. Do not use a chart alone to make health, financial, or career decisions.
</Callout>

## The History of Astrology

Ancient Babylonian horoscope tablets recorded birth dates and celestial positions. Historian Alexander Jones compares these with Greek horoscopes, which also recorded the zodiacal position rising on the eastern horizon at birth. This provides historical context for the Ascendant used in later chart reading.

These records document a cultural tradition developed alongside astronomical observation and calculation. Their age does not establish the accuracy of modern personality or predictive claims. See Jones, [The Horoscope Casters (2000), PDF pages 1–3](https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/59909/2/Jones%202000%20The%20horoscope%20casters%20Archaeology%20Odyssey.pdf).

## What Is a Natal Chart?

A natal chart is a diagram of celestial positions and houses calculated for a birth time and place. Connecting those positions to personality or life patterns is a separate interpretive step, not a result established by the calculation itself.

### Information Needed for a Natal Chart

1. **Date of birth**: Determines the Sun sign and planetary positions
2. **Time of birth**: Needed with location for the Ascendant and houses; identify the time zone and any daylight-saving adjustment
3. **Place of birth**: Used to calculate the houses for your location

<Callout type="tip">
**Birth-time uncertainty**: The Ascendant is the intersection of the eastern horizon and the ecliptic. A sign does not always take two hours to rise; the interval varies with latitude and time. Without a reliable birth time, do not assert a specific Ascendant or house placement. A solar chart is a separate interpretive convention, not a method for recovering an unknown birth time. See the [Swiss Ephemeris documentation, sections 6 and 6.2](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swisseph.htm) for calculation conventions, not evidence of personality prediction.
</Callout>

## The Sun Sign and Beyond

An astrological sign is one of twelve 30-degree divisions of the ecliptic, not the same thing as the sky region occupied by a constellation with that name. The tropical zodiac uses the March equinox as zero degrees Aries. Sidereal systems use a stellar reference, with results depending on the reference chosen. Compare the time zone, birthplace, zodiac reference, and house system before interpreting differences between calculators. [Swiss Ephemeris section 2.8.1](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swisseph.htm) describes these zodiac conventions.

The sign most people know is the **Sun Sign**. But a full chart contains much more.

| Sign Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sun Sign** (e.g., ♉ Taurus) | The conscious self; the direction of life |
| **Moon Sign** | Emotions, the unconscious, instinct |
| **Ascendant (Rising Sign)** | External impression; how you appear at first |

The table summarizes meanings assigned by astrological tradition. It does not establish that Moon signs or Ascendants explain measured personality differences between people with the same Sun sign.

## Astrology vs. Four Pillars of Destiny (Saju)

| Aspect | Western Astrology | Four Pillars (Saju) |
|---|---|---|
| Time reference | Birth-time celestial coordinates and local horizon | Year, month, day, and hour expressed through stems and branches |
| Boundaries to check | Time zone, location, zodiac reference, and house system | Commonly the beginning of spring for the year and solar-term boundaries for the month; day-boundary conventions also matter |
| Core elements | Planets, signs, houses | Heavenly Stems, Earthly Branches, Five Elements |
| Method | Symbolic interpretation | Five Element relationships and yongshin (용신), a traditional interpretive category |
| Shared feature | Both assign symbolic meanings to birth information | Neither thereby measures a shared physical energy |

Calling Saju simply lunar or Moon-based overlooks its solar-term boundaries. Entering a lunar birth date still requires date conversion and checking the relevant boundaries. The [Academy of Korean Studies entry on Saju](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0025957) describes the four pillars and their boundary conventions. It documents a tradition, not the accuracy of personality predictions.

Historical sources document which conventions existed. Calculation documentation explains how positions are obtained. The accuracy of personality prediction requires a separate empirical test; neither a long history nor a precise coordinate establishes it. Read a study's design and results rather than treating its existence as certification.
