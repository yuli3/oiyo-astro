## 1. Decide what reading should help you do

Finishing books and understanding them are different goals. For exam revision, explain a concept and its conditions; for work, find evidence that informs a decision. Enjoying a novel can be a goal in itself. A celebrity's book count need not be your benchmark.

| Purpose | Approach | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find information | Browse contents, index and relevant sections | An answer and its supporting page |
| Learn a concept | Connect definitions, assumptions and examples | Explain with the book closed, then verify |
| Enjoy a work | Read at your own pace | Impressions, questions and scenes to revisit |

## 2. Make starting easy

Try a small number of pages or minutes. This is a personal experiment, not a scientifically established threshold for forming a habit. Connect reading to an existing routine and keep the book accessible; adapt when your schedule changes.

Missing a day is not failure. Mark where to resume. Compare reading one book at a time with alternating two, rather than assuming one method works for everyone.

## 3. Match the book and the depth to your purpose

Sample the contents and a passage to check relevance, prerequisites and difficulty. Age or sales do not guarantee accuracy. For changing rules or technologies, check the edition and dates of evidence.

Skimming can locate relevant passages; close reading can examine arguments and conditions. Turning pages quickly is not proof of deep understanding. In nonfiction, separate claims, evidence and counterexamples. For fiction, a brief note on characters or events can help you resume. There is no requirement to read novels for 30 uninterrupted minutes.

## 4. Recall, then check

After a section, close the book and write its claim and an example in your own words. Reopen it to correct omissions and misunderstandings. Record page numbers or locations for quotations. You can also try briefly recalling the previous section at your next session.

Karpicke and Blunt reported learning benefits from retrieval practice in experiments using science learning materials. This is not a universal effect for every reader or book, nor proof that teaching someone multiplies memory by a fixed amount. [Original study](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf)

Paper or an app can hold your notes. Separate book details, claims, evidence locations, questions and possible applications. Being able to explain and verify matters more than the number of highlights.

## 5. Check the assumptions behind an annual plan

This is editorial arithmetic, not an experimental finding.

| Assumption | Calculation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 minutes on 350 days | 30 × 350 ÷ 60 = 175 hours | Assumes that time is available |
| 4 hours per book | 175 ÷ 4 = 43.75 books | At most 43 complete books and part of another |
| 8 hours per book | 175 ÷ 8 = 21.875 books | Difficulty and notes change the estimate |

<details>
<summary>How much time would 50 books take?</summary>

At 4 hours each, 50 × 4 = 200 hours. Across 350 days, that is about 34.3 minutes a day, still assuming every book takes 4 hours. Understanding one demanding book may be a better goal than a book count.

</details>

Log your actual reading time for a week, then adjust. You do not have to add every missed page to the next day's workload.

## 6. Choose a format for the situation

| Format | Useful features | What to consider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E-book | Portability, search, digital notes | Device, notifications, layout and access terms |
| Print | Page location and spreads | Weight, storage, lighting and cost |
| Audiobook | Listening when your hands are occupied | Attention, navigation and safety |

No format guarantees comfort or comprehension. Reading-media research is not a ranking of every device, genre and reader. Check actual prices and subscription access. During driving or other safety-critical activities, maintaining attention comes before increasing your book count.

## 7. Use discussion to check understanding

A group does not guarantee years of reading. Start with a manageable passage and discuss why interpretations differ, with page references. Return to the text when an explanation breaks down. Questions worth revisiting can matter more than judging who finished.

## Sources and limits

- Karpicke & Blunt (2011), linked above: evidence for retrieval practice under specific learning tasks and conditions.
