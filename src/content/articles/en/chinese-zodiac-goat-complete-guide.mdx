## The eighth animal

The goat (羊) is the **eighth animal** of the twelve, and its earthly branch is Wei (未). In East Asian tradition the animal is often drawn as gentle, as part of a flock, and as an auspicious offering. That is symbolism. It is not a measured personality of people born in a goat year.

Depending on the country and the source, the animal is rendered as goat, sheep, or ram. The branch Wei stays the same.

## What the tradition assigns

The rows below are traditional assignments. They are not a personality score.

| Item | Traditional assignment |
|---|---|
| Earthly branch | Wei (未) |
| Element and yin-yang | Yin earth |
| Direction | Southwest in the later-heaven layout |
| Time | The Wei double-hour, 13:00–15:00 |

Red, purple, and green, the numbers 2 and 7, and the carnation and the primrose are names that show up often on modern popular lists. They are not values fixed by the old texts.

## Year pillars

An animal year does not start on January 1. Folk custom usually counts from **Lunar New Year**, while saju (Four Pillars) uses **Lichun, the start of spring (around February 4)** as the boundary of the year pillar. So someone born in January or early February may belong to the previous animal.

The heavenly stem is the year's element in the sexagenary cycle. The branch stays Wei. Only the stem changes from year to year. This page does not read a personality from that name. The same stem and branch are the notation saju uses for the year pillar. That notation is not evidence of character.

## The portrait the tradition tells

The tradition often draws a goat year as someone gentle who looks for harmony rather than a fight, who is drawn to beautiful things, and who notices how others feel first. A story of staying a long time beside someone once trusted is attached too. A complaint follows: decisions are easily put off. A warning follows: worry runs high and self-regard runs low. All of that is a traditional portrait. It is not a law for people born in those years.

## Love and relationships

The tradition speaks of goat-year love as tender and devoted. The story is one of valuing a deep emotional bond, and of stepping back rather than standing firm when conflict comes. There is also a warning about leaning on a partner too much. That is neither a diagnosis nor a destiny. As a wished-for partner, the tradition names someone who supports warmly and also helps make decisions. That, too, is the tradition's story.

## Names of traditional relations

This page does not use star ratings or the language of destiny. It keeps the names.

| Name | Pair | Branches |

| Six-harmony (六合) | Horse and goat | 午未 |
| Clash (冲) | Ox and goat | 丑未 |
| Harm (害) | Rat and goat | 子未 |

Triad and six-harmony are not a higher score, and clash and harm are not a lower one. They are the names this scheme has used.

## Work

The tradition often names work tied to an eye for beauty or to care: design, a hand craft, writing, teaching or caring for others, gardening. It does not claim a unique standing in those fields or an innate gift. It is also not proof that those jobs fit best.

## Names kept only as birth years

Only the birth year is written. It is not used as evidence of personality. Mark Twain was born in November 1835 (乙未), and Julia Roberts in October 1967 (丁未). Both were born after Lunar New Year and after Lichun.

## 2026

2026 is Bing-Wu (丙午). Wu (午) and Wei (未) are a six-harmony pair in this scheme. This page does not forecast the year. For your physical health, please rely on a doctor, not an animal year.
