## 1. Introduction: A 'Map of the Mind', Not Destiny

This article explains the Ten Gods as a traditional classification of relationships, not a personality assessment. Calculating a category and establishing facts about a person are different tasks. We do not assume that birth timing reveals a hidden mind or determines a career.

## 2. Five relationships, divided by yin–yang polarity

The day master is the reference. Other heavenly stems are classified by whether their element matches, generates or controls it, or is generated or controlled by it. Matching or opposite polarity divides each relationship into two. Generation and control are terms within this system, not measurements of support or domination between real people.

| Relationship | Same polarity | Opposite polarity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same element | Friend (比肩) | Rob Wealth (劫財) |
| Generated by day master | Eating God (食神) | Hurting Officer (傷官) |
| Controlled by day master | Indirect Wealth (偏財) | Direct Wealth (正財) |
| Controls day master | Indirect Officer (偏官) | Direct Officer (正官) |
| Generates day master | Indirect Resource (偏印) | Direct Resource (正印) |

For yang-wood Jia (甲), another Jia is Friend and yin-wood Yi (乙) is Rob Wealth. Of the fire stems generated by wood, yang-fire Bing (丙) is Eating God and yin-fire Ding (丁) is Hurting Officer. These examples classify characters; they do not predict the behavior of people whose charts contain them.

## 3. Symbols are not observed behavior

Traditional readings connect Peers with companionship and competition, Output with expression and production, Wealth with resources and management, Officer with rules and responsibility, and Resource with learning and support. These are symbolic themes, not personality scores. Rob Wealth does not identify a thief, Hurting Officer does not establish genius or rebellion, and Wealth does not predict income or business success.

Traditional mappings to spouses and parents reflect historical family arrangements and gender roles. They should not be transferred into judgments about modern families or the quality of an actual relationship.

## 4. Check reflection against experience

- Peers: When did cooperation or competition feel comfortable? Record a recent example.
- Output: Do you prefer speaking, writing or making things?
- Wealth: How do you manage time and money? Compare the story with records.
- Officer: Which responsibilities do you accept, and where do you need boundaries?
- Resource: Which people, materials and conditions have helped you learn?

These are reflection examples from this article, not standardized test items. Look for counterexamples rather than forcing yourself to match a category.

## 5. Use other evidence for career decisions

We do not conclude that many Hurting Officers mean you should become an innovator, or that Rob Wealth means you should become a gambler. Career choices need actual interests, experience, skills, working conditions and opportunities. Categories do not establish morality, family relationships or ability.

## 6. Learning and Support: Indirect and Direct Resource

The element that generates the day master is classified as Resource (印星). Matching yin–yang polarity gives Indirect Resource (偏印); opposite polarity gives Direct Resource (正印). For a yang-wood Jia (甲) day master, yang-water Ren (壬) is Indirect Resource and yin-water Gui (癸) is Direct Resource. This is a rule within the traditional system, not a personality measurement.

- **Indirect Resource:** Traditionally associated with unconventional learning, intuition and independent inquiry. Use it as a prompt: “What helps me understand something?” Its presence does not establish genius or social isolation.
- **Direct Resource:** Traditionally associated with teaching, protection and learning resources. A useful question is: “Which people and resources can support me?” Its presence or absence does not predict grades or parental care.

The five relationships—Peers, Output, Wealth, Officer and Resource—each divide into two, completing all ten categories. Experience and environment also matter for actual behaviour; these categories are not a diagnosis or a basis for career decisions.

## 7. Do not turn a classification into destiny

A category does not prove someone’s worth or a weapon they were born with. Symbols might start a conversation, but lived experience and the person’s own account take priority. You do not have to use this framework for self-understanding.
