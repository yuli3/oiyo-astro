## 1. A Life Metaphor, Not an Energy Measurement

The twelve stages, or Sib-i-un-seong, are names used in the Saju tradition. Images of conception, growth, decline, and endings describe traditional qi, not measured physical energy or mental health. Nor do the Ten Gods establish actual desires or the stages measure a present capacity to act.

The [Academy of Korean Studies entry on Saju](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0025957) includes the stages among its interpretive elements. This describes a cultural practice, not experimental predictive validity.

The order below begins with conception to explain the life metaphor. It does not mean everyone passes through twelve real periods in this order or that a name fixes age, condition, or future. Applying a rule to birth data and demonstrating that its output predicts reality are separate tasks.

## 2. The Twelve Names and Their Boundaries

These explanations organize the existing article's symbolism. Modern personality descriptions are not presented as direct quotations from classics, and none establishes a trait of a person with that label.

### 1. Tae (胎): Conception

Conception evokes a possibility before it becomes visible. A soul entering a womb is not a biological finding. The label does not require postponing action.

### 2. Yang (養): Nurturing

Nurturing evokes protection and preparation, not a promise that the universe provides income or support. Check who can actually offer the help you need and how to request it.

### 3. Jangsaeng (長生): Birth

Birth evokes a new beginning. It guarantees neither innocence, helpful elders, nor good fortune with people, and does not establish the right time to launch something.

### 4. Mok-yuk (沐浴): Bathing

Bathing connects exposure, change, and unfamiliar experience as metaphors. It does not diagnose puberty or emotional instability. Boldness is not an obligation.

### 5. Gwan-dae (冠帶): Formal Dress

Formal dress evokes taking a social role. It establishes neither confidence nor arrogance and guarantees no career achievement.

### 6. Geon-nok (建祿): Position

Position connects role, independence, and foundations symbolically. It does not identify your most productive period or a time to build wealth and reputation. Work and financial decisions need actual conditions.

### 7. Jewang (帝旺): Peak

The peak evokes vigor and authority, not evidence of power, success, or loneliness. It does not establish that a lack of humility causes a fall.

### 8. Soe (衰): Decline

Decline evokes withdrawal and change, not incompetence or an obligation to become an adviser. Age or disability does not reduce a person's worth.

### 9. Byeong (病): Illness

Illness is an image of weakening, not a finding about disease risk, stamina, or empathy. Physical weakness does not establish heightened spiritual sensitivity. Symptoms and treatment require appropriate medical assessment.

### 10. Sa (死): Death

Death evokes stopping and ending; it predicts neither actual death nor a departing soul. It does not diagnose concentration or mastery, or require abandoning public roles to focus only on research.

### 11. Myo (墓): Grave

The grave evokes burial, storage, organization, and memory. It measures neither memory nor financial management. Do not use the label to decide saving, investing, or disposing of assets.

### 12. Jeol (絶): Termination

Termination evokes an interruption before another beginning. It means neither that you are at rock bottom nor that improvement is guaranteed. It does not establish sensitivity or exceptional creativity.

## 3. Reassurance Is Not Prediction Either

Illness, death, grave, and termination labels need not frighten you. But replacing fear with “Grave makes you stable” or “Termination makes you brilliant” has the same evidential problem. Names do not rank lives or people's worth.

Connecting a stage with Daewoon is not a validated calendar for action. Bathing does not command risk-taking, and Death does not command retreat. Do not attribute difficulties to fate or personal fault; examine actual health, economic, relationship, and environmental conditions.

## 4. Optional Reflection Needs Real Information

If an image interests you, you might ask what you want to begin or organize and whether time, money, and support are sufficient. These are optional editorial reflection prompts, not a psychological test or a validated intervention. Skip them if they feel irrelevant or uncomfortable.

You do not have to fit your life into these names. Important career, investment, treatment, and relationship decisions should prioritize preferences, constraints, actual records, and appropriate professional support.

## Sources and Scope

The encyclopedia text was read directly, not used to validate a calculation table or personality interpretation. The existing bibliography includes *Yuanhai Ziping*, *Sanming Tonghui*, *Ziping Zhenquan*, and *Ditian Sui Chanwei*. Their full texts were not compared here. The [Chinese Text Project record for Sanming Tonghui](https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?if=en&remap=gb&res=758991) was located, but text access was blocked. Exact passages, edition differences, and calculation rules remain unverified in this review.

The previously cited [Carlson astrology study](https://doi.org/10.1038/318419a0) concerns natal charts and personality descriptions; [Hartmann and colleagues' birth-date study](https://helmuthnyborg.dk/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/Publ_2006_Date-of-birth.pdf) concerns personality and general intelligence. The abstract scopes checked in an earlier review must not be expanded into direct tests of these stages or all Saju predictions. This review did not establish independent validation of the stages' predictive power, and this article makes no such claim.
