## 1. Origins: A Psychobiological Model

Cloninger did not see personality as a purely psychological phenomenon; he tried to explain it together with its biological basis. In 1993 he proposed a seven-dimension model combining four temperament dimensions and three character dimensions. He **hypothesized** that each temperament dimension was linked to a particular neurotransmitter (dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine). Later research has not consistently confirmed these one-to-one links, so please read the neurotransmitter labels below as Cloninger's hypothesis, not as established fact.

## 2. The Four Temperaments: A Relatively Stable Foundation

Temperament is your automatic emotional response: how your 「instincts」 react when something happens.

### ① Novelty Seeking - Dopamine (hypothesis)

- **Core:** Chases the new and has a hard time tolerating monotony.
- **High:** Passionate and creative, but impulsive and easily bored.
- **Low:** Careful and frugal, but wary of change and prone to sticking with routine.

### ② Harm Avoidance - Serotonin (hypothesis)

- **Core:** Highly sensitive to danger, punishment, and uncertainty.
- **High:** Prepared and good at planning, but prone to worry and withdrawal.
- **Low:** Optimistic and bold, but may underestimate risk.

### ③ Reward Dependence - Norepinephrine (hypothesis)

- **Core:** Responds strongly to other people's feelings, approval, and relationships.
- **High:** Warm and empathetic, but may be easily swayed or hurt by rejection.
- **Low:** Independent and rational, but may come across as cold or distant.

### ④ Persistence

- **Core:** The ability to keep going without an immediate reward.
- **High:** Hardworking and achievement-oriented, but can be stubborn and inflexible.
- **Low:** Flexible and adaptable, but may give up easily.

## 3. The Three Characters: A Structure You Shape

Character is the self-regulating system you build from the raw material of temperament through learning, experience, and will.

### ① Self-Directedness

- 「Am I in charge of my own life?」
- High scorers are responsible and goal-oriented; low scorers tend to blame others or drift with circumstances.

### ② Cooperativeness

- 「Can I live well alongside others?」
- High scorers are tolerant and collaborative; low scorers may be self-centered or find cooperation difficult.

### ③ Self-Transcendence

- 「Am I part of something larger?」
- High scorers tend to be spiritual and imaginative; low scorers tend to be practical and down-to-earth.

## 4. Accept Your Temperament, Cultivate Your Character

The core message of the TCI is that **temperament is not a flaw**.
High Harm Avoidance doesn't make you a coward; it gives you the instincts of a risk manager. High Novelty Seeking doesn't make you scattered; it gives you the leanings of an explorer.
What matters is how you steer those tendencies with the two pillars of character, Self-Directedness and Cooperativeness. Even if you were born with a fearful temperament, a well-developed character can help you find courage when it counts.

## 5. In Closing: Building the House You Want

You may not love your 「factory settings.」 Why am I so sensitive? Why am I so impatient? But there's no need to sigh at the bricks and give up on building. The TCI seems to say: 「Your materials are already enough. Now build the house you want.」

### FAQ

**Q: Does temperament really never change?**
A: Temperament is influenced by heredity and tends to be fairly stable, but it isn't completely fixed. Later research also found that the character dimensions are partly heritable too, so the split 「temperament is inborn, character is built」 is best seen as a simplification that aids understanding. As character matures, the weaker sides of temperament tend to show less and its strengths can shine more.

**Q: Which temperament combination is best?**
A: There is no 「best」 combination. For example, someone high in Novelty Seeking and low in Harm Avoidance may thrive in fast-changing settings but feel stifled by repetitive work, and the reverse can be true for others. What matters is finding an environment that fits your temperament.
