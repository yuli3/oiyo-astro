## The eleventh animal

The dog (戌) is the **eleventh animal** of the Chinese zodiac. In the widely told race folktale, the dog also arrives eleventh, and the story often adds that it stopped to play in the water. That is a folktale. Because the dog stays close to people, the tradition often gives the dog year the symbol of loyalty. It is not a measured personality of people born in a dog year.

## What the tradition assigns

The rows below are traditional assignments. They are not a personality score.

| Item | Traditional assignment |
|---|---|
| Earthly branch | Xu (戌) |
| Element and yin-yang | Yang earth |
| Direction | Northwest |
| Time | The Xu double-hour, 19:00–21:00 |

Green, red, and purple, the numbers 3, 4, and 9, and the rose and the osmanthus are names that show up often on modern popular lists. They are not values fixed by the old texts.

## Year pillars

When the animal year changes depends on the convention. In saju, the year pillar changes at **Lichun** (the start of spring, around February 4). In everyday talk, many people count from **Lunar New Year** instead. The table below follows Lichun. For example, someone born in January 1958 still belongs to the 丁酉 (rooster) year.

The heavenly stem is the year's element in the sexagenary cycle. The branch stays Xu. Only the stem changes from year to year. This page does not read a personality from that name.

| Year | Year pillar | Element name of the stem |
|---|---|---|
| 1946 | 丙戌 | fire |
| 1958 | 戊戌 | earth |
| 1970 | 庚戌 | metal |
| 1982 | 壬戌 | water |
| 1994 | 甲戌 | wood |
| 2006 | 丙戌 | fire |
| 2018 | 戊戌 | earth |
| 2030 | 庚戌 | metal |

## The portrait the tradition tells

The tradition often draws a dog year as someone who is faithful to the people close to them, who dislikes lies, and who cannot stay quiet in front of injustice. It also writes someone who tries to keep a task and a promise to the end, and who stays beside a person going through a hard time. On the other side, it names worrying too much about loved ones, a pessimistic view of the world, a stubbornness that does not easily change a conviction, and a self-sacrifice that ends in exhaustion. All of that is a traditional portrait. It is not a law for people born in those years.

## Love and relationships

The tradition speaks of dog-year love as serious and devoted. The story is someone who puts the partner first once they give their heart, who prefers honest conversation, and who builds trust over time. There is also a warning that broken trust hurts the most. That is neither a diagnosis nor a destiny. As a wished-for partner, the tradition names someone who values honesty and shares similar values. That, too, is the tradition's story.

## Names of traditional relations

This page does not use star ratings or the language of destiny. It keeps the names.

| Name | Pair | Branches |
|---|---|---|
| Triad (三合) | Tiger, horse, dog | 寅午戌 |
| Six-harmony (六合) | Rabbit and dog | 卯戌 |
| Clash (冲) | Dragon and dog | 辰戌 |
| Harm (害) | Rooster and dog | 酉戌 |

Triad and six-harmony are not a higher score, and clash and harm are not a lower one. They are the names this scheme has used.

## Work

The tradition often links the dog year with work that protects and cares for people: law, care work, teaching, community work, and safety. That is an association drawn from the symbol. It is not proof that those jobs fit dog years best, and it is not a statistic that dog years are common in any job.

## Names kept only as birth years

Only the birth year is written. It is not used as evidence of personality. Mother Teresa was born in 1910, Bill Clinton in 1946, and Michael Jackson in 1958.

## 2026

2026 is Bing-Wu (丙午). Wu (午) and Xu (戌) are two of the three branches of the triad 寅午戌. All three are not present, so it cannot be called a year in which the triad is completed. This page does not forecast the year.
