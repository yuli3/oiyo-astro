## Second in a folktale

In a widely told race folktale, the ox is the **second animal**. The ox is about to finish ahead, and the rat, which has been riding on its back, jumps off at the finish. That is a folktale. The theme the story keeps returning to is patience. It is not a measured personality of people born in an ox year.

## What the tradition assigns

The rows below are traditional assignments. They are not a personality score.

| Item | Traditional assignment |
|---|---|
| Earthly branch | Chou (丑) |
| Element and yin-yang | Yin earth |
| Direction | Northeast in the later-heaven layout |
| Time | The Chou double-hour, 01:00–03:00 |

White, yellow, and green, the numbers 1 and 4, and the tulip and the peach blossom are names that show up often on modern popular lists. They are not values fixed by the old texts.

## Year pillars

The heavenly stem is the year's element in the sexagenary cycle. The branch stays Chou. Only the stem changes from year to year. This page does not read a personality from that name. The same stem and branch are the notation saju uses for the year pillar. That notation is not evidence of character.

## The portrait the tradition tells

The tradition often draws an ox year as someone who continues the work rather than the speech, who tries to keep a promise once taken, and who looks at a near goal in a practical way. It also writes a pillar that finishes what was taken on, and a stubbornness that receives change slowly. A complaint follows: the inside is hard to move into words. A warning follows: a line, once crossed, is remembered for a long time. All of that is a traditional portrait. It is not a law for people born in those years.

Patience stays as the folktale's theme. This page does not turn it into a crude power of holding on.

## Love and relationships

The tradition speaks of ox-year love as serious and long. The story is a slow building of trust, and care shown by the steadiness of ordinary days rather than by a large event. There is also a warning that broken trust is hard to come back from. That is neither a diagnosis nor a destiny. As a wished-for partner, the tradition names someone who can hear a plain way of speaking and who wants steadiness more than swing. That, too, is the tradition's story.

## Names of traditional relations

This page does not use star ratings or the language of destiny. It keeps the names.

| Name | Pair | Branches |
|---|---|---|
| Triad (三合) | Snake, rooster, ox | 巳酉丑 |
| Six-harmony (六合) | Rat and ox | 子丑 |
| Clash (冲) | Ox and goat | 丑未 |
| Harm (害) | Ox and horse | 丑午 |

Triad and six-harmony are not a higher score, and clash and harm are not a lower one. They are the names this scheme has used.

## Work

The tradition often names steady crafts: cooking, a hand craft, or a post one keeps. It does not claim an innate medical talent, money that follows from the earth element, or a unique result. It is also not proof that those jobs fit best.

## Names kept only as birth years

Only the birth year is written. It is not used as evidence of personality. Park Chan-ho was born in 1973, Barack Obama in 1961, Napoleon in 1769, and Charlie Chaplin in 1889.

## 2026

2026 is Bing-Wu (丙午). Chou (丑) and Wu (午) are not a clash. In this scheme they are a harm pair. This page does not forecast the year.

## Suggestions

1. Try delaying a stubborn line by a little. There is no guarantee that more possibilities open.
2. Try one sentence about what is inside. If it is not said, the other person has a hard time knowing it.
3. A suggestion, not an order: do not refuse change as a whole. Changing slowly is all right.
