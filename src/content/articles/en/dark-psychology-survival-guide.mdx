Pages called dark psychology are easy to find. There is no research field by that name. Under the label, a 2002 personality paper is usually mixed with unsourced advice about control. This article separates the two and helps you notice speech and choices that work like manipulation. It is not a diagnosis. It is not a license to manipulate anyone.

## What the paper actually studied

In 2002, Delroy Paulhus and Kevin Williams looked at three personality traits together in a student sample: subclinical narcissism, Machiavellianism, and subclinical psychopathy. They treated the three as overlapping questionnaire traits that did not collapse into a single score. They are not clinical diagnoses. The paper does not say that everyone carries these traits as weapons.

Questionnaires on the narcissism side of this research usually ask how grand a person feels and how much admiration they want. A score is not a diagnosis of narcissistic personality disorder.

Machiavellianism in this line of work follows the scale Christie and Geis published in 1970. That scale is about a cynical view of people, interpersonal tactics, and a disregard for conventional morality. This page does not explain how to use those tactics. The slogan that the ends justify the means is not the definition of the scale. Machiavelli did not write that sentence in *The Prince* as a research definition. *The Prince* does discuss political deception. That is not the same text as a personality scale.

Subclinical psychopathy in the 2002 paper is not a forensic diagnosis. The paper did not find that people are social chameleons. It is a questionnaire score.

## Patterns described in popular writing

The seven patterns below are not results from Paulhus and Williams. They are patterns that popular writing on the internet describes again and again. Each paragraph says what to notice if you are on the receiving end. None of them is a way to deploy the pattern.

**Hidden intent.** When kindness, favors, or offers of help keep arriving, and the stated reason keeps missing the result, set the spoken reason next to what actually happens. What the other person wants may sit outside the sentence. You do not need to conceal your own intent in return.

**Flattery that creates dependence.** Praise may show up when you stay close, agree, or make the other person look good, and cool when you do not. That warmth can pull you toward seeking their approval. Noticing the pattern is different from handing out praise as a strategy.

**Fear of exclusion.** There may be a hint that if you do not go along, you will be left out of the group, miss a chance, or end up alone. Even without a direct threat, refusal can start to feel urgent. Naming the hint is enough. This page does not describe how to plant that fear in someone else.

**Guilt lines.** A request may arrive tied to care: they are saying it only because they care, or you will hurt them if you refuse. A request can stay a request. The thing to notice is whether care is being turned into a debt.

**Inconsistent reactions.** Warmth and coldness, praise and withdrawal, may switch in ways you cannot predict, until you spend your attention trying to keep the peace. Some popular pages tell readers to be unpredictable on purpose. That is a tactic, not a research finding. If you are receiving it, you can notice the pattern of the switch. You have no reason to copy it.

**Mirroring.** Someone may match your phrasing, tastes, or feelings very quickly, then push the next step as if it were your idea. Early similarity does not prove a bond. You can slow down before you agree.

**False choices.** Two or three options may all lead to the same result, or the list may have no refusal on it. A narrow menu can feel like freedom. Noticing what was left out is the recognition. It is not a suggestion to narrow someone else's choices the same way.

## Where these patterns did not come from

Nietzsche, Dostoevsky, and Machiavelli did not found dark psychology, and they did not establish the patterns above as research. *The Prince* discusses political deception. Later writers used the phrase will to power for a drive to overcome. That phrase is not a result of the Dark Triad paper, and it is not the origin of the seven patterns on this page.

Positive psychology is a research conversation about well-being. A soft self-help line, that kindness is repaid with luck, is not that research. The claim that the world is a jungle, and that you must learn a predator's habits or be eaten, is not research either.

## After you notice

Seeing a pattern does not mean you should hide your weak points or read someone else's desires in order to come out ahead. You can watch whether the same line repeats over time. You can describe the scene to a person you trust. You can leave a conversation that keeps shrinking your choices. If your body is unwell, a clinician is the right person to see. This page does not make that judgment for you.

This page is not a diagnosis. It is not a license to manipulate.
