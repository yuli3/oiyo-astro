## What attachment theory can explain

Bowlby's theory concerns seeking support and proximity when security is threatened. Early relationships matter, but an adult's current responses cannot reconstruct a specific childhood history. Ainsworth's infant observation paradigm and adult relationship questionnaires examine different questions. [Research overview by R. Chris Fraley](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~rcfraley/attachment.htm)

An internal working model is a theoretical account of relationship expectations, not a brain scan or a permanent script that determines every relationship. Avoid inferring a person's caregiving history, trauma or brain activity from their answers to an online quiz.

---

## 1. Limits of type percentages

Type proportions depend on samples, age, culture and measurement. This article has no source establishing a global distribution and therefore supplies no percentages. Adult self-report categories and infant observational classifications are not the same measurement; type labels are not diagnoses.

---

## 2. Read adult attachment through two dimensions

**Attachment anxiety** concerns worries about a close other's availability and responsiveness. **Attachment avoidance** concerns discomfort with dependence or closeness. Scores vary continuously; four labels are shorthand for regions, not four diagnoses.

| Descriptive region | Anxiety | Avoidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secure | Lower | Lower |
| Preoccupied / anxious | Higher | Lower |
| Dismissing / avoidant | Lower | Higher |
| Fearful-avoidant | Higher | Higher |

This table illustrates the framework, not a scoring rule or clinical cutoff. Adult fearful-avoidant self-report descriptions should not be treated as identical to infant disorganized attachment observations. [Shaver and Fraley on adult self-report measures](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~rcfraley/measures/measures.html)

A single behavior, such as asking for reassurance or wanting time alone, is insufficient to assign a category. Consider the situation, relationship and repeated pattern rather than turning a moment into an identity.

---

## 3. Separate observation from interpretation

| What happened | An interpretation to examine | What else to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A reply arrived later than expected | “They no longer care” | Their schedule and the agreed response expectations |
| A partner requested time alone | “They are rejecting me” | Whether the request included when to reconnect |
| A conversation paused | “This is their attachment type” | Fatigue, safety, workload and the actual discussion |

These are editorial examples, not explanations of another person's motives. Check an interpretation with the person when appropriate rather than treating the table as proof.

<summary>A short reflection before assigning a label</summary>

Record the event without guessing motives. Note your feeling, the interpretation you made and what information is still missing. Then write one specific question or request. This exercise organizes observations; it does not diagnose attachment or guarantee conflict resolution.

## 4. Observe situations rather than assigning type scores

This article has no measurement data that score emotional regulation, independence or trust out of 100 by attachment type. A label cannot establish an individual's relationship abilities.

| Situation to reflect on | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| A disagreement | Actual words, actions and what changed afterward |
| Asking for help | The request and the response |
| Time alone | Feelings and the surrounding circumstances |

These are self-reflection prompts, not a diagnosis or averages for each type.

---

## 5. Do not rank treatment effectiveness

The previous treatment numbers have been removed because they lacked comparable samples, outcome measures, follow-up periods and sources. Partner relationships, psychotherapy and mindfulness cannot be ranked on one unsupported success scale. An attachment label does not select a treatment.

NIMH explains that treatment planning should reflect individual needs and medical circumstances under professional guidance. [Official psychotherapy information](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies)

This article and online tests do not replace diagnosis or treatment decisions.

---

## 6. A pair of labels is not a compatibility verdict

No pairing table in this article establishes who should date, stay together or separate. A partner is not responsible for “curing” another person's attachment pattern. Labels do not excuse coercion, threats or breaches of consent.

Discuss concrete expectations: how to ask for support, how to request a pause and when to resume, and what boundaries each person needs. Assess actual behavior and respect for agreements, not whether both labels appear “compatible.” This is a discussion checklist, not a promise that communication resolves every relationship problem.

---

## 7. Do not use attachment labels to rate workers

This article has no evidence that a label predicts leadership, turnover or individual performance. Do not infer a colleague's type from feedback preferences or use it in hiring or appraisal.

| Work issue | Evidence to examine |
| --- | --- |
| A deadline was missed | Scope, resources, dependencies and the agreed date |
| Feedback was difficult | Specific wording, setting and opportunity to clarify |
| Collaboration stalled | Roles, handoffs and decision ownership |

Keep work evaluation tied to relevant behavior and job requirements. A personality interpretation is not a substitute for investigating the circumstances.
