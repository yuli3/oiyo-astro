## Why the thought of death gets pushed aside

Phones, feeds, overwork, shopping. A lot of modern life does one job: it keeps death out of view.

In the *Pensées*, Blaise Pascal left a fragment usually numbered Lafuma 139. Paraphrased, it says that human unhappiness comes from not being able to sit quietly in a room. That is not a verified English quotation.

What shows up in the quiet? Irvin Yalom, a major existential psychiatrist, puts death at the center of that question.

## Four ultimate concerns

In *Existential Psychotherapy* (1980), Yalom sets out four ultimate concerns: death, freedom, existential isolation, and meaninglessness. This is his framework. *Staring at the Sun* (2008) is the later popular book.

His clinical thesis is that many problems come from avoiding death anxiety. That is his thesis, not an established finding.

Ernest Becker, in *The Denial of Death* (1973), argues that cultures are built partly to manage death anxiety. That sentence is his thesis.

## Heidegger, in paraphrase

In *Sein und Zeit* (1927), Martin Heidegger describes human existence as being-toward-death. He also writes of *das Man*, the anonymous they of everyday life: living by what one does, and not taking one's own death as one's own. A more owned life, in this paraphrase, does not look away from that fact. These sentences are paraphrases, not quotations.

## What illness seemed to change

Yalom reports people who changed how they lived after a serious illness. Small things mattered less, what mattered grew clearer, postponed acts were started, and relationships shifted. Those reports are clinical impressions, not a rule. Death meditation is not shown to produce the same effect.

## This page is not therapy

This page is not medical care. If fear of death is overwhelming, see a clinician.

The questions below are for reflection only. They are not a method shown to copy the effect of illness.

### Sitting with absence

Ask what the world would be if you had not been born, and what continues after you. The question is a way of noticing that a life is not guaranteed. It is not a treatment.

### Epictetus

*Enchiridion* 3 says, in substance, that when you kiss your child, you should remember that the child is mortal. The point is to see the person who is here, not to rehearse despair.

### A specific question

Vague dread is easy to circle. A plainer question is whether the life being lived is one you can stand beside. Stay with it only as far as it stays bearable.

### A time horizon

Ask what you would keep, and what you would drop, if the horizon were five years, or ten, or thirty. The numbers are a mirror, not a forecast.

## Wave and ocean

Yalom uses the metaphor of a wave and the ocean. The wave fears that it will disappear. In the image, the wave is a shape the ocean holds for a while. It is a metaphor he uses, not a description of what death is.

## Closing

Plato's *Phaedo* 64a reports Socrates saying that philosophy is practice for dying.

Read in paraphrase, Heidegger's being-toward-death means a life can become more one's own when it stops treating death as only other people's business. Read in paraphrase, Yalom treats awareness of death as a pressure to look at how one lives. Neither sentence is a quotation, and neither is medical advice.

Looking straight at death can make an ordinary hour feel less automatic. That is a philosophical suggestion on this page, not a promise of care.
