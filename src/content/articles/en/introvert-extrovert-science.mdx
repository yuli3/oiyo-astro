## The same gathering can leave people with different moods and levels of fatigue

You can feel tired after an enjoyable gathering, or want to see people even while resting quietly. Introversion and extraversion can help us talk about these experiences, but one response cannot establish your personality or how your brain works.

This article separates biological explanations, behavioral experiments and examples of everyday adjustments. An average difference in a study does not mean everyone responds in the same way.

## Think in terms of a continuum rather than two types

As a personality trait, extraversion is a dimension along which people vary in degree. It is also distinct from a single behavior in a conversation or gathering. A usually quiet person may speak actively in a particular situation.

The middle range is sometimes called ambiversion, but without defined assessment criteria and a representative sample, we cannot claim that “most people are ambiverts.” An extraversion score does not automatically reveal listening ability, decision speed, social skills or suitable careers either.

## How much can neurotransmitters and brain imaging explain?

[DeYoung's 2013 theoretical paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3827581/) integrates explanations of dopamine's relationships with reward, exploration and personality. It is not a new single experiment, and it leaves the range of dopamine-related personality traits as an open question. It cannot be translated into two dedicated circuits: dopamine for extraverts and acetylcholine for introverts.

The [abstract of Johnson and colleagues' 1999 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9989562/) reports associations between extraversion scores and regional cerebral blood flow measured by PET in 18 healthy adults. This does not mean it discovered two kinds of brain structure. It did not measure neurotransmitters, fatigue after socializing or burnout. Here we describe what the abstract supports, without reproducing the full paper's detailed analyses.

So we do not diagnose fatigue as forced use of an unfamiliar neural circuit or a shortage of a particular substance. A self-report introversion–extraversion test cannot measure cerebral blood flow or neurotransmitters either.

## Experiments in acting extraverted show both benefits and burdens

The [2019 paper by Jacques-Hamilton, Sun and Smillie](https://jessiesun.me/publication/jacques-hamilton-2019/jacques-hamilton-2019.pdf) followed 147 people randomly assigned to an acting-extraverted condition or an active control condition for one week. Data from 76 people in an earlier study were also used for comparison, but this was not an experiment randomly assigning 223 people at the same time. Compared with the active control condition, there were average benefits in positive affect and authenticity, while more introverted participants had smaller benefits or some costs in fatigue and negative affect. Momentary and retrospective reports must also be distinguished.

By contrast, the [2020 paper by Margolis and Lyubomirsky](https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Margolis-Lyubomirsky-2020.pdf) used a randomized-order design in which 131 university students spent one week acting extraverted and another acting introverted. It reported positive outcomes during the extraverted week and did not find differences in effects by baseline extraversion. Keeping these differing findings in view helps avoid the conclusion that acting extraverted necessarily harms introverts.

Short-term emotional changes do not demonstrate lasting personality change or the prevention or treatment of burnout. Research findings should not become mandatory behavioral prescriptions.

## Consider leadership alongside the team's proactivity

The [original 2011 paper by Grant, Gino and Hofmann](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/GrantGinoHofmann_Reversing.pdf) studied data from 57 managers and 374 employees at 57 US pizza stores, alongside a T-shirt-folding experiment involving 163 university students in 56 groups.

The store study examined associations, while the T-shirt-folding experiment manipulated leader behavior and employee proactivity. More extraverted behavior was advantageous with passive members, and less extraverted behavior with proactive members. Do not generalize this into a ranking of leaders across all occupations or an innate listening ability in introverts.

In practice, instead of type labels, you can examine how members' suggestions are heard, who makes decisions, and how direction and autonomy are adjusted. These are editorial reflection questions informed by research, not validated criteria for hiring or promotion.

We do not assume that every introvert must have time alone or that quick decisions are a strength of every extravert. Change a small condition at a time and listen to your own and others' responses.

## Self-reports do not diagnose your brain or abilities

The [Introversion–Extraversion Spectrum Test](/en/introvert-extrovert-test/) is a reference tool for reflecting on your current answers. It does not measure brain wiring, neurotransmitters, occupational abilities or your ideal environment. Do not use OIYO's results as an official clinical diagnosis.

Write down recent situations in which you felt comfortable, what felt demanding and which conditions you would like to change next. The aim is to examine situations and actual experiences rather than rely on a single score or type label.

The linked sources comprise a theoretical paper, the abstract of a small observational study, behavioral intervention studies, and field and experimental research on leadership. Each can address different questions. This article's everyday adjustment examples are not a validated treatment program.
