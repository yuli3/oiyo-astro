## 1. What Is Saju-Palja?

**Saju-palja (四柱八字)** is a core system of destiny interpretation in East Asian philosophy. "Saju" means the four pillars (四柱) and "palja" means the eight characters (八字): the **year, month, day, and hour** of a person's birth are each expressed as two Chinese characters, and your destiny is read from those eight characters in total.

Later texts transmit that Li Xuzhong (李虛中; Korean 이허중) worked from the year of birth, and that Xu Ziping (徐子平) is associated with the day-master method. Their lives are thinly recorded. The sources do not show the two of them jointly finishing the system in the Tang. *Yuanhai Ziping* (《淵海子平》) is a Song compilation associated with Xu Sheng (徐升). The study of destiny (*myeongri*, 命理) was carried on through later dynasties, and it is still widely used in Korea, China, Japan, and Taiwan.

The philosophical foundation of saju is the thought of **Yin-Yang and the Five Elements (陰陽五行)**. The theory holds that all things in the universe are composed of two energies, Yin (陰) and Yang (陽), and that the five elements — Wood (木), Fire (火), Earth (土), Metal (金), and Water (水) — circulate in endless relationships of generation and control. A person's destiny is seen as part of this vast cosmic flow.

## 2. Heavenly Stems (天干): The Ten Energies of the Sky

The **Heavenly Stems (天干, Cheongan)** are ten Chinese characters representing the energies of the sky. Each carries its own element and Yin-Yang attribute. The notes below are traditional images of character and talent, not measured personality scores.

### Personalities of the Ten Stems

**Gap (甲) – Yang Wood (陽木)**
Like a sprout breaking through the soil in spring, it has strong vitality and a pioneering spirit. Strong in leadership and willing to take on challenges, but at times it can be domineering and inflexible. Excellent at starting new things.

**Eul (乙) – Yin Wood (陰木)**
Like a climbing vine, it adapts flexibly to its environment. Delicate and richly sensitive, it values relationships. Rather than fighting head-on, it achieves goals through gentle means.

**Byeong (丙) – Yang Fire (陽火)**
Bright and warm like the sun. Outgoing and sociable, with the charm to brighten everything around it. Passionate, though at times impulsive.

**Jeong (丁) – Yin Fire (陰火)**
Steady, concentrated energy like a candle flame. Gifted with artistic sensibility, devoted, and capable of deep love. It holds an inner passion while appearing calm on the outside.

**Mu (戊) – Yang Earth (陽土)**
Stable and dependable like a high mountain. Strongly responsible and trusted, well suited to the role of mediator. It seeks stability over change.

**Gi (己) – Yin Earth (陰土)**
It has the embracing capacity to hold everything, like fertile ground. Practical and meticulous, sensitive to others' feelings, with a strong spirit of service.

**Gyeong (庚) – Yang Metal (陽金)**
Strong and decisive like an axe. It values principle, speaks plainly, and does not compromise with injustice. At times it can appear overly rigid.

**Sin (辛) – Yin Metal (陰金)**
Refined and perfectionistic like a jewel. It has an outstanding aesthetic sense and sharp analytical power, with an excellent ability to focus on detail.

**Im (壬) – Yang Water (陽水)**
Wise and flexible like a great river. Possessing deep thought and excellent memory, with strong philosophical leanings. Highly adaptable, though it can easily lose direction.

**Gye (癸) – Yin Water (陰水)**
Quiet, with a deep inner world like a drizzle. Highly intuitive, with a mysterious charm. Richly sensitive and often showing artistic talent.

## 3. Earthly Branches (地支): The Twelve Energies of the Earth

The **Earthly Branches (地支, Jiji)** are twelve Chinese characters representing the energies of the earth, linked to twelve animals. They symbolize the seasons, the hours, and the stages of human life.

### Meaning and Traits of the Twelve Branches

| Branch | Animal | Old month name | Hours (approx.) | Key Traits |
|------|------|-----|------|----------|
| Ja (子) | Rat | Nov | 23:00–01:00 | Wisdom, agility, wealth luck |
| Chuk (丑) | Ox | Dec | 01:00–03:00 | Patience, diligence, persistence |
| In (寅) | Tiger | Jan | 03:00–05:00 | Courage, leadership, passion |
| Myo (卯) | Rabbit | Feb | 05:00–07:00 | Gentleness, artistry, delicacy |
| Jin (辰) | Dragon | Mar | 07:00–09:00 | Charisma, power, change |
| Sa (巳) | Snake | Apr | 09:00–11:00 | Wisdom, prudence, intuition |
| O (午) | Horse | May | 11:00–13:00 | Activity, passion, freedom |
| Mi (未) | Goat | Jun | 13:00–15:00 | Peace, artistry, consideration |
| Sin (申) | Monkey | Jul | 15:00–17:00 | Wit, versatility, opportunism |
| Yu (酉) | Rooster | Aug | 17:00–19:00 | Perfectionism, diligence, sharpness |
| Sul (戌) | Dog | Sep | 19:00–21:00 | Loyalty, sense of justice, tenacity |
| Hae (亥) | Pig | Oct | 21:00–23:00 | Purity, abundance, optimism |

The **January** beside the Tiger is the old name of the first month, not solar January. In myeongri the month changes at a solar term: the Tiger month (인월) starts at Ipchun (입춘), around 4 February. The hours are the traditional double-hours, a clock approximation.

## 4. The Four Pillars: Year, Month, Day & Hour

Saju-palja places a Heavenly Stem and an Earthly Branch on each of the year, month, day, and hour of birth, forming four pillars (四柱).

Age bands sometimes attached to the pillars — about 0–15, 15–30, 30–50, and after 50 — are a **later teaching**, not a fixed classical rule.

### Year Pillar (年柱) – Ancestors and the early picture
The stem-branch of the birth year. Traditional writing places ancestral blessings, the relationship with parents, and the outward impression beside it. A later teaching also reads it as the setting of childhood. Someone born in 1990, the Gyeong-O year, has a year pillar of **庚午 (Gyeong-O)**.

### Month Pillar (月柱) – Parents and the social setting
The stem-branch of the birth month. The month is not the solar-calendar month; it changes at a solar term. Traditional writing often places parents, siblings, work, and the social setting beside the month pillar. A later teaching also divides it as the picture of youth. It is one of the pillars readers treat as weighty, but one pillar does not decide a temperament.

### Day Pillar (日柱) – Oneself and the partner
The stem-branch of the birth day. The **Day Stem (日干, Ilgan)** stands for the self and is the reference point of a reading. Traditional writing also places the relationship with a spouse beside the day pillar. A later teaching divides it as the picture of midlife.

### Hour Pillar (時柱) – Children and the later picture
The stem-branch of the birth hour. The hour is a traditional double-hour, matched only approximately to the clock. Traditional writing places children and the setting of later life beside the hour pillar. A later teaching divides it as the years after about 50. It is not a cell that settles how old age will turn out.

## 5. The Five Elements (五行): Balance Is Everything

The Five Elements are **Wood (木), Fire (火), Earth (土), Metal (金), and Water (水)** — the basic energies that compose the universe and human beings. In saju, balance among the five elements is a traditional symbol for how a chart is read. It is not a medical reading. If the body is unwell, see a clinician.

### Generation (相生) and Control (相剋)

**Generation (mutually nourishing relationships)**
- Wood generates Fire (木生火): wood makes fire
- Fire generates Earth (火生土): fire makes earth
- Earth generates Metal (土生金): earth makes metal
- Metal generates Water (金生水): metal makes water
- Water generates Wood (水生木): water makes wood

**Control (mutually restraining relationships)**
- Wood controls Earth (木剋土): wood pierces the earth
- Earth controls Water (土剋水): earth dams the water
- Water controls Fire (水剋火): water extinguishes the fire
- Fire controls Metal (火剋金): fire melts the metal
- Metal controls Wood (金剋木): metal cuts the wood

### Plenty, absence, and emptiness

Counting only the eight characters and calling three or more of one element **excess** is a **popular counting shortcut**. It is not a fixed classical law, and it ignores the hidden stems inside the branches (지장간).

An element that does not appear at all is **deficiency** (결핍). **Emptiness, 공망 (空亡), is not a missing element.** It is the empty-branch pair of the ten-day cycle (旬) to which the day pillar belongs.

The notes below are images traditional writing often draws when one element stands out. They are not personality scores and not a medical finding.

- **When Wood (木) stands out**: a picture of pushing ahead, with an image of stubbornness that passes over other people's words.
- **When Fire (火) stands out**: a picture of passion, with an image of a wide swing of feeling and of haste.
- **When Earth (土) stands out**: a picture of stability, with an image of putting change off and of slow movement.
- **When Metal (金) stands out**: a picture of decision, with an image of coldness and of sharp speech.
- **When Water (水) stands out**: a picture of flexibility, with an image of delaying a decision.

## 6. The Ten Gods (十神): Ten Keywords for Reading Your Chart

In saju interpretation, the **Ten Gods (十神, Sipsin)** are ten concepts that express the relationship between the Day Stem (you yourself) and the other characters.

| Ten God | Meaning | Traits |
|------|------|------|
| Bigyeon (比肩) | A peer like yourself | Independence, competitiveness |
| Geopjae (劫財) | One who takes your wealth | Combativeness, entrepreneurial nature |
| Siksin (食神) | What you generate | Expressiveness, good fortune, creativity |
| Sanggwan (傷官) | That which injures the Officer | Eloquence, rebelliousness, artistry |
| Jeongjae (正財) | Proper wealth | Diligence, saving, spouse (for men) |
| Pyeonjae (偏財) | Uneven wealth | Business, investment, father |
| Jeonggwan (正官) | Proper office | Honor, discipline, spouse (for women) |
| Pyeongwan (偏官) | Uneven office | Power, struggle, intuition |
| Jeongin (正印) | Proper seal | Learning, honor, mother |
| Pyeonin (偏印) | Uneven seal | Intuition, solitude, special talents |

In the table, proper wealth is the spouse in a male chart, proper officer is the spouse in a female chart, uneven wealth is the father, and the proper seal is the mother. These are the **old kinship labels of a male chart**, not a universal rule.

## 7. Luck Pillars, Annual & Monthly Luck: Reading the Flow of Time

Saju does not stop at static analysis. Through the **Luck Pillars (大運, Daeun), Annual Luck (歲運, Seun), and Monthly Luck (月運, Wolun)**, it predicts changes in fortune as time flows.

### Luck Pillars (大運) – The Big Flow in 10-Year Cycles

The Luck Pillars run on from the stem-branch of the month pillar, in stretches of about ten years. The common teaching takes a yang-year man or a yin-year woman **forward**, and a yin-year man or a yang-year woman **backward**.

Count the days toward the **next** solar term if the count goes forward, and toward the **previous** solar term if it goes backward. About three days count as one year. The count is not always to the next term.

If that count comes out as 3, the reading steps the stem-branch about every ten years from that age. Ages 3, 13, 23, and 33 are only that example.

### Annual Luck (歲運) – The Yearly Flow

Annual Luck is the stem-branch of each changing year. 2025 is the year of Eul-Sa (乙巳), and 2026 is the year of Byeong-O (丙午). Traditional writing calls a smooth fit a milder year, and clash, punishment, or control a year of collision and movement. That is not a forecast that an accident will happen.

### Monthly Luck (月運) – The Detailed Monthly Flow

Monthly Luck reads a finer flow through the stem-branch of each month. The month itself is counted from a solar term. Even when the luck pillars and the year are read as smooth, a rough relation in that month is a symbol of care in the traditional reading; even when the luck pillar is rough, a fitting month is a symbol of an opening. Neither settles the events of the month.

## 8. Clash, Punishment, Combination & Breaking: Relationships Between Characters

The eight characters of a chart influence one another.

**Combination (合) – Two characters merge to create a new element**
- Stem combinations: Gap-Gi combine into Earth (甲己合化土), Eul-Gyeong combine into Metal (乙庚合化金), etc.
- Branch triple-combinations: In-O-Sul form a Fire frame (寅午戌合火局), Sin-Ja-Jin form a Water frame (申子辰合水局), etc.

**Clash (沖) – Two characters collide and the energy is shaken**
- Branch clashes: Ja-O clash (子午沖), Chuk-Mi clash (丑未沖), In-Sin clash (寅申沖), etc.
- The texts read clash as collision, movement, and change. It is not a forecast of accidents.

**Punishment (刑) – Three characters meet and generate punishing energy**
- In-Sa-Sin (寅巳申) and Chuk-Sul-Mi (丑戌未) are called the triple punishments.
- They are symbols of friction and difficulty, not a forecast of punishment or illness.

**Breaking (破) – Two characters break each other**
- Ja-Yu break (子酉破), O-Myo break (午卯破), etc.
- A symbol of a relationship or a plan falling out of line. It does not settle a rupture.

## 9. The Useful God (用神): The Energy Your Chart Needs Most

The **Useful God (用神, Yongsin)** is the element a chart is said to need most. There is more than one way to seek it. The list is the **support-suppress method (억부), climate adjustment (조후), mediation (통관), the illness-and-remedy method (병약), and following the dominant phase (전왕)**. Readers do not all take the same path. The illness-and-remedy method is a traditional name for a way of reading. It is not a medical diagnosis.

If Wood (木) and Fire (火) are plentiful and Water (水) is absent, taking Water as the useful phase is **one support-suppress (억부) reading**. It is not the only method.

Traditional reading sometimes writes colors, directions, and settings beside the useful phase. That is not a measured prescription. Matching a name's stroke count to the useful phase belongs to the later naming custom below.

## 10. What to Know Before a Saju Reading

### An Accurate Birth Date and Time Are Essential

In saju-palja, the **Hour Pillar (時柱)** is determined by your birth time. Because it changes every two hours, even a one- or two-hour difference in birth time produces a different chart. Check your exact birth time on your birth certificate or with your mother.

### Saju Is a Map of Possibility

Saju is not a verdict that settles a fate. The same eight characters can belong to more than one life, and those lives are not the same. Read the chart here as a **map** of traditional symbols for temperament and setting. The map does not choose the road.

### How to Choose a Good Saju Reader

1. **Check qualifications and experience**: confirm their study of myeongri and their counseling experience.
2. **Counseling style**: avoid readers who use intimidation or ominous hints to push extra charges.
3. **Concrete advice**: choose a place that gives realistic, specific advice rather than vague prophecy.
4. **Transparent pricing**: a place that clearly explains fees in advance is more trustworthy.

### Saju and naming

Matching the stroke count and the elements of a name to the useful phase is a **later naming custom**, not a measured effect. It can be consulted as a custom when naming a child or considering a change of name. It is not a method that repairs fortune.

Saju is a long East Asian tradition of interpretation. Read it here as a traditional **map of symbols**.
