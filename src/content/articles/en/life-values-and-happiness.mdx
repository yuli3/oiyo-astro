## You've Succeeded — So Why Doesn't It Feel Like Enough?

You landed the job you wanted. Your salary went up. And yet something feels off — an inexplicable emptiness.

That feeling alone does not establish that you pursued the wrong values. Work demands, rest, relationships and health also deserve attention. Values reflection is one way to explore your situation, not a diagnostic test.

Here, a gap between values and actions means a difference between stated priorities and choices. It is not a diagnosis explaining every experience of emptiness.

---

## What Are Values, Really?

Values aren't just preferences or things you enjoy. They are **deep beliefs about what truly matters in life**.

Milton Rokeach's classification distinguishes:

- **Instrumental Values**: modes of behavior used to reach your goals (honesty, responsibility, creativity)
- **Terminal Values**: the ultimate states you want to reach (freedom, family happiness, security, world peace)

Distinguish this classification from the editorial examples below. Resources, rules, habits and responsibilities can also shape behavior.

| Concept | Example | What to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value | Continue learning | What direction matters to you? |
| Goal | Pass a qualification exam | How will you recognize completion? |
| Action | Explain one mistake today | Is it feasible now? |
| Constraint | Care responsibilities limit study time | What adjustment or support is needed? |

This table compares concepts; it is not a personality assessment.

## Why Most People Don't Know Their Own Values

Putting priorities into words can be difficult. These are reflection prompts, not survey findings that most people do not understand themselves.

**1. The influence of socialization**
Consider whether standards learned from family, school or media still matter to you. Learning a value from others does not make it false.

**2. The comparison trap**
Notice whether seeing others' achievements changes what you want. Comparison alone does not establish that your values have been taken over.

**3. No time for reflection**
If a large life question feels overwhelming, start with one recent choice. Consider the conditions limiting time for reflection as well.

## Do not infer a single cause from a feeling

| Experience | Question to explore | Conclusion to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emptiness after achievement | What change did you expect, and what changed? | You definitely pursued someone else's values |
| Exhaustion or low motivation | What were workload, rest and living conditions like? | Changing values alone will resolve it |
| Difficult decisions | Is information missing, or do responsibilities conflict? | You have no genuine values |
| Envy | Which part of the other person's situation appeals to you? | Envy precisely reveals a core value |

This is not a diagnostic checklist. If low mood or loss of interest persists or affects daily life, consider discussing it with a health professional rather than relying on a values exercise. [NIMH depression information](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression)

## Positive Psychology and a Values-Driven Life

PERMA describes well-being through positive emotion, engagement, relationships, meaning and accomplishment. It does not make meaning the sole cause or guarantee happiness. [Penn overview](https://ppc.sas.upenn.edu/learn-more/perma-theory-well-being-and-perma-workshops)

The ACT association connects acceptance, mindfulness, behavior change and chosen values with psychological flexibility. This does not mean ignoring emotions, and sorting cards cannot substitute for treatment. [ACT association overview](https://contextualscience.org/act)

Clarifying values can help articulate a decision criterion. It does not guarantee easy decisions or greater satisfaction.

## How to Discover Your Values

**Method 1: Recall peak experiences**
Recall meaningful moments and consider possible priorities. One common thread does not establish a definitive core value.

**Method 2: Trace your anger backward**
Ask what mattered in a situation that made you angry. Do not treat anger as proof of a value violation or a verdict on another person.

**Method 3: The 100-year imagination**
Imagine yourself at 100, looking back at your life. What do you most want to feel proud of?

**Method 4: Forced-choice test**
Compare costs, responsibilities and priorities between two good options. One forced choice does not reveal a true personality or permanent value.

<details>
<summary>Questions for turning a priority into action</summary>

Choose one direction that matters now. What action is feasible today, and what support do you need? If responsibilities conflict, what could be adjusted? Afterward, separate what worked from difficult conditions. These reflection questions have no score or passing threshold.

</details>

## Values Can Change

Priorities can be reconsidered as circumstances change. Family may become more important than adventure, for example, but this is not a prescribed age sequence or a grade of personal growth.

Express current priorities while considering safety, resources and others' rights. Being unable to act on a preference does not establish a lack of authenticity.
