## FOMO is closer to relational anxiety than to a lack of information

FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) means, quite literally, the fear of missing something. It is the sense that you were the only one left out of a gathering, that everyone on social media is living a more interesting and meaningful life, and that if you miss an opportunity or a trend, you will fall behind.

On the surface it looks like a wish to know the latest. At the center of FOMO, though, there is often **belonging and comparison**. People are social, so they are sensitive to the feeling of being left out of a group that matters. The trouble is that phones and social media keep that sensitivity switched on all day.

This article is a psychology guide for self-understanding. It does not replace a medical diagnosis or a decision about treatment.

---

## Przybylski's concept of FoMO

One of the researchers who organized FOMO as a topic in psychology is Andrew K. Przybylski. With his colleagues, Przybylski described FoMO as **a persistent worry that other people might be having rewarding experiences, and a desire to stay continually connected so you know what they are doing**.

Two parts of that definition matter.

| Core | Meaning |
|------|------|
| Other people's rewarding experiences | The place, opportunity, or relationship you missed starts to look more valuable |
| The desire to stay connected | Not checking feels anxious; checking brings a short relief |

FOMO is not simple curiosity. It is closer to the unease of not knowing than to the wish to know. Checking calms you for a moment, then it calls for more checking.

---

## Why social media feeds FOMO

Social media does not show the whole of someone else's life. It shows **selected scenes**. The dull hours of travel, the friction before a gathering, and the fatigue after an achievement mostly stay out of frame. What stays in front is the smile, the striking place, the fast success, and the tightly woven social life.

The brain then compares their highlight with the whole of your ordinary day. The comparison was unfair from the start, and still it slides easily toward the conclusion that you are the one who is lacking.

Algorithms play a part too. Content that draws more reaction is shown more often, and the more dazzling experience is the one that stays visible. Scenes far more dramatic than the average life around you begin to feel like an ordinary day.

As FOMO grows, checking social media stops being a rest and becomes surveillance. The same questions keep surfacing. What happened? Who met whom? Was I the one not invited?

---

## FOMO and basic psychological needs

Self-determination theory holds that people have basic psychological needs, including autonomy, competence, and relatedness. FOMO tends to grow especially when relatedness and competence are shaken.

When relatedness is shaken, the worry gets louder: am I still connected to the people who matter? When competence is shaken, the comparison gets stronger: am I living this in a way that counts? When autonomy is low, other people's choices look more like the right answer than your own.

That is why FOMO is hard to solve by only saying use social media less. Using it less helps. If you do not look at the need underneath, the checking can move somewhere else. Work chat, investing communities, group threads, and calendar invites can all become a stage for FOMO.

## Why one check calls for another

FOMO stays in place through a loop of anxiety and checking.

| Stage | What the mind does |
|------|---------------|
| Anxiety | It feels as if I am the only one missing something |
| Checking | Social media, chats, news, communities |
| Relief | A brief sense of having caught up |
| Anxiety again | Wondering whether something newer is already there |

Checking pays off immediately. The mind learns the loop: when I feel anxious, I should check. Over time, the ability to stay with not knowing gets thinner. Even a small gap of information feels uncomfortable, and quiet time starts to feel like a threat.

---

## JOMO: recovering the joy of missing out

JOMO (Joy of Missing Out) is often named as the counterpart of FOMO. It does not mean you must enjoy missing things on principle. It is closer to accepting that you cannot be in every room or follow every trend, and to being able to stay with the time you chose.

JOMO is not an information blackout. It is **a return of choice**. You sleep instead of going to one gathering. You read with some depth instead of chasing one trend. You stay with the person in front of you instead of answering a group thread at once.

It helps to start small.

1. Choose a stretch of the day when notifications stay off.
2. Before you open social media, write one sentence about what you are trying to check right now.
3. Separate accounts that pull you into comparison from accounts where you learn or actually connect.
4. After you miss something, note whether a serious problem actually followed.

If the urge to check keeps returning, you can look at your pattern with the [FOMO scale test](https://oiyo.net/en/fomo-test/). The result is not a score for judging yourself. Use it as a clue to the situations where the anxiety grows.

---

## Look at what you are losing, not only at what you missed

FOMO tells you that you cannot miss that. Sometimes the more useful question sits on the other side. What am I losing by checking all the time?

If focus, sleep, deeper conversation, ease in being alone, and trust in your own choices are thinning, FOMO may be more than a habit. It may be a sign that the rhythm of your life is being shaken. The harder you try not to miss anything, the more of the time you already have slips away.

Reducing FOMO is not the same as cutting yourself off from the world. It is recovering the sense that you do not have to stay connected every minute, so that the connections you do keep can be healthier.

---

## In short

- Przybylski's concept of FoMO describes the worry of missing other people's rewarding experiences, and the desire to stay continually connected.
- Social media can intensify FOMO by setting other people's selected highlights next to your ordinary day.
- Checking brings a short relief, and over time it can make the anxiety easier to flare up again.
- JOMO is not an attitude of giving everything up. It is the ability to stay with the time you chose.
