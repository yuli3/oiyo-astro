A chart marks your two types as an ideal match. Does that tell you whether your partner keeps promises, how you will handle conflict, or how long the relationship will last? This article examines the evidence behind dating-style descriptions and compatibility charts rather than choosing an ideal partner for each type.

## Define what “compatible” means

An enjoyable first date, satisfying conversations, and relationship duration are different outcomes. If a chart does not define its outcome, readers can give the same color or grade different meanings.

[The Myers & Briggs Foundation's relationship guidance](https://www.myersbriggs.org/type-in-my-life/personality-type-and-relationships/) does not identify a best or more successful type combination. This is the model provider's guidance, not independent research showing that this page's examples improve relationships.

| Wording | What to check separately |
| --- | --- |
| They complement each other | In what respect, and in which situation? |
| An often-mentioned pair | Which articles were counted, when, and how many? |
| A lasting match | Was duration tracked, or does the explanation merely sound plausible? |

Even an accurate count of mentions would not measure relationship success. Without a source, “often mentioned” establishes neither popularity nor effectiveness.

## Do not turn type into character

“ENFPs struggle with commitment” and “ISTJs are unwavering partners” skip the work of checking an individual's behavior. An ENTJ or ESTJ label does not make controlling behavior acceptable; T does not excuse disregarding a partner's feelings.

Type cannot choose someone's ideal partner either. Contact habits, time apart, money and promises are subjects to ask the person about. For practical coordination examples, read [relationship conversations through four preference pairs](/en/mbti-relationship-patterns/).

## Five checks for a compatibility chart

This is an editorial reading checklist, not a score or validated scale.

| Check | What to look for in the original source |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome | Did it measure satisfaction, conflict, or duration? |
| Sample | Who participated, how were they recruited, and how many were in each pairing? |
| Measurement | Which tools and time points established types and relationship outcomes? |
| Comparison | Were other pairings compared and differences such as time together considered? |
| New-case prediction | Was prediction checked on people not used to create the chart? |

Recruiting only current couples can omit relationships that ended. People who volunteer reviews cannot be assumed to represent all couples. Unequal numbers in each pairing also make raw counts an inadequate ranking.

An observed difference does not by itself establish that type caused it. A group result cannot settle the future of two individuals either. When the original source or methods are missing, distinguish “this chart cannot establish its claim” from “the claim has been disproved.”

## Do not delegate relationship choices to a type

[The official code of ethics](https://www.myersbriggs.org/using-type-as-a-professional/mbti-code-of-ethics/) advises against recommending or limiting personal relationships solely on type.

You may use [OIYO's type-reflection tool](/en/mbti/test/) as a conversation prompt if you wish. We do not present it as the official MBTI assessment or a validated predictor of romantic success. Respect someone's choice not to take it or share a result.
