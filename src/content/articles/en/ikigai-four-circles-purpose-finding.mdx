## What is ikigai?

**Ikigai** (生き甲斐, pronounced ee-kee-guy) is a Japanese word made of *iki* (生き, life, living) and *kai* (甲斐, worth, value). Taken literally, it means "the worth of being alive."

In Japanese culture the word is far more everyday and subtle than "life goal" or "mission." It can be a cup of coffee in the morning, tending a garden, or the small ritual of greeting your coworkers each day. In Japanese, an ikigai does not have to earn money or be something you are good at. The psychiatrist Mieko Kamiya (1966) and the neuroscientist Ken Mogi (2017) both describe it as the feelings and things that make you glad to be alive.

What spread much more widely in the West, however, is a **diagram of four overlapping circles**. It actually comes from a "Purpose" (*Propósito*) diagram drawn by the Spanish astrologer Andrés Zuzunaga in 2011. In 2014 the British blogger Marc Winn replaced the label in the middle with "Ikigai," and the picture took off as "the ikigai diagram." So the four circles are not what ikigai originally means in Japanese; they are a Western reinterpretation. The diagram is not a validated psychological assessment, but as a set of questions for self-reflection it can still be useful.

---

## The four circles

The four-circle diagram asks you four questions:

| Circle | Question |
|---|---|
| **What you love** | What do you enjoy so much that you do it without anyone pushing you? |
| **What you are good at** | What do you do better than most people? |
| **What the world needs** | What genuinely helps other people or society? |
| **What you can be paid for** | What can bring you a sustainable income? |

<Callout type="tip">
**What the overlaps mean** (using the labels from the Zuzunaga/Winn diagram):
- Love + good at = **Passion**
- Love + world needs = **Mission**
- Good at + paid for = **Profession**
- World needs + paid for = **Vocation**
- **The very center** = the spot the diagram labels "ikigai"
</Callout>

---

## When the areas are out of balance

If you sit in the overlap of only two circles rather than in the center, people commonly describe feelings like these:

**Passion (love + good at, missing pay and the world's needs)**  
→ There is satisfaction, but money can be tight, and it may feel like "just a hobby, not real work."

**Profession (good at + paid, missing love)**  
→ Materially stable, but empty, with a resigned "so this is it" feeling.

**Mission (love + world needs, missing pay)**  
→ Full of meaning, but hard to sustain and easy to burn out.

**Vocation (world needs + paid, missing love)**  
→ A decent income, but you don't want to get out of bed in the morning.

## How to look for your ikigai

### Step 1: Make your lists

Write down:
- 10 things you love
- 10 things you are good at
- 10 things you think the world needs
- 10 things you have been, or could be, paid for

### Step 2: Look for overlaps

Don't expect to find "the one perfect thing." Ikigai is more of a direction than an exact coordinate. And remember the original Japanese meaning: something can be your ikigai even if it never earns you a cent.

### Step 3: Test in small steps

Don't quit your job to "chase the dream." Test your hypothesis in your spare time first. A side project, a weekend experiment or a stint of volunteering will all give you real data.

## Ikigai and longevity: don't overstate it

Okinawa is often listed among the world's longer-lived regions, and many bestsellers credit that longevity to ikigai. But long life depends on many factors, such as diet, social ties and healthcare, and no study has shown that ikigai is the cause of Okinawans' longevity. A cohort study in Ōsaki, Miyagi Prefecture (Sone et al., 2008) found that people who said they had ikigai had lower mortality, but it was an observational study: it shows an association, not cause and effect.

Perhaps the deepest insight of ikigai is this: **meaning isn't something you find; it's something you slowly build through everyday actions.**

You don't need the perfect answer before you start living. All you need today is one reason, however small, that makes getting up worthwhile.
