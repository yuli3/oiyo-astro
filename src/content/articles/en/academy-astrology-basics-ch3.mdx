## What Are Planets in Astrology?

Astrology uses **planet** as a conventional grouping for symbolic interpretation. Astronomically, the Sun is a star, the Moon is Earth's natural satellite, and Pluto is a dwarf planet. The phrase ten planets is therefore not an astronomical count. [NASA's planetary guide](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/) distinguishes the eight planets from dwarf planets; it does not validate personality interpretations.

This chapter organizes the interpretive conventions into three groups. Energy here is a metaphor, not a measured physical quantity:

| Group | Planets | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal planets** | Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars | Move quickly; represent personal traits |
| **Social/generational planets** | Jupiter, Saturn | Mid-speed; represent social patterns |
| **Transpersonal planets** | Uranus, Neptune, Pluto | Move slowly; represent generational influence |

Orbital periods listed from Mercury through Pluto are approximate circuits around the Sun. Residence times in signs use Earth-viewed positions and are not the same quantities; retrograde motion and orbital geometry affect them. Assigning personal meanings is separate from calculating these periods.

## Personal Planets

### ☀️ Sun — The Core Self

**Symbolizes**: Ego, will, conscious identity, father
**Ruling sign**: ♌ Leo
**Apparent circuit of the ecliptic as seen from Earth**: About one year, averaging roughly 1° per day. This does not mean the Sun orbits Earth once a year.

The Sun represents **"Who am I?"** The 'zodiac sign' most people know is the Sun sign.

Themes traditionally assigned to the Sun include the following. These are not measurements of a person's purpose or family relationships:
- The central purpose and direction of life
- Conscious self-identity
- Relationship with father and authority figures

A strong Sun is sometimes read as a metaphor for confidence, and a challenged Sun for questions of self-worth. Do not use these labels to evaluate someone's actual leadership or self-esteem.

### 🌙 Moon — Emotions and the Unconscious

**Symbolizes**: Emotions, unconscious, mother, habits, instinct
**Ruling sign**: ♋ Cancer
**Sidereal orbital period around Earth**: About 27.3 days. This is not the lunar phase cycle.

The Moon is associated with the question **"How do I feel?"** This does not mean a Moon sign measures emotional responses or unconscious instincts.

<Callout type="tip">
**Reading the Moon**: Astrologers assign emotional and habitual themes to the Moon. This is not a measured finding that emotions follow Moon signs, or evidence that Moon signs predict behavior better than Sun signs.
</Callout>

### ☿ Mercury — Communication and Thought

**Symbolizes**: Thinking style, communication, language, learning, movement
**Ruling signs**: ♊ Gemini, ♍ Virgo

Mercury represents **"How do I think and communicate?"**

Retrograde motion is a temporary apparent reversal across the sky as viewed from Earth, not a reversal of Mercury's orbit around the Sun. Its 88-day orbit is not its residence time in each sign. Associations with communication or contract problems are interpretations, not established causes of accidents. [NASA's Mercury facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mercury/facts/) describes its 88-day solar orbit.

### ♀ Venus — Love and Beauty

**Symbolizes**: Love, beauty, relationships, values, pleasure
**Ruling signs**: ♉ Taurus, ♎ Libra

Venus represents **"What do I love and how do I form relationships?"**

Themes associated with Venus include the following. Confirm preferences through the person's own experiences and statements, not assumptions based on a chart:
- What type of partner you're attracted to
- How you express and receive love in relationships
- What you find beautiful

### ♂ Mars — Action and Desire

**Symbolizes**: Drive, desire, passion, competition, anger
**Ruling sign**: ♈ Aries (also traditionally assigned to ♏ Scorpio)

Mars represents **"How do I act and what do I desire?"**

Mars is associated symbolically with attraction, desire, and initiative. A chart is not a tool for inferring someone's sexual preferences or consent.

## Social Planets

### ♃ Jupiter — Expansion and Luck

**Symbolizes**: Expansion, luck, philosophy, education, optimism
**Ruling sign**: ♐ Sagittarius (also traditionally assigned to ♓ Pisces)

Jupiter represents **"Where do I grow and encounter good fortune?"**

Reading Jupiter's sign and house as growth or expansion is a symbolic convention. It does not calculate where success or investment returns are most likely.

### ♄ Saturn — Structure and Discipline

**Symbolizes**: Limitation, responsibility, structure, discipline, time
**Ruling sign**: ♑ Capricorn (also traditionally assigned to ♒ Aquarius)

Saturn represents **"Where am I tested and where do I grow?"**

**Saturn Return**: Saturn's Earth-viewed ecliptic longitude returns to its birth-time value, with the first return occurring roughly around age 29–30. Astrologers associate it with responsibility and growth, but it does not guarantee a life transition or establish when someone becomes mature.

<Callout type="note">
**Limits of this reading**: Responsibility, limitation, and growth are symbolic themes. Hardship does not necessarily produce growth, and a celestial placement does not establish why someone suffers.
</Callout>

## Transpersonal Planets

### ♅ Uranus — Innovation and Freedom

**Symbolizes**: Revolution, originality, freedom, change, technology
**Ruling sign**: ♒ Aquarius

Uranus remains in a sign for years and is therefore grouped as a generational planet. Near ingresses or retrograde crossings, people with nearby birth dates can have different placements. Revolution is a symbolic association, not evidence that a shared placement determines a generation's political behavior.

### ♆ Neptune — Spirituality and Dissolution

**Symbolizes**: Spirituality, illusion, art, compassion, dissolution
**Ruling sign**: ♓ Pisces

Neptune is associated symbolically with boundaries, art, and imagination. Its placement does not measure artistic talent or establish someone's honesty.

### ♇ Pluto — Transformation and Regeneration

**Symbolizes**: Destruction and rebirth, power, depth, transformation
**Ruling sign**: ♏ Scorpio

Pluto is associated symbolically with change and regeneration. Its passage through a position does not establish that a person or society must undergo complete breakdown.

## Orbital periods are not sign-residence times

The table transcribes the orbital-period row of the [NASA Planetary Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/) in days. The Moon orbits Earth; the other entries orbit the Sun. Including Earth's period does not mean the Sun orbits Earth.

| Body | Orbital period (days) |
|---|---:|
| Mercury | 88.0 |
| Venus | 224.7 |
| Earth | 365.2 |
| Moon | 27.3 |
| Mars | 687.0 |
| Jupiter | 4331 |
| Saturn | 10747 |
| Uranus | 30589 |
| Neptune | 59800 |
| Pluto | 90560 |

These values compare astronomical timescales. Sign-residence times viewed from Earth vary with relative motion and retrograde crossings; dividing an orbital period by twelve does not give a fixed stay in each sign. The Moon's 27.3 days is also not the full-Moon-to-full-Moon phase cycle.

## Planetary Strength: The Dignity System

Dignity is a traditional system for classifying relations between bodies and signs. It does not measure gravity, brightness, or a person's abilities.

| State | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Domicile** | In a sign traditionally assigned to that body | Sun in Leo |
| **Exaltation** | In a sign traditionally assigned as its exaltation | Sun in Aries |
| **Detriment** | In the sign 180° opposite its domicile | Sun in Aquarius |
| **Fall** | In the sign 180° opposite its exaltation | Sun in Libra |

<Callout type="tip">
**Scope**: These are different correspondences, not a single strongest-to-weakest ranking. Traditional readings also consider aspects and houses. Do not use these labels as scores for a person's worth, health, or luck.
</Callout>

The Sun–Leo assignment appears in [Tetrabiblos Book I, section 17, and the Aries exaltation and opposite Libra assignment in section 19](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ptolemy/Tetrabiblos/1B*.html). The text's natural philosophy is historical, not a modern physical explanation of causation.
