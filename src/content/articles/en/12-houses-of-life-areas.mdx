## Introduction: The Stage of Experience

In Western astrology, the twelve houses are divisions used to connect a birth chart with themes of life. Planets are sometimes compared to actors, signs to costumes, and houses to stages. This explains an interpretive metaphor; it is not evidence that cosmic energy causes personality or events.

For example, traditions that read Mars as a symbol of drive may connect the fifth house with play and the tenth with public activity. A placement does not determine a career or behavior. This is a guide to symbolism, not a tool for predicting health, lifespan, wealth, or relationships.

## Check the Calculation System First

House calculations need a birth time, the historical time zone, geographic latitude and longitude, and a house system. If the time is unknown, do not interpret an arbitrary noon as the actual birth time. Planetary house placements cannot then be treated as established.

The Ascendant (ASC), Descendant (DSC), Midheaven (MC), and Imum Coeli (IC) are chart reference points, but they do not always coincide with house boundaries. The [Swiss Ephemeris system documentation](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swisseph.htm) distinguishes equal houses starting at the Ascendant in 30-degree steps from whole-sign houses starting at the beginning of its sign. Thus a whole-sign first house need not start at the Ascendant's exact degree, and the MC is not necessarily the tenth-house boundary in equal or whole-sign houses. Changing systems can change placements with the same birth data. Reproducible calculations do not establish predictive validity.

Some methods also have polar-region limitations. The [official API documentation](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swephprg.htm) describes a switch to Porphyry with an error return when Placidus or Koch cannot be calculated. That describes this library, not a claim that OIYO uses it. Check the system and any fallback used in an actual result.

## The Structural Anatomy of the 12 Houses

Traditions use three groups. Their roles below are symbolic classifications, not measured effects:

1. **Angular (1, 4, 7, 10)**: Associated with beginnings and visibility.
2. **Succedent (2, 5, 8, 11)**: Associated with maintenance and resources.
3. **Cadent (3, 6, 9, 12)**: Associated with transition and learning.

## Detailed House-by-House Guide

The following are themes used in modern Western astrology. They vary by period and school and are not validated personality categories or forecasts. Read the retained keywords as cultural vocabulary, not facts to assign to a person.

### 1st House: Self and First Impressions

This is a theme of self-presentation and first impressions, not an objective assessment of appearance or temperament or a diagnosis of a persona. Check how the Ascendant relates to the first-house boundary in the selected system.

- **Key Themes**: Identity, vitality, temperament, first impressions.

### 2nd House: The House of Values and Possessions

This house represents what you own and what you value. It is not just about money, but about your self-worth and your ability to sustain yourself.

- **Key Themes**: Income, moveable property, personal values, material security.

### 3rd House: The House of Communication

This connects communication, nearby surroundings, early learning, and short journeys. It does not measure reasoning or learning ability.

- **Key Themes**: Siblings, short trips, writing, speaking, the logical mind.

### 4th House: The House of Home and Roots

The most private part of the chart. It represents your ancestry, your family foundation, and your emotional sense of safety.

- **Key Themes**: Parents, heritage, domestic life, the literal home.

### 5th House: The House of Creativity and Joy

The arena of self-expression, romance, and pleasure. It is where you let your inner child play and take risks for the sake of joy.

- **Key Themes**: Children, dating, hobbies, speculative investments, art.

### 6th House: Daily Work and Care

This connects daily work, routines, and care. Traditional health keywords provide no basis for judging disease risk or treatment. Actual symptoms call for appropriate medical assessment, not chart interpretation.

- **Key Themes**: Employment, pets, hygiene, refined skills, daily duties.

### 7th House: The House of Partnerships

This is a theme of one-to-one commitments such as marriage or business partnerships, not a test of trustworthiness or relationship success.

- **Key Themes**: Marriage, business partners, open enemies, legal contracts.

### 8th House: The House of Transformation and Shared Resources

This connects shared resources, loss, and intimacy. Death, rebirth, and healing are traditional symbolic terms, not predictions of lifespan, death dates, or recovery. Inheritance, tax, and investment decisions need actual records and relevant professional evidence.

- **Key Themes**: Inheritance, taxes, sexuality, psychology, deep healing.

### 9th House: The House of Philosophy and Higher Learning

The house of the long-distance journey—both physical and mental. It represents your quest for meaning and the expansion of your horizons.

- **Key Themes**: Higher education, religion, law, international travel, wisdom.

### 10th House: Public Roles and Career

This connects reputation, career, and public roles without guaranteeing a calling or success. Assigning parental roles to the fourth and tenth houses varies by school; no gender or family structure is the correct template. Also check whether the MC coincides with the tenth-house boundary in the selected system.

- **Key Themes**: Authority, success, father figures, public image.

### 11th House: The House of Friendships and Aspirations

The arena of collective groups and the vision for the future. It is about your hopes, dreams, and the people who help you achieve them.

- **Key Themes**: Social networks, humanitarianism, long-term goals.

### 12th House: The House of the Unconscious and Solitude

This connects solitude, withdrawal, and less visible experiences. Karma and hidden enemies are not verified explanations of reality. Do not attribute disability, illness, or isolation to past-life wrongdoing or identify a person as an enemy from a chart.

- **Key Themes**: Dreams, subconscious, confinement, hidden enemies, transcendence.

## Distribution Is Not a Personality Test

Traditions sometimes connect the following themes with distribution. Counting positions is not the same as discovering personality. In particular, do not assume numbered groups in whole-sign or equal houses always coincide with the actual horizon or meridian divisions.

- **Houses 1–6**: An interpretive theme of private experience.
- **Houses 7–12**: An interpretive theme of public experience.
- **Houses 10, 11, 12, 1, 2, 3**: An interpretive theme of initiative.
- **Houses 4–9**: An interpretive theme of relationships with others.

These groups do not establish introversion, extraversion, independence, or dependence.

## Wrapping Up: Living Your Full Chart

A house with none of the selected bodies is an empty house, not an area without events or interest. Traditions may connect the sign at its boundary with a ruling planet. This is another interpretive rule, not an established cause governing life.

Houses can be used to compare traditional symbols and generate questions. They do not create an obligation to do a certain job or become a certain person. Prioritize your experience, preferences, constraints, and verifiable information in actual decisions.
