"Happiness isn't just about the pleasant life. It's the meaningful life, the engaged life, and the good life." — Martin Seligman

Traditional psychology largely focused on treating illness and dysfunction. Positive psychology, by contrast, studies human strengths, happiness, and flourishing. Founded in 1998 by Martin Seligman during his term as president of the American Psychological Association, the field answers the question "how can we live well?" with scientific evidence.

## The PERMA Model — Five Elements of Flourishing

Martin Seligman proposed five core elements of wellbeing.

### P — Positive Emotions

Experiencing emotions like joy, gratitude, awe, love, and hope.

**Barbara Fredrickson's "broaden-and-build" theory**:
Positive emotions broaden our thinking and build lasting psychological resources.

**Cultivating positive emotion**:
- Gratitude journaling: write down three things you're grateful for each evening
- Seeking awe: find moments of wonder in nature, art, or music
- Recalling good memories from the past

**Positive emotions broaden**: in Barbara Fredrickson's broaden-and-build theory, positive emotions widen thinking and action and help build resources. The once-popular "3-to-1 positivity ratio" was withdrawn after its mathematics were shown to be flawed (Brown et al., 2013).

---

### E — Engagement

Participating in activities that fully absorb you — what Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi called a state of Flow.

**Conditions for flow**:
- The challenge should be slightly above your skill level
- Clear goals
- Immediate feedback

**Using your strengths**: flow tends to occur most often when you're drawing on your own strengths.

---

### R — Relationships

Humans are social animals. Deep relationships are essential to health and happiness.

**The Harvard Study of Adult Development (tracked since 1938, over 80 years)**: the happiest, healthiest older adults in the study shared one thing in common — good relationships. Relationships mattered more than wealth, fame, or intelligence.

**Improving relationship quality**:
- Active-constructive responding: react with genuine enthusiasm to a partner's good news
- Active listening
- Expressing gratitude

---

### M — Meaning

A sense of belonging to, and contributing to, something larger than yourself.

**Sources of meaning**:
- Contributing to family or community
- Religion or spirituality
- A sense of vocation in your work
- The pursuit of art or knowledge

**Logotherapy (Viktor Frankl)**: meaning can be found in any circumstance. The reason some survived the Nazi concentration camps, Frankl argued, was meaning.

---

### A — Achievement/Accomplishment

Pursuing success and accomplishment as ends in themselves.

Not simply as a means to something else, but the satisfaction of mastery — the process of becoming skilled — in its own right.

**Self-efficacy**: Albert Bandura's research shows that the belief "I can do this" influences actual performance.

## The Science of Happiness: What the Research Shows

### What Determines Happiness

| Factor | Share | Meaning |
|------|------|------|
| Genetics/temperament | About 50% | Your innate happiness set point |
| Circumstances | About 10% | Income, marital status, and similar factors |
| Intentional activity | About 40% | The things we actively choose to do |

**A caution**: this 50/10/40 split (Lyubomirsky et al., 2005) is widely quoted, but its calculation was later criticized as invalid (Brown & Rohrer, 2020). Take away only the direction — intentional activities can help.

### Counterintuitive Research Findings

| Common belief | Research finding |
|------|---------|
| "Money buys happiness" | Happiness generally rises with income, but by smaller steps (Killingsworth et al., 2023) |
| "Marriage brings happiness" | A temporary boost that fades back toward baseline (hedonic adaptation) |
| "Buying things makes you happy" | Experiential purchases sustain happiness longer than material purchases |
| "More choice is better" | Too many options can lower satisfaction — though the effect depends on the situation (Schwartz, 2004) |

### Hedonic Adaptation

Both good and bad events tend to fade back toward your baseline level of happiness over time. One study found that lottery winners were no happier than controls, and that people paralyzed in accidents lost less happiness than expected (Brickman et al., 1978).

**How to use this**: gratitude practice slows hedonic adaptation. It's important to practice not taking good things for granted.

## Positive Psychology Practices

### Gratitude Practices

**Three Good Things**: each evening, write down three things that went well that day, along with why. Research (Seligman et al., 2005) found this improved happiness and reduced depression symptoms after two weeks.

**Gratitude letters**: write — and read aloud in person — a letter to someone who had a major positive impact on your life but whom you never properly thanked. Effects have been found to last up to a month.

### Using Your Strengths

**The VIA Character Strengths Survey** (free): www.viacharacter.org
- Identifies your top 24 character strengths
- Encourages using your strengths in new ways each day

**Integrating your top 5 strengths into your work**: improves job satisfaction and engagement.

### Acts of Kindness

Performing random acts of kindness. Doing five kind acts in a single day tends to have a bigger effect than spreading them out.

### Mindfulness Meditation

A 6–8 week MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction) program has been shown to reduce anxiety and depression and increase positive emotion.

### Growth Mindset

The belief "this can be learned" is a foundation for both resilience and happiness.

## Building Resilience

People who flourish aren't people without hardship — they're people with the **ability to recover from hardship**.

**Five elements of resilience**:
1. Meaning — finding meaning in suffering
2. Positive emotion — finding positive elements even in difficult situations
3. Social support — asking for and accepting help
4. Self-efficacy — "I can get through this"
5. Growth mindset — "this is making me stronger"
