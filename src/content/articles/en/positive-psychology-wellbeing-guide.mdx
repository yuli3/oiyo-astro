Martin Seligman did not describe a full life as pleasant feeling alone. He also wrote about an engaged life and a meaningful life, and later he set relationships apart as their own part.

A lot of earlier psychology worked on illness and on what was not functioning. Positive psychology is the stream that set out to study strengths, happiness, and living well. In 1998, while he was president of the American Psychological Association, Seligman made a public call in that direction. A 2000 paper became a front door to the field (Seligman & Csikszentmihalyi, 2000). Similar words were older than that movement. One person did not found a science in a single year.

**This article is not a diagnosis and not a treatment plan.** The practices below are practices. They do not come with a guaranteed effect.

## The PERMA model — five parts of a wellbeing story

PERMA is the wellbeing model Seligman set out in his 2011 book *Flourish* (Seligman, 2011). It is not a score, and it is not a set of diagnostic criteria. The five letters are positive emotion, engagement, relationships, meaning, and achievement.

### P — Positive emotion

This part is about feelings such as joy, gratitude, awe, love, and hope.

Barbara Fredrickson's broaden-and-build theory says positive emotions can widen thought and action, and can help build psychological resources for later (Fredrickson, 2001). It is a theory. It is not a law that this happens for every person.

A few things people try: writing three things they are grateful for in the evening, looking for a moment of awe in nature, art, or music, and recalling a good memory. You can try them or skip them. They are not treatment.

A positivity-to-negativity ratio of 3 to 1 once traveled widely. It was withdrawn after its mathematics were shown to be wrong (Brown, Sokal, & Friedman, 2013). Do not aim at that number.

---

### E — Engagement

What Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi called flow is close to being absorbed in what you are doing. The conditions he described include challenge and skill both high and roughly matched, clear goals, and quick feedback (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990). A rule that challenge must sit a little above skill is not the heart of his account.

Seligman ties the engagement part of PERMA to using your strengths. That is his model. It is not a finding that flow happens only when a person is working from a strengths list.

---

### R — Relationships

People live with other people. Close relationships show up in many stories of health and happiness. That does not mean they weigh the same for every person.

The Harvard Study of Adult Development began in 1938. Robert Waldinger and Marc Schulz wrote *The Good Life* (2023) for general readers about that long study. The thread they stress is warm relationships. The early cohorts were particular groups of people, not a stand-in for every human. This article does not report, as a study result, that relationships always outrank wealth, fame, and intelligence.

Things you can practice in a relationship: meeting good news with interest and a real question (a style sometimes called active-constructive responding), listening, and saying thank you. These are practices, not a guarantee that a relationship will improve.

---

### M — Meaning

A sense of belonging to something larger than yourself, or of contributing to it. People look for that sense in family, community, religion or spirituality, a calling in work, and art or knowledge. The list is a set of examples, not a complete map.

Viktor Frankl's logotherapy says people can search for meaning even in extreme conditions. *Man's Search for Meaning* is his account of life in Nazi concentration camps. Meaning is not an established cause of who survived. Survival had many causes that were not meaning.

---

### A — Achievement

This part treats success and mastery as satisfying in themselves, not only as tools for some other reward.

Albert Bandura's theory of self-efficacy says the belief "I can do this" shapes effort and performance (Bandura, 1977). It is not a guarantee that belief is enough.

---

## Happiness research: a picture, and its limits

### The pie that was said to divide happiness

In 2005, Sonja Lyubomirsky, Kennon Sheldon, and David Schkade offered a rough picture: about 50 for genes, about 10 for circumstances, and about 40 for intentional activity (Lyubomirsky, Sheldon, & Schkade, 2005). Many readers met that picture in Lyubomirsky's book *The How of Happiness*.

Those three numbers are one research group's rough picture. They are not measured shares of any person's happiness. They are not a split that applies to each person. In 2021, Sheldon and Lyubomirsky agreed with many critiques of the pie. They described the original numbers as speculation, not doctrine, and wrote that any effect of intentional activity may be weaker than they first thought (Sheldon & Lyubomirsky, 2021). Critics had already argued that the evidence for that tidy split was weak (Brown & Rohrer, 2020). Later discussion found the picture too tidy.

Intentional activity is something you can try as a practice. It is not 40 percent of your happiness.

### Where the slogan is simpler than the study

"Money buys happiness" does not finish in one line. A 2023 reanalysis of U.S. experience-sampling data reported that, among happier people, reported happiness kept rising with income, while among the least happy the rise flattened at higher incomes (Killingsworth, Kahneman, & Mellers, 2023). It is one country's data. It does not hand you a dollar figure for your own life.

"More choice is better" is also hard to keep as it stands. Barry Schwartz argued that too many options can make choosing harder and satisfaction lower (Schwartz, 2004). The effect depends on the situation. This article does not put a size on it.

Two sentences are left out: that marriage brings a brief lift and then a return to baseline, and that experiential purchases keep happiness longer than material ones. The reference list here does not contain a specific study for either sentence.

### Hedonic adaptation

Hedonic adaptation is the name for good and bad feelings tending, over time, to settle back toward an earlier level. An early, small study compared lottery winners, people with paralysis after accidents, and a comparison group. Winners were not clearly happier than the comparison group, and the drop for the accident group was smaller than outsiders expected (Brickman, Coates, & Janoff-Bulman, 1978). The sample is small. The study illustrates adaptation. It does not show that good or bad events stop mattering.

Gratitude can be a practice of noticing good things instead of treating them as background. It is not confirmed as a device that slows adaptation for every person.

## Practices that come from positive psychology

These are practices. They are not guaranteed treatments, and they are not a medical protocol.

### Gratitude

**Three good things.** In a 2005 internet study by Seligman, Steen, Park, and Peterson, people asked to write three things that went well each day, and why, for one week, were linked with higher happiness scores and fewer depressive symptoms than a comparison exercise. In that study the difference was still there at the six-month check. So was the exercise of using signature strengths in a new way (Seligman, Steen, Park, & Peterson, 2005). Those are group results from one study. They are not a promise of the same change for you.

**A gratitude visit.** In the same study, writing a letter to someone you had never properly thanked, and delivering it in person, was linked with a large short-term change. By the three-month check it was no longer different from the start. That is not a general rule that the effect lasts one month.

### Strengths

Christopher Peterson and Seligman classified 24 character strengths (Peterson & Seligman, 2004). The VIA Institute's survey ranks those 24. It is at [viacharacter.org](https://www.viacharacter.org). It is a tool for reflection, not a clinical test. It does not identify your top 24. It orders the whole set of 24.

A practice is to try a strength that ranks high, in a new way, on ordinary days. The claim that putting your top five strengths into your job raises job satisfaction is left out. Nothing in this reference list supports that sentence.

### Kindness

Doing kind things is a practice. The comparison that says five kind acts packed into one day work better than the same acts spread out is left out. This reference list does not pin that comparison to a specific study.

### Mindfulness

MBSR, mindfulness-based stress reduction, is the program Jon Kabat-Zinn built. The usual form is eight weeks. It has been studied in health settings. This article does not prescribe it, and results differ from person to person. The sentence that it reduces anxiety and depression and raises positive emotion is left out. No specific study for that sentence is attached here.

### Growth mindset

What Carol Dweck called a growth mindset is the belief that abilities can be developed (Dweck, 2006). It is not a guarantee that this belief is the foundation of resilience or happiness. It is not a medical protocol.

## Resilience is not a protocol

People who are doing all right are not people who never met hardship. Here, resilience is an everyday word for a capacity to adapt after hardship. **It is not a medical protocol, and it is not a diagnosis.**

Writers sometimes mention meaning, people nearby, and a sense that a next step is possible. Those are ideas people use. They are not the official five elements of resilience. None of them guarantees recovery.

<Callout type="tip">
**A practice you can try tonight**: before sleep, write three things that were all right today. It is a practice. It is not a treatment, and it is not a promise that you will feel different in a set number of weeks.
