Face reading is an old East Asian practice of reading a face. It has attached symbols of personality, relationships, and the course of a life to the forehead, eyes, nose, mouth, ears, and jaw. It is not a ranking of looks, and it is not a science that finds the future written on a face.

Modern psychology does study expression and first impressions. People do guess at personality from a face, and they do it very quickly. That guess is not the same thing as the person. Todorov and colleagues show that these impressions have social consequences and are not a reliable reading of personality.

<Callout type="info">
The tables on this page are readings passed down inside a tradition. They do not diagnose illness, crime, length of life, or fate. If you are worried about your health, see a clinician instead of a face-reading chart.
</Callout>

## Core principles

### Yin-yang, the five elements, and the face

The tradition places itself inside the story of yin-yang and the five elements. Charts that match each element to a feature differ from manual to manual, so this article does not invent one. The frame that introductions repeat is the three zones of the face.

A common textbook draws the boundaries like this. Other manuals move the lines a little.

| Zone | Area | Themes the tradition attaches |
|------|------|------|
| **Upper zone** | Forehead to the eyebrows | A symbol for parents, learning, and the earlier part of a life story |
| **Middle zone** | Eyebrows to the tip of the nose | A symbol for work, resources, and the middle of the story |
| **Lower zone** | Below the nose to the chin | A symbol for relationships and the later part of the story |

Popular guides also pin numbers for age onto each zone. Those numbers are not a measured developmental table, so this article leaves them out. The saying that balanced zones mean a balanced life is a sentence inside the tradition. It is not a formula for fortune.

<Callout type="info">
Face reading is not a prediction. It is the tradition's language of tendencies. Keep it as how a face has been read, not as what a face must mean.
</Callout>

## Reading the forehead

The tradition links the forehead to learning, to a story about parents, and to the earlier part of a life. It does not measure an intelligence score or a parent's fortune.

### Forehead shapes

The table is a reading often passed along. It does not settle personality or fate.

| Forehead shape | Traditional reading |
|---------|------|
| **Broad, high forehead** | A symbol of learning, intuition, and a capacity to lead |
| **Narrow forehead** | A symbol of practical thinking and of gathering attention in one place |
| **M-shaped forehead** | A hairline that recedes at the temples and looks like an M. Some guides read it as strong individuality. It is also a common hairline, not a personality test |
| **Straight, flat forehead** | A symbol of principle, logic, and practicality |
| **Protruding forehead** | A symbol of drive and of asserting oneself |

### Forehead lines

Lines also come from age, expression, sunlight, and habit. Do not read them as a code.

| Line pattern | Traditional reading |
|---------|------|
| One horizontal line | Some guides read it as a symbol of high status and of deciding alone |
| Three horizontal lines | Guides disagree: some say long life and success, others say worry. It is not one code |
| Vertical lines | A tradition of reading them as traces of focus and of thinking for a long time |
| A 川-shaped set of lines | A tradition of reading them as strong will and stubbornness. Not a mark of disease |

Some sources give three horizontal lines a special name and treat them as a sign of long life. The name and the meaning change from source to source, so this article does not fix that name as a standard term.

## Reading the eyes

The tradition most often names the eyes as the place for wisdom, emotion, and relationships. That importance is itself a sentence inside the tradition.

### Eye shapes

| Eye shape | Traditional reading |
|--------|------|
| **Phoenix eyes** (balanced upper and lower lids, outer corners slightly raised) | A symbol of wisdom, of a pull on other people, and of work that comes out well |
| **Long eyes** | A symbol of seeing through things and of thinking over a long span |
| **Large eyes** | Read as feeling readily and expressing largely |
| **Small eyes** | A symbol of focus, analysis, and care |
| **Upturned outer corners** | A symbol of self-respect, leading, and challenge |
| **Downturned outer corners** | A symbol of mildness, empathy, and consideration |

### The look in the eyes

A gaze shows the expression and the fatigue of that moment. It does not report depth of character or the name of an illness.

- **Clear, bright eyes**: a tradition of reading them as liveliness
- **Dull, clouded eyes**: a tradition of reading them as fatigue and inner strain. Not a diagnosis
- **Deep, calm eyes**: a tradition of reading them as thinking for a long time
- **Sparkling, animated eyes**: a tradition of reading them as curiosity and liveliness

If you are worried about your eyes, or if pain, fatigue, or a change in vision lasts, that is a matter for a clinician, not for face reading.

## Reading the nose

The tradition links the nose to resources, to the self, and to will. It is not a tool for measuring income or personality.

### Nose shapes

| Nose shape | Traditional reading |
|--------|------|
| **High, straight nose** | A symbol of self-respect, independence, and leading |
| **Wide nose** | A symbol of resources gathering, and of generosity |
| **Upturned nose** | A symbol of warmth, of feeling readily, and of spending freely |
| **Sharp, pointed nose** | A symbol of keen sense and of disliking gaps |
| **Large nose** | A symbol of nerve, of carrying a thing out, and of getting it done |

**The tip of the nose**: one tradition reads a round, fleshy tip as resources that stay. Another reads a pointed or upward tip as resources that do not stick. Both are symbols, not a judgment of a bank account or of fate.

## Reading the mouth and lips

The tradition links the mouth to speech, awareness, and showing emotion. Lip color also changes with blood flow, cold, and makeup. Do not use it as a health verdict.

### Mouth and lip shapes

| Lip feature | Traditional reading |
|---------|------|
| **Full, red lips** | A symbol of plentiful feeling and of showing affection readily |
| **Thin lips** | A symbol of coolness, logic, and few words |
| **A fuller lower lip** | A symbol of persistence, practicality, and attention to material things |
| **A fuller upper lip** | A symbol of ideals, expression, and sociability |
| **Upturned corners** | A symbol of optimism and sociability. Not a verdict that someone is lucky |
| **Downturned corners** | A tradition of reading caution, realism, or a pessimistic lean. Not a settled personality |

## Reading the ears

The tradition links the ears to a long life, to wisdom, and to the earlier part of a life. It has spoken of them as symbols of longevity and sturdiness. It does not measure either.

| Ear feature | Traditional reading |
|--------|------|
| **Large, thick ears** | A symbol of long life and sturdiness. Not a health diagnosis |
| **High-set ears** | A symbol of quickness and of high hopes |
| **Low-set ears** | A symbol of practicality and diligence |
| **Full, thick earlobes** | A symbol of resources and sturdiness. Not a verdict on wealth or illness |
| **Thin or absent earlobes** | A symbol of softness and of feeling readily |

## Reading the jaw

The tradition links the jaw to the later part of the story, to will, and to relationships.

| Jaw shape | Traditional reading |
|--------|------|
| **Broad, angular jaw** | A symbol of will, endurance, and a steadier later chapter |
| **Round jaw** | A symbol of drawing people in, sociability, and warm relationships |
| **Pointed jaw** | A symbol of feeling, art, and liking change |
| **Double chin** | Some texts read it as ease and as resources set aside. It is not a score for wealth or health. Body size is not a moral score or a fortune score |
| **A weak jawline** | A traditional reading of weak practicality and of putting decisions off. Not a settled personality |

## Overall balance

Face shape comes from inheritance, age, weight, and expression. Below is a tradition that has attached personality to those outlines.

### Face shapes

| Face shape | Traditional reading |
|-------|------|
| **Round** | A symbol of optimism, sociability, and feeling readily |
| **Square or angular** | A symbol of will, carrying things out, and leading |
| **Long** | A symbol of care, analysis, and principle |
| **Inverted triangle** | A symbol of intellect, art, and sense |
| **Diamond** | A symbol of a pull on other people, and of two sides |

## Face reading now

Today it fits better as a mirror for a story about yourself than as a tool for hitting a prediction.

- You can lay the words in the tables over your own strengths and sore spots. After you do, the story is still a story.
- A first impression forms quickly. It is not the same as character. Expression, sleep, and tension can show on a face for a while. The shape of the bone cannot manufacture trust.
- There is a tradition of commenting on complexion. That comment does not diagnose illness. If skin or color worries you, see a clinician. A face-reading chart will miss medical causes.

Judging character or crime from facial bone and outline was also used, in other traditions including nineteenth-century criminal anthropology, to rank people. That misuse is a reason to keep this reading symbolic, and optional.

<Callout type="warning">
Face reading is a story about tendencies, not fate and not a diagnosis. Faces change with age, expression, weight, illness, and habit. An inner life is not written on the bone as a sentence. Use it only as a reference for understanding yourself. Do not use it to settle illness, crime, or the future.
</Callout>
