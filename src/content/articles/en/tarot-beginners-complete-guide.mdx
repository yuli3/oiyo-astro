## What Is Tarot?

Tarot is a symbolic system built from **78 cards**. It's often treated as a fortune-telling tool, and today it is also used as a **tool for self-reflection**.

If you're new to tarot: it isn't a device for predicting the future with precision. It's better understood as **a way of looking at your present situation from a different angle and uncovering possibilities you hadn't considered**.

---

## The Structure of the 78 Cards

```
The 78-Card Tarot Deck
├── Major Arcana: 22 cards
│   └── Life's big themes, spiritual lessons
└── Minor Arcana: 56 cards
    ├── Wands: 14 cards — fire, passion, creativity
    ├── Cups: 14 cards — water, emotion, relationships
    ├── Swords: 14 cards — air, thought, conflict
    └── Pentacles: 14 cards — earth, material life, reality
```

## Reading Reversed Cards

A reversed card shows a **blocked, internalized, excessive, or lacking** version of the card's upright meaning.

| Card | Upright | Reversed |
|---|---|---|
| The Sun | Joy, success | A temporary setback, a cloudy day |
| The Moon | Intuition, the unconscious | Confusion, facing your fears |
| The Star | Hope, healing | Lost hope, a need to recover |
| Strength | Inner strength | Lack of confidence, a need to recover |

## Practicing a Three-Card Spread

The most basic spread. Three cards give you a multi-angle view of a situation.

### Past–Present–Future Spread

```
[Card 1] — [Card 2] — [Card 3]
  Past        Present       Future
```

**How to read it:**
1. Take in each card's basic meaning
2. Notice the story the three cards tell together
3. Remember: the "future" card isn't a prophecy — it's the direction your current momentum is heading

### Situation–Action–Outcome Spread

```
[Card 1] Current situation / [Card 2] Action to take / [Card 3] Likely outcome
```

**Example use**: Considering a job change
- Card 1: Nine of Swords (anxiety, worry) → concern about your current job
- Card 2: Ace of Wands (a new beginning) → the message is to take the leap with courage
- Card 3: Nine of Cups (a wish fulfilled) → a satisfying outcome

## How to Start Reading Tarot

### 1. Choose a Deck
Recommended for beginners: **the Rider-Waite Tarot** — the most foundational deck, and the one covered in the most reference books

### 2. Build a Relationship with the Cards
- Draw one card each morning to check the day's energy
- In the evening, note how the day actually connected to it

### 3. Ask Good Questions
Good question: "What do I need to know about this situation?"
Poor question: "Does this person love me?" (asking about someone else's inner state)

### 4. Trust Your Intuition
Before you memorize the "official" meanings, let the imagery speak to you first. Intuition comes before the textbook.

## The History and Origins of Tarot

Tarot cards are one of the best-known divination tools in the world today. Their origins, however, lie not in fortune-telling but in a **card game**.

### 15th-Century Italy: The Game of Trionfi

The earliest form of tarot is generally traced to **Trionfi** cards, which appeared in northern Italy in the 1440s. They were made for a card game popular among the nobility and, unlike tarot today, were not used for divination.

The most famous early tarot deck is the **Visconti-Sforza tarot**, made in the mid-15th century for the Visconti and Sforza families of Milan. These hand-painted cards are among the oldest surviving tarot cards.

### 18th-Century France: Becoming a Divination Tool

Tarot began to be used for divination in **18th-century Europe**, especially in France. In 1781, Antoine Court de Gébelin claimed that the tarot was a book containing the secret wisdom of ancient Egypt. That claim has since been shown to have no historical basis, but fortune-tellers such as Etteilla went on to publish divination decks and guides, and tarot reading spread widely among occultists.

In 1909, **Arthur Edward Waite** and **Pamela Colman Smith**, both members of the British occult society the **Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn**, released the widely used **Rider-Waite-Smith Tarot** (often called the Rider-Waite Tarot) through the publisher Rider. The deck gave every card, including the numbered pip cards, an illustrated scene, which made the cards easier to read from their pictures. Especially in the English-speaking world, it became something like the reference deck for modern tarot.

## Three Ways to Lay Out a Spread

A **spread** is the pattern in which cards are laid out, and different spreads suit different kinds of questions.

### One-Card Spread

The simplest method: you draw a single card to reflect on the day's energy or a simple question.

**How to use it**: calm your mind, focus on your question, shuffle the deck and draw one card.
**Good questions**: "What energy do I need today?" or "What should I focus on in this situation?"

### Three-Card Spread

This is the method most often recommended for tarot beginners. Three cards give you a more rounded view of a situation.

| Position | Meaning |
|------|------|
| Left | Past / cause |
| Center | Present / situation |
| Right | Future / outcome |

You can also use layouts such as **mind / body / spirit**, **situation / action / outcome**, or **me / the other person / us**.

### Celtic Cross Spread (10 Cards)

The most traditional and comprehensive spread. Ten cards are used to look at the overall flow of a situation.

1. **Present situation** – what is happening now
2. **Crossing card** – a challenge or obstacle
3. **Foundation** – the root cause of the situation
4. **Past** – influences that have recently passed
5. **Possibility** – a potential outcome
6. **Near future** – influences coming soon
7. **Self** – how the querent sees themselves
8. **Environment** – the influence of people around you
9. **Hopes and fears** – inner expectations and worries
10. **Final outcome** – the overall direction

## Common Card Combinations

In a tarot reading, **combinations of cards** are thought to carry a more important message than single cards. The examples below are simply common symbolic readings; they do not predict real outcomes.

**The Lovers (6) + Two of Cups**: deep, mutual connection in love; a symbol of a closely bonded relationship
**The Tower (16) + Death (13)**: sudden and complete transformation, change with no going back
**The Empress (3) + Ace of Wands**: the birth of a creative project, the seed of a new venture
**The Moon (18) + Nine of Swords (the nightmare card)**: an image of intense anxiety and fear, a psychological crisis (if anxiety or sleeplessness lasts a long time in real life, please talk to a professional rather than relying on the cards)
**The Sun (19) + The World (21)**: an image of achievement, completion and a bright ending
