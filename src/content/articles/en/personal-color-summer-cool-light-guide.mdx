**Summer Cool Light** is a type in personal-color consulting, grouped by the idea that cool, bright, soft colors look less muddy when they are placed beside the face. It often overlaps with the Korean marketing phrase cool-tone white skin. Those words are a marketing phrase, not a dermatology label for a skin type.

## Where this frame comes from

Carole Jackson's *Color Me Beautiful* (1980) popularized the **four seasons**: spring, summer, autumn, and winter. The **12 tones**, which split summer again into light and mute, are a later frame of the consulting trade, especially in practice in Korea and Japan. Johannes Itten's *The Art of Color* (1961) is a textbook on color contrast, not a test that diagnoses a person. Studies of how often raters agree are scarce.

In this frame, summer is a cool undertone. It is described as softer than winter, and as a different grain from the warm clarity of spring. **Light**, inside that, is spoken of like this.

- Saturation is low or middle. The color is not loud.
- Lightness is high. It sits toward the bright, clear side.
- Pastels with a blue cast are often brought out.
- The words for the impression are clarity, delicacy, and mist.

**Cool mute** of the same summer is a cool that is more grayed and sunk like smoke. Light is split from that as a clearer, brighter cool. The boundary between the two types differs from consultant to consultant.

## A list to look at in the mirror

Natural light, before makeup. Checking these items does not settle the type. They are questions a consultant uses before holding cloth up to the face.

### Skin

- There are times when the undertone is read as pink beige, rose, or cool ivory
- It is a metal test in which silver looks less out of place than gold. It is not a scientific experiment
- There is a pink or rose flush on the cheeks
- The skin looks clear
- People say of themselves that they tend to flush red in the sun. This is not advice on how to prevent a burn
- The impression looks delicate

### Eyes

- The iris is written as light brown, reddish brown, or cool black
- It looks more toward the cool side than toward gold
- The white of the eye is bright, or slightly bluish

### Hair

- Natural hair is black, dark brown, or a dark color
- Warm red or gold looks scarce
- It is a consulting phrase that a cool, light dye makes the face look less muddy
- The phrase that a warm red dye makes the face look dull is the same kind of industry talk

### Colors held up as cloth

Colors this frame often names on the matching side are lavender, powder blue, cool pale pink, rose pink, and light lilac.

Colors set aside are coral with a strong orange, golden yellow, warm red, and neon.

## A palette often suggested

There is no starred ranking. These are colors that keep coming up on this type's card.

### Pink

- Rose pink. Often written as a representative color of this type
- Baby pink. A cool pastel
- Soft pink
- Blush. Even if it is apricot, only the cool side

Coral and warm peach are usually left out.

### Blue and purple

- Powder blue
- Lavender
- Light lilac
- Sky blue
- Periwinkle

### Green

Only pale greens with a blue cast, such as mint and soft sage, are put in, and only in a limited way. Yellow-green, olive, and lime are usually left out.

### Neutral

Rose white, light grey, silver grey, lavender grey, and ice pink come up. Yellow ivory, camel, and warm beige are usually left out.

## Directions often written for makeup

People often say the base goes toward looking clear, rather than toward covering.

- Foundation is pink beige. A yellow base is often left out
- Concealer is a pale pink
- Powder is transparent, or a pale pink
- Highlighter is a silver pearl, or a rose gold that is not a warm gold

Eyeshadow often comes up as pink beige, lavender, rose, soft brown, and light purple. Eyeliner is grey brown, soft black, or a pale purple. Mascara is black or soft brown. Golden bronze, orange brown, and warm terracotta are usually left out.

Blusher is cool pink, rose, or baby pink. Orange, coral, and peach are usually left out.

Lips are rose pink, baby pink, light raspberry, a nude on a pink base, or soft mauve. Orange-red, coral, and a golden beige nude are usually left out.

## Combinations often written for clothes

- A lavender shirt and white trousers
- A pale pink knit and light grey slacks
- A powder blue dress and silver metal
- A rose pink accessory with white clothes

Orange, coral, golden tones, terracotta, burnt orange, yellow-green, and olive are usually left off this card. They are not forbidden colors that must not be worn.

## Hair color

Names consultants often write are ash brown, light ash, cool black, and light rose brown. Very light platinum divides from person to person. If the face looks washed out, leave it out even for this type. Warm golden brown, caramel, copper, and a warm red cast are usually left out.

<Callout type="note">
Holding color cloth up to the face is a matter of taste and of consulting language. It is not a test in which the skin becomes healthier, or in which there is one correct type.
</Callout>
