A newspaper horoscope usually looks only at the **Sun sign**, the stretch where the Sun sat on the birthday. A birth chart puts the date, the time, and the place in together. It moves the sky of that moment onto a round drawing. It is not a test result that measured a personality.

## Three things in the chart

1. **Bodies.** Tradition uses seven: the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn. Modern charts often add Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto, and draw something like ten. Astronomy reclassified Pluto as a dwarf planet in 2006. Astrology charts still often mark Pluto after that.
2. **Signs.** The twelve sections of the tropical zodiac.
3. **Houses.** Twelve areas of life, divided by the horizon at the place of birth.

Astro.com, Astro-Seek, and Café Astrology are places people often mention for drawing a chart. That does not mean the automatic reading on those sites is correct.

<Callout type="info">
**Time.** The rising sign changes by about one sign every two hours. A difference of one hour can change the rising sign and the start of the houses near a boundary. It does not always change them by much. If the birth time is unknown, it is better to read only the Sun and the slow planets, and to leave the rising sign and the houses blank.
</Callout>

## Sun, Moon, and rising sign

Modern textbooks split the three this way. These are words for reading, not a measurement. The sentence that the Moon is more accurate than the Sun is not used here.

**The Sun** is the stretch set by the birthday. It is matched to the question of a conscious style, and of what the effort goes toward.

**The Moon** is matched to words about feeling, habit, and need on the bodily side. The Moon stays in one sign for roughly two to three days. That is not a fixed 2.5 days.

**The rising sign** is the sign that was on the eastern horizon at birth. It is used as the wording for a first impression and for the way of starting.

These are phrases often attached to a Moon sign. They do not mean that a person with that Moon is always like this.

| Moon | Phrases often written | What is written as a need |
|----|--------------|-------------------|
| Aries | a quick response | room to move on one's own |
| Taurus | the senses, slow change | a familiar place |
| Gemini | speech, a sketch of changing mood | conversation |
| Cancer | care, memory | people close by |
| Leo | expression, recognition | a place to be seen |
| Virgo | order, use | a daily round one can handle |
| Libra | a pair, balance | someone to consult |
| Scorpio | depth, secrecy | a range one can trust |
| Sagittarius | a far story | room to go out |
| Capricorn | responsibility, restraint | structure |
| Aquarius | distance, a notion | time to think alone |
| Pisces | soaking in, imagination | quiet time |

## Traditional topics of the bodies

| Body | Words the tradition often assigns |
|------|------------------------|
| Sun | center, vitality, the direction of the day |
| Moon | habit, a bodily rhythm, care |
| Mercury | speech, writing, short moves |
| Venus | liking, agreement, shape |
| Mars | action, anger, cutting off and starting |
| Jupiter | expansion, belief, far places |
| Saturn | a limit, time, responsibility |
| Uranus | Modern. A break, reform |
| Neptune | Modern. A blur, an ideal |
| Pluto | Modern. Removing and making again |

Calling Saturn karma is a phrase of modern astrology. It is not a term of the *Tetrabiblos*. The modern topics attached to Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto are twentieth-century additions as well, not terms of that book.

## The twelve houses

A house is a traditional name for an area of life. A planet in that house does not mean that an event in that area will happen.

| House | Traditional topic |
|--------|-----------|
| 1 | the body, the way of starting, a first impression |
| 2 | money, possessions, what is held as valuable |
| 3 | speech, siblings, a nearby road |
| 4 | home, parents, roots |
| 5 | play, romance, making. Children are one of the traditional topics |
| 6 | daily life, tasks, the body's routine. Not a health diagnosis |
| 7 | a one-to-one relation, a contract |
| 8 | resources used together, transformation. The old name, house of death, is not a forecast of death |
| 9 | a long road, belief, higher study |
| 10 | work seen from outside, reputation |
| 11 | the group, hope, support |
| 12 | what is hidden, isolation, an ending |

## An example of a reading

This is only an example. It is not a real person.

If the drawing has the Sun in Scorpio and in the 8th house, the Moon in Taurus and in the 2nd house, and Leo rising, the traditional way of speaking unpacks it like this.

- Sun, Scorpio, 8th house. Secrets and resources used together are tied to the story of identity.
- Moon, Taurus, 2nd house. Familiar possessions and the senses are tied to an emotional need.
- Rising, Leo. The way of being seen first is written as a large, warm expression.

This is not that person's measured personality.

## References

The meanings of the signs, the planets, and the houses follow the Western astrological tradition passed on in the works below. Controlled verification studies did not predict personality and events from a birth chart or a birth date (Carlson, 1985; Hartmann and others, 2006).

- Ptolemy. *Tetrabiblos* (2nd century CE). Trans. F. E. Robbins. Loeb Classical Library, Harvard University Press, 1940.
- Campion, N. (2008–2009). *A History of Western Astrology* (Vols. 1–2). Continuum.
- Carlson, S. (1985). A double-blind test of astrology. *Nature*, 318, 419–425.
- Dean, G., & Kelly, I. W. (2003). Is astrology relevant to consciousness and psi? *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 10(6–7), 175–198.
- Hartmann, P., Reuter, M., & Nyborg, H. (2006). The relationship between date of birth and individual differences in personality and general intelligence: A large-scale study. *Personality and Individual Differences*, 40(7), 1349–1362.
