Chinese zodiac compatibility is an old East Asian tradition that describes how the twelve earthly branches (地支) relate to one another. Classical Chinese astrology (mingli) texts record rules by which branches combine (合) or clash (沖), and folk practice has long used those rules to compare animal signs. This guide sets out the rules accurately, and presents compatibility not as a prediction of how a relationship will turn out but as **one language that tradition uses to talk about relationships**.

<Callout type="info">
**Before you read on**: Zodiac compatibility is a cultural tradition. There is no scientific evidence that the combination of two birth years decides whether a relationship succeeds. No pairing can be said to work out or to fail.
</Callout>

## Signs and branches

The twelve animals pair with the earthly branches in this order, and the order forms a circle.

Rat (子) · Ox (丑) · Tiger (寅) · Rabbit (卯) · Dragon (辰) · Snake (巳) · Horse (午) · Goat (未) · Monkey (申) · Rooster (酉) · Dog (戌) · Pig (亥)

Every rule below depends on where two branches sit on that circle. Your sign does not change on January 1. Folk custom uses the Lunar New Year and saju (Four Pillars) uses Lichun, the start of spring in early February, so if you were born in January or February, please double-check your sign.

## Triads (三合)

Three branches spaced four positions apart on the circle are said to form one element group. Tradition counts them as the most harmonious combination.

| Triad | Signs | Element |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 申子辰 | Monkey · Rat · Dragon | Water (水) |
| 亥卯未 | Pig · Rabbit · Goat | Wood (木) |
| 寅午戌 | Tiger · Horse · Dog | Fire (火) |
| 巳酉丑 | Snake · Rooster · Ox | Metal (金) |

## Six harmonies (六合)

Two branches pair up and combine. Tradition reads these as signs that draw each other in and complement each other.

| Six harmony | Signs |
|-------------|-------|
| 子丑 | Rat + Ox |
| 寅亥 | Tiger + Pig |
| 卯戌 | Rabbit + Dog |
| 辰酉 | Dragon + Rooster |
| 巳申 | Snake + Monkey |
| 午未 | Horse + Goat |

## Clashes (沖)

Two branches directly opposite each other on the circle, six positions apart. Tradition reads a clash as a symbol of friction and change.

| Clash | Signs |
|-------|-------|
| 子午 | Rat ↔ Horse |
| 丑未 | Ox ↔ Goat |
| 寅申 | Tiger ↔ Monkey |
| 卯酉 | Rabbit ↔ Rooster |
| 辰戌 | Dragon ↔ Dog |
| 巳亥 | Snake ↔ Pig |

## Harms (害)

A harm interferes with a six harmony: one branch clashes with the other's six-harmony partner. For example, 子 (Rat) combines with 丑 (Ox), and 未 (Goat) clashes with 丑, so 子 and 未 form a harm. Tradition reads it as a symbol of crossed wires or hurt feelings.

| Harm | Signs |
|------|-------|
| 子未 | Rat + Goat |
| 丑午 | Ox + Horse |
| 寅巳 | Tiger + Snake |
| 卯辰 | Rabbit + Dragon |
| 申亥 | Monkey + Pig |
| 酉戌 | Rooster + Dog |

A pairing that does not appear in the table is simply not linked by any of these four rules. Mingli texts also discuss relations such as punishment (刑) and destruction (破), but this guide covers only the four in common use.

## How to read compatibility

- **Your sign is only one part of a chart.** The animal sign looks at a single branch, the one for your birth year. Even in mingli, the year pillar is two of eight characters, and the tradition itself does not judge compatibility from the animal sign alone.
- **These are symbols, not outcomes.** A triad or six harmony does not guarantee a good result, and a clash or harm does not mean a pairing should be avoided. They are symbols tradition attaches to how two branches relate.
- **Two people make a relationship.** What a relationship is really like depends on communication, respect, ways of living, and effort on both sides. Please enjoy sign pairings lightly, as a conversation starter.
- **Please do not base important decisions on it.** It is better not to make choices about marriage, hiring, or business, or to exclude anyone, because of zodiac compatibility.
