## 1. What do the eight functions describe?

Ti, Te, Fi, Fe, Si, Se, Ni, and Ne are terms associated with the tradition of Jungian psychological types. They combine Thinking (T), Feeling (F), Sensing (S), or Intuition (N) with an introverted (i) or extraverted (e) orientation. Orientation here is not simply a count of friends or how much someone talks.

Despite the name, these functions are not eight independently measured pieces of the brain. This is a guide to reading a type model, not a neurological explanation or an ability assessment. Understanding a model does not establish that all its claims have been validated.

## 2. Judging and perceiving

Thinking and Feeling describe approaches to judgment within the model; Sensing and Intuition describe approaches to taking in information. Feeling is not a kindness score, and Thinking is not an intelligence score.

The questions below are editorial examples, not assessment items. Counting the questions you like will not establish a type.

| Category | Focus in the model | Editorial comparison question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thinking T | Reasons and decision criteria | Are the reasons consistent? |
| Feeling F | Values and meaning for people | Whose needs have we considered? |
| Sensing S | Concrete information | What did we actually observe? |
| Intuition N | Connections and possibilities | What alternative explanation fits? |

## 3. Comparing the eight names

These short explanations are reading aids. They do not mean that people exhibit only one function or think in this sequence.

### Ti · Introverted Thinking

This description emphasizes internal logical consistency. An editorial example is checking whether a term keeps the same definition throughout a meeting. The label does not establish superior reasoning ability.

### Te · Extraverted Thinking

This description emphasizes external criteria and organizing action. Asking who owns a task and what counts as completion is one example. Te is not evidence that someone is an effective manager.

### Fi · Introverted Feeling

This description emphasizes alignment with personally important values. One example is asking how a choice fits your principles. It does not establish greater morality or authenticity.

### Fe · Extraverted Feeling

This description emphasizes shared values and the relational context. An example is asking whether people who have not spoken can participate. It does not guarantee accurate emotion reading or empathy.

### Si · Introverted Sensing

This description relates present experience to familiar experience. Asking what has changed since a previous attempt is one example. It is neither a memory score nor a requirement to follow tradition.

### Se · Extraverted Sensing

This description emphasizes the concrete environment currently encountered. Checking what is visibly happening on a screen is one example. The term does not measure athletic skill or sensory acuity.

### Ni · Introverted Intuition

This description emphasizes an integrating interpretation or direction. An example is asking what hypothesis emerges when observations are considered together. The hypothesis is not necessarily true or predictive; check contrary evidence.

### Ne · Extraverted Intuition

This description emphasizes exploring connections and alternatives. Asking whether a problem can be approached another way is one example. The label is not a measured creativity score.

## 4. Function order is a model convention

Dominant, auxiliary, tertiary, and inferior are names used in type dynamics, not four abilities ranked by measurement. “Inferior” does not mean a person has less worth or intelligence.

For example, the provider describes INFJ with Ni as dominant and Fe as auxiliary, and INFP with Fi and Ne. Its guidance also acknowledges disagreement about the orientation of the tertiary function. The comparison retains that uncertainty.

| Position | INFJ | INFP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dominant | Ni | Fi |
| Auxiliary | Fe | Ne |
| Tertiary | T: Ti or Te | S: Si or Se |
| Inferior | Se | Te |

The familiar sequences Ni–Fe–Ti–Se and Fi–Ne–Si–Te fix the tertiary orientation. They should not be presented as the only settled account. Whatever convention is used, a function table is not an observation of someone's actual thinking. [Provider explanation of type-dynamics processes](https://www.myersbriggs.org/unique-features-of-myers-briggs/type-dynamics-processes/)

You could attach function names to these questions, but every team member can ask every question. This is not a validated type-specific workflow or a reason to allocate roles by type.

## 5. Using the terms without overclaiming

“I use Ni, so I predict well” and “my Fe is weak, so relationship problems are inevitable” turn labels into unsupported explanations. Examine actual behavior, results, experience, training, and circumstances separately. The provider also distinguishes MBTI preferences from ability and skill. [MBTI versus ability assessment](https://www.myersbriggs.org/unique-features-of-myers-briggs/is-the-mbti-a-test/)

Independent evidence is a separate question. The abstract of McCrae and Costa's 1989 study interprets MBTI results in terms of continuous dimensions rather than sharply separated types. It does not directly validate the eight editorial examples here or every function-order theory. [Article and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-6494.1989.tb00759.x)

Acting outside a type description is not a personal failure. Practising functions does not guarantee becoming a “whole” person. Use the vocabulary as a starting point for discussion, not as a diagnosis or a verdict on hiring or relationship suitability.
